# Vesperis

<img width="1074" height="661" alt="зображення" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0ddfabf4-8f5b-4e0d-a10a-a638a07d64ca" />



A first-person, goal-less space exploration game, heavily inspired by
Alessandro Ghignola's *Noctis IV* (2001). Vesperis is vibe coded: the whole
thing was written with an AI coding agent, from the software renderer to the
planet function, in the space of a few days.

You fly a small ship between the stars of a procedurally generated galaxy,
approach planets and moons, pick a landing sector on a map and walk (or drive)
around on surfaces generated by the same function that painted the globe you
saw from orbit. Nothing has a name until you give it one. There is no
objective. Everything is deterministic: the same sector, star, planet and
square metre of ground is always the same.

The picture is rendered in software at 320x200 (scaled up 1x-4x) through
palette intensity ramps and a soft smoothing filter, in the spirit of the
original.

Since 1.1.0 the galaxy has the proportions of ours (200 billion stars in a
disc with a bulge, arms, globular and open clusters, the band of the Milky Way
in every sky), fourteen kinds of star (red dwarfs that flare, neutron stars,
protostars, Wolf-Rayet stars, black holes among them) and dead peoples: one
desert or felisian world in eight once had a civilisation, whose ruins, roads
and graves read the terrain they stood on, and whose recordings, taken from the
ruins and decoded on the ship, are texts, music and star charts in a language,
a voice and a musical tradition of the world's own. A signal radar on the ship
points at the worlds that still transmit. Saves from 1.0.0 load, but the galaxy
was rebuilt: a saved star may be gone.

1.2.0 adds time and witness: an almanac of the coming events (sunrises, eclipses,
moonrises, a front's arrival, a meteor shower's peak) that runs the clock to
them; a telescope on the ship; weather fronts that move and arrive; nights with
meteor showers, the zodiacal light and earthshine; and probes dropped into gas
giants, each giant with its own decks, storms and glow, whose descents come back
as recordings in the gallery and profiles on the data sheet. The Vimana jumps
any number of light years along the aim, and on the ground a scanner points to
ruins, lakes, peaks and the other sights while the explorer marks and names
what they find on the sector map. The giants were regenerated (generation 12).

1.3.0 is the far, the faint and the peoples. Each dead people is found at the
age it had reached when it fell, from the stone age to a far future, and its
world shows it: towns, roads and great works of that age across the people's
own lands, buildings in an architecture of its own, and on a world of two
peoples the frontier between them and the battlefields of their wars. Their
records come in the genres and the tongue of the people and the age (letters,
chronicles, songs, sermons, broadcasts, a star chart on a cave's ceiling or a
navigation chart of routes), and the decoder reads them as a history along the
people's eras. Instruments taken from the ruins are played from a case on the
ship, a drone bay sends three drones down to bring back records and
instruments, and the ship's player plays what the ship has heard and held. A
chart of the whole galaxy plots the next jump, and the radar's gain hears
signals thousands of light years away. The peoples were regenerated (generation
13): saves load, and the shards already found are kept with new contents.

1.3.1 is faster, with the same worlds and pictures. A frame takes about half
the time it did (less of a gain on fewer cores): on the Apple M4 it was
measured on, the game holds 60 frames a second at the largest scale in the
cabin, at the decoder, walking and driving, where 1.3.0 ran at 30 to 58.
Saving to the guide, taking a photograph or a recording and opening the
landing map or the drone bay over a world not yet mapped no longer stall the
game, and a landing's first frame takes about half as long. The forest looks
the same on every machine, and on Windows a telescope photograph shows its
whole plate. Saves load as they were.

1.3.2 plays itself, and plays with a controller. Auto-explore (the menu's second
row) hands the game to an explorer of one of six tastes, from the stars and the
long jumps to the ruins and records of the dead peoples, which flies, lands,
walks, drives and reads at a hand's unhurried pace with nothing written on the
screen; while it plays, `M` starts and stops a tape, four tracks that come with
the game or your own music, a cassette deck clunking between the tracks. A controller now reaches every action, and any
controller can be set up button by button. A rocky world's moons stand farther
out, so a system has distance seen from the ship; a red dwarf's companion giant
no longer fills its worlds' skies, a Wolf-Rayet star's giants trail streaks of
gas instead of a white blob, and a capital's records lie in its buildings rather
than heaped on its archive's floor. The systems were regenerated (generation
15): saves load, and the moons and companion stars they show may have moved.

1.4.0 is the worlds and the wanderers. Thirty-eight new kinds of world, each
from a real world or a published model, join the old ones: magma oceans,
glass, salt and rust worlds in the inner orbits; archean, hothouse and
superhabitable worlds; the eyeball and terminator worlds of the red dwarfs,
hycean and ammonia worlds and snowballs; felisian worlds that froze or were
scorched, one in eight with a dead people's ruins; nitrogen, cryovolcanic and
dry ice worlds in the far cold; grooved, two-faced, tiger-striped and sponge
moons; and water-cloud, azure, alkali and silicate giants, gas dwarfs and
super-puffs, each a different fall for the probe. A world holds up to six
traits instead of three, thirty of them new, from great tides, hot springs and
milky seas to airglow, fog seas, petrified forests and meteorite fields, and
three are heard rather than seen: singing dunes, singing ice and quakes.
Between the stars drift worlds with no sun, heard only by the radar turned
above x1, each in a voice of its own: locked, the ship jumps to a black disc in
the dark. On their ground the explorer carries a lamp, and on one in four of
the frozen felisian and desert ones lie the ruins of a people whose records
tell how they lost their sun. The worlds were regenerated (generation 16):
saves load, 1.3.2's stars and peoples where they were, and a world may now be
of a new kind.

## Playing

Download the build for your machine from the
[releases page](https://github.com/pibblokto/vesperis/releases), unzip it and
run `vesperis` (on macOS, `Vesperis.app`). The game writes its save, settings
and guide files next to itself (the macOS app uses `~/Documents/vesperis`).

* **macOS**: the app is not signed. If it is refused, right-click it and choose
  *Open* once, or run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine Vesperis.app`.
* **Linux**: needs an X11 display (XWayland is fine) and OpenGL 3.3.
* **Windows**: a single `vesperis.exe`, no installer.

Press `H` at any time for the key reference, `Esc` for the menu.

## Building from source

Requirements: a C++17 compiler and [raylib](https://www.raylib.com) 5.x
(`brew install raylib` on macOS; on Linux install the `raylib` development
package or let CMake fetch it). Then:

```
make                # builds ./vesperis (the game) and ./vesperis_test (headless checks)
./vesperis
```

CMake works too: `cmake -B build-cmake && cmake --build build-cmake -j`. Add
`-DVESPERIS_FETCH_RAYLIB=ON` to download and build raylib when none is
installed (Linux, Windows), `-DVESPERIS_BUILD_GAME=OFF` to build only the
headless harness, and `ctest --test-dir build-cmake` runs the headless checks.
`packaging/make_app.sh` wraps the macOS binary into `dist/Vesperis.app`,
`packaging/make_zip.sh` makes a portable zip, `web/build_web.sh` targets the
browser with emsdk (experimental). The release's six zips come from
`packaging/build-release.sh` (after `packaging/toolchains.sh`; the Linux builds
run in Docker).

The headless harness needs no window: `./vesperis_test flow` plays through a
whole expedition and dumps frames to `shots/tests/`, `./vesperis_test unit`
runs the unit tests, `./vesperis_test regress` the determinism and frame
hashes; `docs/reference/08-testing-and-tools.md` describes every mode.

## Controls

A controller works out of the box: the left stick walks, the right stick looks,
A confirms (jumps on foot), B goes back, X uses, START opens the menu, and **Y
opens the commands**: every action of the screen you are on as a list to pick
from, a page of general ones and an on-screen keyboard with every key, so the
whole game is playable from a controller whatever its buttons. Text entry and
the console bring the keyboard up by themselves. Settings > Controls has a
controller page: press the button you want for each of A, B, X, Y, the bumpers,
triggers, sticks and d-pad (any controller, known or not: the game reads its
buttons, axes and hats raw), with look speed, inversion and dead zone; it is
saved per controller in `vesperis_pad.txt`. A controller the system does not
know the layout of can also be taught with SDL's `gamecontrollerdb.txt` placed
next to the game. Keyboard layouts: copy `vesperis_keys.example.txt` to
`vesperis_keys.txt` and list `PHYSICAL LOGICAL` pairs, or use the keyboard page
of Settings > Controls.

In space:

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Mouse / arrows | Look around |
| `R` | Remote target: aim at a star, `Enter` or click to lock; `N` cycles the nearest stars; type light years and `Enter` to jump that far along the aim |
| `V` | Vimana flight to the remote target (`V` again aborts) |
| `L` / click | Local target: next body, then the asteroid belts / the body under the crosshair |
| `Tab` | Solar system analyzer (bodies and belts; `Enter` approaches, `L` targets) |
| `Enter` | Fine approach to the local target |
| `X` | Center the view on the local target |
| `Z` | The telescope (zoom, the reticle, photographs) |
| `O` | Toggle orbit / fixed point chase; `Shift`+arrows carry the parked ship round its world |
| `F` | Field amplificator (brighter star field) |
| `G` / `M` / `J` | The GUIDE (name stars and worlds, notes, targets by name or coordinates, home, history, export/import) / star map (`Tab` turns to the galaxy chart: `Enter` plots a jump to the cursor, `Enter` again flies it) / expedition log |
| `C` | Deploy the surface capsule (from orbit around a landable world), or drop a probe into a gas giant |
| `B` | The signal radar (the sweep, lock a signal, its distance and age; `+`/`-` turn the gain up to hear farther) |
| `E` at the cabin's back wall | The shard decoder: a world's records, timeline and history (`Tab`; `Shift` with the arrows runs the cursor), read, hear and play what the ruins held, name a piece with `N`; `Tab` at the worlds turns to the ship's player |
| `E` at the cabin's left wall | The instruments' case (play what the ruins held) and, by the front window, the drone bay (`Enter` sends three drones down for records and instruments) |
| `I` (`F2`) | Data sheet of the local target |
| `T` / `Ctrl+T` | Time warp x1 / x10 / x100 / x1000 / x10000 / the almanac |
| `Ctrl+S` / `Ctrl+L` (`F5` / `F9`) | Save / load |
| `Ctrl+P` / `Ctrl+Shift+P` / `Ctrl+R` | Photo mode / panorama / frame recorder to `movies/` |
| `Ctrl+K` / `Ctrl+F` / `P` (`F10` / `F11` / `F12`) | Scanlines / fullscreen (also `Alt+Enter`) / screenshot (saved in `shots/`) |
| `H` (`F1`) | Help |
| `Esc` | Menu |

You live in the ship's cabin: the mouse turns your head, `W A S D` walk between
the windows, `E` uses what you face (the console on the left, the flight
computer at the front, the screens on the right, the capsule in its cage), `U`
lights the cabin, `Y` turns the hull to glass, `PgUp`/`PgDn` take the lifter to
the roof deck. The classic cockpit camera is one setting away (SHIP VIEW).

Landing map: arrows move the cursor (`Shift` = fast), `Z` zooms into the
cursor's sector, `R` picks a random lit land site, `C` toggles the cloud
overlay, `J` jumps under the biggest body in that world's sky, `V` shows the
peoples (where their records were kept; a people's whole map once all its
records are held), `Enter` lands, `Esc` returns.

On the surface:

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Mouse | Look around |
| `W A S D` | Walk (`Shift` sprints, with stamina) |
| `Space` | Jump; held in the air, the jetpack thrusts |
| `1`-`9` / `0` | Autowalk speed presets / stop |
| `C` / `Z` | Crouch / stand tall (hold) |
| `Ctrl` | Dive while swimming |
| `B` | Unfold a buggy next to the capsule; `E` gets in and out; `W/S` drive, `A/D` steer, `Space` brake; `V` outside view |
| `F` | Launch the drone from the capsule (`Space` lifts off, `W/S` thrust, `A/D` turn) |
| `M` | Set a waypoint on the spot under the crosshair, or clear it |
| `Q` / `E` | Board the capsule when standing next to it (return to orbit); `E` also takes a record or an instrument from a ruin |
| `K` | Call the capsule to your position |
| `R` / `Shift+R` | The scanner: ruins, works, lakes, peaks, craters, canyons, mesas, geysers, crystals, off / leave out or take in what you have marked |
| `Tab` | The readings: the place, the sky, the air, the ground, the vehicle |
| `L` | Mark the spot under the crosshair and name it |
| `N` | Sector map (2 km to 2048 km wide): a cursor (arrows or mouse), `Enter` marks or renames, `Delete` twice removes, `M` places the waypoint, `Tab` the next mark, `+`/`-` zoom |
| `G` | The GUIDE: name the world, write a note |
| `V` | Vision modes |
| `X` | Highlight nearby creatures |
| `I` (`F2`) | Environment data sheet |
| `T` / `Ctrl+T` | Time warp / the almanac |
| `Ctrl+S` / `Ctrl+L` | Save / load |

Auto-explore: the menu's `AUTO-EXPLORE` hands the game to an explorer that plays
by itself in one of six profiles (WANDERER, STARGAZER, PLANETFALL, SURVEYOR,
ARCHAEOLOGIST, GIANTS), with no HUD; any key or button gives the controls back.
While it plays, `M` (a controller's `SELECT`) starts and stops a tape: the game
comes with four tracks, Jarrad Wright's *Forwards*, *It's time to wake up* and
*BLUE-MEAN-EYES* and Black Sabbath's *Planet Caravan*, in its `music` folder.
Your own MP3, OGG or WAV files go in that folder too (for the macOS app, in
`~/Documents/vesperis/music`, which then plays instead of the game's tape). Its
`playlist.txt` sets the order and the credit shown as each track begins, a track
a line: `file | author | title | gain in dB`; files it does not list play after.

The expedition is saved to `vesperis_save.txt` in the working directory when
you quit, and can be continued from the title screen. Names, notes and the log
live in `guide.txt`; settings in `vesperis_settings.txt`.

## Layout

```
src/core      math, hashing, noise, 8-bit framebuffer + palette + smoothing filter,
              scanline rasteriser with depth buffer, 5x7 font, PNG writer, input
src/galaxy    star field and star classes, system generator, the planet function,
              drainage, landmarks and maps
src/peoples   the peoples that lived on some worlds: their histories, towns, buildings and ruins,
              records and their grammar, music and signals
src/space     star field, suns, ray-traced globes and rings, sky bodies
src/surface   landing site geometry, terrain caches, sky, terrain, water, objects, life
src/game      state machine, autopilot, HUD, menus, save/load, audio synthesis
src/platform_raylib.cpp   window, input and audio via raylib
src/harness/              vesperis_test: tests and screenshots without a window (`vesperis_test help`)
docs/         development notes: bug reports, plans, reference notes
```
