# B-404: the ground changes colour in front of you (a brown band ends a hundred metres out and moves along)

status: resolved 2026-10-01
severity: S2
area: terrain, planet function

## Report

On an airless icy moon (Eshhioyioth IV, a europan world: 28.2 S 48.8 E, -142 C, 0.11 g), flying the jet at 13-15 m, the ground near the explorer was red-brown and the ground beyond a wobbly edge was pale cracked ice; the edge kept its distance as the explorer flew, "the brown thing renders as you go, just in front of you", while the mountains in the distance had their normal rendering distance. The same was seen on another world. "Unacceptable, doesn't make sense." Three screenshots.

## Cause

The planet function samples every terrain ring at the ring's own cell size (`detailMeters`: 4, 16, 64, 512 and 2048 m), and fades each feature by that scale (`lodFade`: full at twelve samples per wavelength, gone under four), so that a coarse ring draws a smooth version of the same ground and the finer rings add the relief as they take over (B-313's geomorph slides the heights in). The materials followed the same faded values: the europan ground is dust (the brown stain of the lineae) where `stain > 0.5`, and the stain of each of the three lineae sets was multiplied by the set's fade. The 6 km set's stain (a band 90 m wide) was faded to 0.41 at 16 m, so the 16 m ring never crossed the threshold, and the brown bands of that set ended exactly at the near ring's edge, 120 m from the explorer; the 40 km set's (330 m) ended at the 16 m ring's edge, 640 m out. The edges swept along with the camera. The reference note's rule ("material decisions must depend only on values available at both detail levels, never on `detailMeters` itself", `05-planet-function.md`) was broken wherever a material was decided by a faded term: the lineae stains, the lava tongues and pit chains (`lavaFlows`, half there in the 64 m ring and gone at 512), the cliff bands' rock and scree (`cliffBands`: the riser's rock followed the fade of the band's run, so hillsides turned to rock within 120-640 m), the icy and molten worlds' crack lines (`crackAt`, faded by the cell's size), every landform's material (`landformsAt`: the canyon walls, the mesa rims, the karst towers' walls, the inselbergs' rock, the cinder cones' dark ground, the chaos cracks, the ergs' sand (the whole sand sea turned to dust beyond the 64 m ring: 30% of a desert world's ground), the playas' salt, the trap terraces' basalt, the sinter) and the slope-based rules (the felisian bare rock over 1:1.6 and the thin-atmosphere rock on high ridges read the relief's slope at the ring's own scale, which the fine octaves steepen).

The new harness mode `matlod` measured it over 1.5 million points on 204 random land sites of every landable type (three bodies and four sites a type, a 6 km square at 75 m): the material differed between the 4 and 16 m samples on 0.31% of all ground, between 16 and 64 m on 0.78%, 64 and 512 m on 4.8%, 512 and 2048 m on 5.4%; on europan worlds 4.4% (4/16) and 3.4% (16/64), both dust to ice; on volcanic worlds 2.6% and 4.9% of basalt (the tongues) to sulphur; on deserts 30% of sand to dust at 64/512; on icy worlds 9% (64/512) and 23% (512/2048) of rock to ice; at the reported site 9.5% of the ground within 3 km was dust at 4 m and ice at 16 m.

## Fix

A material is decided by the unfaded feature wherever its patch spans at least a quarter of a cell of the sampling scale (`matVisible(widthKm, detail)`; half a texel on the world map), so every ring that can draw a patch at all draws the same one, and a patch narrower than that is the coarser ring's surroundings (a line narrower than a cell comes out as a chain of cells with gaps, which is what a line looks like at that scale). The relief keeps its fades. Per feature: the europan stain is unfaded within three spans of its line (90, 330 and 900 m) and the double ridges fade by their own width (`featFade`, four spans) rather than by `lodFade` of an arbitrary kilometre figure (the 12 m ridges stood at 41% in the 16 m ring); the lava tongues and pits decide the basalt where they span a quarter cell and fade their relief by their own width; the crack lines return a faded value for the relief and an unfaded one for the material (`Crack {h, m}`); the cliff bands' strength is free of the scale (`on`), the relief fades by the band's run as before and the rock and the scree come where the riser (a tenth of the run) spans a quarter cell; the landforms fade by the width of the thing they draw (`featFade`: full at four samples, gone under one and a half; the canyons by their half-width times two, each mesa cap, karst tower, inselberg, cirque and sinter mound by its own diameter, the cinder cones by a kilometre, the chaos blocks by 4 km and their cracks by 240 m, the trap terraces by their run on the material slope) and decide their material by the width of its patch (the walls, the rims, the risers); the ergs' sand is a province, so it is sand at every scale whether or not the draa can show; the playas are unfaded wherever one spans a quarter cell; the slope-based rules (bare rock, scree, the dry ridges, the snow line's aspect, the thin-atmosphere rock, the cliff zone) read a material chain of the relief spectrum (`ReliefSum::hM/slopeM/gradM`): the octaves of 48 m and longer, unfaded at every sampling scale, with the same erosion and hybrid weights (which read only the chain's own coarser octaves), so the slope that decides a material is the same number in every ring, the glacier's depth too (`LandCtx::hM`, the smooth ground minus that chain). Two gates that no ring ever passed are fixed on the way: the crystal spires asked for a 3 m sample (`scaleVisible(0.01)`) and were never drawn though the crystal landmarks stood on them, and the salt flats' cracks asked for 1.7 m; both are the 4 m ring's now.

After: the material differs between the 4 and 16 m samples on 0.06% of all ground (the water planes under river banks and the beaches, a cell and a half wide at every scale by the drainage's design), between 16 and 64 m on 0.46%, 64 and 512 m on 3.0%, 512 and 2048 m on 3.6%; on europan and volcanic worlds the three walked rings agree exactly; at the reported site the 4, 16 and 64 m samples agree everywhere within 3 km, and the frame from 15 m up shows the brown bands running to the horizon. `unit` guards it (the first europan, volcanic, desert and felisian worlds, two sites each). The far rings' residuals are KI-341: the icy worlds' 54 m crack lines end at the 512 m ring (2.3 km, where a line is five pixels wide at the horizon), the 90 m lineae bands and the chaos cracks there too, the drainage's lines widen by the ring, a height threshold (a snow line, a beach, a lava shore, a playa's rim) moves with the far ring's smoother ground.

The planet maps of the regression (ten worlds) changed in one: the felisian world's rock and biome texels follow the material chain's slope. `maps` shows the desert and felisian maps changed (the ergs are sand from orbit now, as they are on the ground; the rest of the maps are bit-identical) and the scene frames of the regression did not move; `terrain` meets the same 25 of 48 class rows as before (cliffs up by 0.1-0.6% on the mountain rows, the 16 km relief of the far samples up where the landforms now reach the 512 m ring). The generation version stays 11 (the G/S series' bump, before anyone keeps a save); `tests/regress_baseline.txt` re-blessed for the one map.
