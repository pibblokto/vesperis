# B-403: green blobs moving in the night sky

status: resolved 2026-10-01
severity: S2
area: surface, sky

## Report

On a thin-atmosphere world of a pulsar system (64.8 S, 0.02 atm) every night the sky held four big green shapes: solid, hard-edged, morphing and drifting smoothly, there all night long; looking straight up they met in a five-petal flower at the zenith. By day the sky was normal. The HUD's environment line read `AURORA`. Six screenshots.

## Cause

The aurora of M4-06 was a per-pixel function of the view direction's azimuth: `0.5 + 0.5 sin(5 az + t/4 + noise)` to the fourth power, times a step in elevation. Five lobes round the zenith, where the azimuth is undefined, so they met in a point (the flower); a threshold of 0.12 cut them with a hard edge and the shade started at 18 of bank 11 (a visible green) right at that edge; and they were written in the sky loop before the stars, whose mask hides a star behind any pixel of shade 22 or more, so nothing showed through them. Round a pulsar (activity 0.9) at 65 degrees the strength reached 0.6 and each lobe grew to a quarter of the sky, drifting at three degrees a second. No harness frame had ever shown one: the `surface` night frames stand at 12 degrees of latitude and the `north` frame is by day.

## Fix

The aurora is curtains in the sky's own geometry. Three sheets of light hang 90-250 km up along the auroral oval, whose latitude is hashed per world (63-69 degrees), a few degrees poleward of the site, over it or beyond it inside the oval (`SurfaceView::auroraOvalLat`; the centre's distance kept within -120..450 km so every high-latitude site sees something). Each sheet is a slab 10-16 km thick (a dense core in two thinner envelopes, for soft edges) whose light follows a gamma profile in height: nothing under 90 km, a bright lower border at 114, 85% gone by 200 km and a faint tail to 400. A cell of the cloud grid (4 logical pixels) takes the light along its ray: the heights where the ray enters and leaves each slab give the profile's integral, over the ray's climb at the border (the ground curving away under it), so a sheet seen along its length is bright, one crossed steeply is faint, and a ray under the border sees nothing. The sheets fold by two slow sine waves along their length, carry rays from two octaves of noise read where the ray crosses the sheet (so from under a curtain they converge toward the zenith; a far arc is a smooth band; a ray running along the sheet sees their average), patches, a breathing over seventeen seconds, the oval drifting over five minutes. The grid's value is tone-mapped with a soft knee (`44 (1 - exp(-4 strength light))`): the sheets seen along their length saturate, a steep crossing stays dark green. Bank 11's dark end is the night sky's zenith colour (a thin atmosphere's night sky is brown; against black-green the faint top of a curtain broke into patches of a different hue at the same darkness). The grid is interpolated per pixel and written after the sky bodies and the stars, on the sky bank's pixels where it beats the pixel's own shade, the twilight glow near the sun washing it out smoothly: no threshold, so it fades into the night. The stars show through it; a sky body's lit disc is brighter and stays, its night side shows the curtains in front, since they hang 100-250 km up, far under any moon or parent; rain and a dust storm take it away, as the rain takes the stars (the night clouds hide neither: KI-336). About a millisecond at 4x. Scenes `thinatmo_aurora` (a thin-atmosphere world round a pulsar or blue giant, 64 degrees, the darkest hour, facing the pole), `thinatmo_aurora_zenith` (the site right under the oval, looking straight up) and `aurora_moon` (any clear-air world with a moon or the parent in the poleward sky at the curtains' height, chosen by a trial frame that counts aurora pixels round its disc: a crescent's night side shows the curtains, its lit limb stays); the scene output prints the strength, the star class, the oval's latitude, the centre's distance, the cloud cover and the flocks in the air.
