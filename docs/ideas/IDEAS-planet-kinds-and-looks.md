# Ideas: new planet kinds, and new looks for all of them

Kept from a discussion on 2026-09-30. Not scheduled or designed. Today: 22 body types,
19 landable, up to three traits per world; the weather fronts of `IDEAS-world-and-sky.md`
overlap with the second list.

## New planet kinds

* **Ammonia world.** Cold, with ammonia-water seas that stay liquid far below freezing, a
  yellow-brown haze, sluggish waves, ice floating in slabs. A second habitable band for
  cold systems.
* **Tidally locked "eyeball" world.** Around red dwarfs: an ocean or a green ring at the
  terminator, a baked desert under the fixed sun, an ice cap on the night side, winds that
  never change direction. Its ground never sees the sun move.
* **Rogue-captured world.** A frozen wanderer caught by the system in a wild orbit: an
  atmosphere frozen out as snow on the ground, cryovolcanoes, a sky where the sun is a
  bright star. Seasons measured in centuries.
* **Supercritical world.** A hot super-Earth whose ocean has no surface: the air thickens
  into water with no shoreline, the light fails into a pressure fog, the "sea" is a place
  you sink through. Landable only at the poles, or not at all.
* **Fungal world.** Life without light: dim under a dead star, mats and columns of pale
  growth fed by heat from below, luminous spores in the air. The bestiary's grazers become
  slow crawlers.
* **Salt world.** An evaporated ocean: crusts, brine pools, pillars, the ground white and
  blinding, mirages, the horizon lost. Karst and salt flats exist as traits; this is a
  whole world of it.
* **Iron rain world.** A scorched world whose night side rains metal: glowing clouds, a
  red glow at the horizon of the day side; or a hot giant seen from its one landable moon.
* **Chthonian world.** The stripped core of a gas giant that wandered too close: a smooth
  mantle of rock and metal with a thin remnant atmosphere, a vast ring system of what it
  lost.
* **Shattered world.** A body broken and re-accreted: a globe of rubble with chasms
  kilometres deep, gravity that varies across the surface, a companion fragment sharing
  its orbit.
* **Shepherd moon in a ring.** A place rather than a type: landing on a small moon inside
  a ring plane, the ring as a wall of stones on the sky from horizon to horizon.
* **Pelagic world with a single island.** An ocean world whose map holds one landmass; the
  landing map's random site always finds it. Rare and memorable.
* **Glass world.** A bombarded world near a hot star whose surface melted to glass:
  reflections of the sky in the ground, sharp shadows, dunes of fragments.

First picks: the eyeball world and the ammonia world, both filling real gaps in cold
systems.

## New looks, textures and effects

* **Atmospheric scattering with altitude.** Haze thickening toward the horizon, mountains
  bluing with distance, the sun reddening as it sets by the world's own pressure and
  dust. The sky has colour bands today; this makes distance visible on the ground.
* **Cloud shadows.** The cloud overlay exists on the map; clouds drifting over the ground
  as moving shadows change every daylight scene for almost nothing.
* **Wet ground.** After rain: darker material, puddles reflecting the sky (the reflection
  pass exists for water), streams forming in the gullies and draining away over minutes.
* **Snow and frost by time of day.** Frost at dawn on cold worlds that burns off by noon,
  snow lines that move with the season, snow that keeps on the north slopes. The climate
  model has the numbers.
* **Dust and sand in the air.** Dust devils exist; a haze layer that dims the sun to a
  disc you can look at, sand streaming off dune crests, deposits on the buggy.
* **Material detail by distance.** Rocks and pebbles scattered per material, cracked mud
  in dry basins, ripples on sand, moss on the shaded side of rocks on wet worlds. Texture
  tiles keyed by material and slope, faded with distance as the flora is.
* **Volumetric light.** Crepuscular rays through cloud gaps or dust, light shafts in
  canyons at low sun, the sun's glow through a geyser's plume.
* **Wind on things.** Vegetation bending and water rippling in the direction of the
  weather; spray off wave crests.
* **Heat shimmer.** The far ground wavering at noon on hot worlds; over lava, always.
* **Bioluminescence.** Night on living worlds: glowing flora along rivers, tides that light
  up when disturbed, creatures with lights. A trait, not a type.
* **Ring light and planetshine.** The parent's glow lighting a moon's night side in the
  parent's colour, ring arcs lighting the ground faintly, sharper double shadows under
  two suns.
* **Palette moods per world.** The material ramps come per body; a per-world grade (a
  cold blue cast, sepia, a bleached desert) chosen from the traits, so two felisian
  worlds do not look like the same place.
* **Weathering on objects.** The capsule and the buggy pick up the world: dust, mud,
  frost, scorch, kept until the next landing.
* **Ground fog and mist.** Valley fog at dawn on wet worlds, sea mist at the coast, steam
  over hot springs and geyser fields, low and drifting.

First picks: atmospheric scattering and cloud shadows, because they change every frame
on every world.
