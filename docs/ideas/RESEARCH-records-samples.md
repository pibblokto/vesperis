# H-01 samples: the prototype's records beside C-02's

Printed 2026-10-08 by H-01's scratch prototype, for reading beside the texts the game gives the same two worlds today (C-02).
The prototype and its grammar are out of the tree, as CLAUDE.md's rule for experiments asks; what it showed and the choice it
led to are in `RESEARCH-records.md`. Fifty records of three genres: twenty-five on each world (nine letters, eight chronicles,
eight epitaphs) in the order of their years, then one plague told from three sides on each world.

How to read them:

* **The ages are set by hand.** The Rauzhia of Aieliaalas II are a medieval people. The Feloya of Leileashphail III went
  through a medieval age and reached the industrial one in year 309 of their count; most of their records are from that
  age. A-01 will draw each people's age from its seed; these are not its draws.
* **The history is a stub of H-02's**, simulated year by year:
  * rulers in a line (and for the Feloya, from year 309, an assembly and its chancellors);
  * six towns;
  * ten families with their births, marriages, moves and deaths;
  * the public events of each year: harvests, famines, sickness, wars and their battles, floods, droughts, earthquakes,
    buildings, the sky's omens, and for the Feloya the machines and the strikes;
  * each people's collapse: the Rauzhia's a sickness that came back in waves until the towns were left, the Feloya's
    the drying of their sea and their bores.

  Every death in a family has its cause, so a letter, a chronicle and a stone can tell the same plague or battle.
* **The names are the people's own.** A record names what its writers named. The game's rule that nothing has a name until
  the explorer gives one is about the game's own screens; the decoder already shows a record's names as they are.
* **On the ship's decoder they read in capitals** (the font has no lowercase). A letter's lines are its paragraphs.
* C-02's texts are the game's: the world's fifty shards less the pieces of music, as `vesperis_test shards <sx> <sz> <body> 50 10`
  prints them (ten shards held, so every word is read).

## Aieliaalas II (sector 151 0 25, body 1): the Rauzhia, medieval

*The history the records read.* 343 years from the founding of Aiyin. Sixteen rulers in one line, nine wars with the people's own towns (nineteen battles), thirteen sicknesses, fifteen famines, eight floods, six earthquakes, thirteen buildings finished, five towns founded after the first. ## Leileashphail III (sector 153 0 70, body 2): the Feloya, medieval, then industrial from year 309

*The history the records read.* 542 years from the founding of Eitroolt. Seventeen kings and queens until year 309, when the hall goes to an Assembly and its chancellors. Ten wars (eighteen battles), forty famines and thirty-six droughts on a drying world, with the sea's retreat measured at the quay. In the industrial age come the steam pump (341), the railway (372), the telegraph (403), gas lamps (434), the deep bores (465) and voices on the air (496), with twenty-seven strikes and riots. From year 454 the bores run dry, the towns are left (Gupaux in 495 to Eyngbalo in 531) and the families go north and into the mountains. 1,698 persons in ten families.

## One plague from three sides

A people's records are one catalogue. An event is told once in a record, and other records of the world can tell it again
from where they stand. In the reading sets above, the grey fever of year 318 is both a chronicle's entry and Pithoom's stone
(Aieliaalas II, records 22 and 23), and the waterworks at Otyathea is both a chronicle's line and Bricecio's letter
(Leileashphail III, 7 and 8). Below, the prototype picked a sickness on each world that killed a member of one of the
families. It printed the chronicle of that year, a letter from a family it struck, and the dead person's stone:

**Aieliaalas II: the sickness of year 42 at Aiyin** (Skookless died of it, aged 63)

*chronicle, year 42*

> The 42nd year from the founding. That spring, sickness came to Aiyin from the hill farms; some three hundred dead before summer.

## The measures

Each sample counts sentences of six words or more whose frame (the words, with names, digits and number words masked)
occurs twice or more in it. The genre's formulas are left out: a letter's greeting and closing, a chronicle year's heading, a
stone's name and blessing. C-02's texts have no formulas. The prototype's samples are drawn from 3,000 records a world, C-02's
from its first fifty shards and then from shard indices 0 to 600.

The harness checks found nothing in 3,000 records a world except lengths at the genres' edges.
