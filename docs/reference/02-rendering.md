# Rendering

## Framebuffer and palette (M10-01/03)

* Logical size 320x200 (`UW`, `UH`) times a runtime scale `FB_SCALE` 1..4 (`FBW`, `FBH`, set by `setFramebufferScale`; the settings item "render scale", default 4 = 1280x800 since B-310 on 2026-09-27, one framebuffer pixel per pixel of the default window; it was 2 = 640x400 before). Every `Framebuffer` is re-created after a scale change (`Game::applySettings`). `Framebuffer::idx` holds 16-bit pixels (`Pix`); `invz` holds 1/z per pixel (0 = infinitely far, larger = closer).
* Pixel format: **16 banks x 12-bit intensity**: bank = `p >> 12`, intensity = `p & 4095`; 64 intensity units per classic shade, so writers still think in shades 0..63 (`pix(bank, shade)` keeps fractions; `pixI(bank, inten)`, `bankOf`, `intenOf`, `shadeOf`, `toInten`). Bank arguments everywhere are plain indices (0..15), never pre-shifted.
* Palette: 64 colour stops per bank (`pal[16*64*3]`); `setRamp(pal, bank, {{pos, RGB}, ...})` defines a bank as a piecewise linear ramp through stops at positions 0..63; `scalePalette` fades all banks. `toRGB(out, gain)` interpolates the 64 stops to 256 steps per bank and looks pixels up by `intensity >> 4`, so smooth gradients (sky, glow) no longer show 64-step banding.
* `Framebuffer::mush(passes)` is Noctis' `psmooth_64` generalised: for each pixel, keep the bank of the pixel one row below and replace the intensity by the average of the k x k block starting one row below. `g_mushKernel` = scale + 1 in the "classic" setting (2x2 at 1x, 3x3 at 2x, 4x4 at 3x, 5x5 at 4x; "soft" = scale + 2, "sharp" = max(2, scale), and since B-310 "fine" = max(2, scale - 1), the default: 3x3 at 4x, so two passes span five pixels, 1.25 logical pixels, and leaves, trunks and ridges resolve while the edges still melt); two passes everywhere. k = 2 keeps the original inner loop; larger boxes use separable running sums (windows clamped at the right edge). Consequences: colours never bleed across banks, edges melt, single-pixel points soften (stars and far bodies are therefore drawn as 2x2 logical blocks with a halo), the image shifts up by about one logical row per pass.
* `glowDisc(cx, cy, radius, coreRadius, maxAddShades, bankIfEmpty)` adds intensity inside a disc (sun glow, flares); `bankIfEmpty` -1 keeps the existing bank.
* Sizes in scale units (M10-15): line thickness, point size, star dots, the far-body point, the sun's minimum corona and the Vimana streak length are multiplied by `FB_SCALE`; the terrain grain scale too, so the grain keeps its on-screen size; the sky cloud grid cell is `4 * FB_SCALE` pixels; at 3x and 4x the sky is evaluated at half resolution.

## Rasteriser (`core/raster.*`)

* View space: x right, y up, z forward. `Proj::fromHFov(70)` gives focal `f = 160 / tan(35 deg) ~ 228.5` px; screen `sx = cx + f*x/z`, `sy = cy - f*y/z`.
* Near plane `NEAR_Z = 0.05`; polygons are clipped with Sutherland-Hodgman before projection (`rasterPolygon`), triangles fully in front skip clipping.
* Scanline fill with linear interpolation of 1/z, shade, and grain coordinates u,v (stored as u/z, v/z so they are perspective correct; a per-pixel divide recovers them). B-311: a texel of a world-mapped tile that spans more than about two and a half pixels on screen is read bilinearly (`GrainTexture::atLerp`, integer arithmetic), nearest beyond; the ground within a few metres used to be a mosaic of texel squares the mush could not melt.
* Two-material cells (B-311): `RVert::w` is a per-corner weight (1 at the corners of the cell's majority material, 0 at the others), interpolated like the grain coordinates; where `RasterParams::bank2` is set, each pixel's weight is wobbled by the world-mapped `edge` noise (`edgeScale` texels per unit, `edgeAmp` weight units per full amplitude) and the pixel goes to `bank`/`grain2`/the shade or to `bank2`/`grain2b`/the shade plus `shade2` on either side of the half-way contour: the boundary is an irregular line through the cell instead of its square edge.
* Band-parallel drawing (B-310): `setRasterBand(y0, y1)` clips every fill of the calling thread (triangles, lines, points) to those rows; the surface draws the terrain rings and the flora with one band per worker thread (`core/parallel.h`: `parallelFor` on a persistent `WorkerPool`, since a frame dispatches a dozen regions and threads created per region cost more than a millisecond). A line walks only the steps whose rows fall into the band.
* B-313/B-315 (2026-09-28): `RasterParams::zbias` multiplies the depth tested and written (a coarser terrain ring is pushed back 0.1% so the finer ring wins where the two coincide at a morphed edge); `RasterParams::cutout` drops every pixel whose wobbled weight falls under a half instead of sending it to a second material (the ragged rim and the holes of a leaf cluster, the serrations of a frond: the fan's centre vertex weighs 1.1 and the rim 0.3); the depth test comes before the texel reads, so a hidden pixel costs nothing more; the polygon clipper takes 16 vertices (a leaf cluster is a fan of up to 12).
* `RasterParams`: `bank` (already shifted, e.g. `2 << 6`), optional `grain` (64x64 signed texture added to the shade, `grainScale` texels per unit), `ztest`, `zwrite`, blend mode (`BLEND_REPLACE`, `BLEND_ADD`, `BLEND_MAX`), shade clamp.
* Lines (`rasterLine3`, with thickness) and points (`rasterPoint3`, size) are depth tested. 2D helpers: `fillRect`, `fillDisc`, `drawLine2D`.

The pixel (O0-02, 2026-09-27): `Pix` is still 16 bits, but the top 5 bits are the bank (32 banks, `BANK_MASK 0xF800`) and the low 11 bits the intensity (`INTEN_PER_SHADE` 32 units per classic shade, 0..2047). The mush, the glow discs, the rasteriser's blends and the RGB conversion work in those units through the constants; the ordered dither nudges by 12 units (1.5/16 of a stop, as before). The regression frames were re-blessed.

Body ramps (N1-02): banks 2/3 carry the rock family's representative material colour (`familyRep(type, FAM_ROCK)`: basalt on molten, venusian, volcanic and cratered worlds, metal, graphite, gas, else rock) from `BodyGen::matColor` times `lerp(starColour, white, 0.5)`, through the shared `materialRamp` (stops {0 black, 14 dark, 34 mid, 47 c, 56 c->white 50%, 63 white}; `dark` = 0.25c, or the body's noon sky light blended in on atmosphere worlds, `mid` = lerp(dark, c, 0.55)); molten and volcanic bodies keep the lava ramp (basalt in its dark part, lava at 50+), substellar bodies their glow ramp, venusian bodies the cloud colour. `SpaceRenderer::globeBank(bodyBank, material)` picks the pixel's bank by `matFamily` (A: 6, 11, 13, 14, 15; B: 7, 16, 17, 18, 19); gas giants, venusian and substellar bodies stay in one bank. The globe's value is `exposureStop(albedo, shade, atmosphere)` (`05-planet-function.md`), the same rule as the ground; the old `63 albedo^0.6 shade 1.1` is gone.

`SurfaceView::materialLook` maps `matFamily(material)` to these banks; a terrain quad takes the majority bank of its four corners (vote over 16 banks); the far floor ring uses the type's dominant family (water, ice on icy worlds, sand on cratered/thin-atmosphere/quartz/volcanic worlds, else rock). Objects: rocks bank 3 (bank 0 on living worlds, so they are rock-coloured and not forest-coloured), quartz crystals bank 2, trees/bushes/grass bank 3, birds bank 1 (dark silhouettes), grazers bank 3, capsule bank 1, rain bank 1. Lightning adds a flash to the whole palette. All colours come from `BodyGen::matColor` (N1); `lookFor` only chooses sky, fog, night and cloud parameters per type.

Distance fog is expressed inside the ramp: shade moves toward 63 (the fog colour) with `1 - exp(-dist / fogDistance)`.

## Draw order

Space: clear (or fade the previous frame by 9 shades during Vimana flight, which makes streaks) -> galactic band (M1-09, the map at half resolution; G-03: `buildGalaxyBand`, the starlight behind the dust on a fixed exposure in a 128 x 64 map, rebuilt after a hop of ten sectors, 24 shades of bank 0 at full brightness: the bulge 21, the plane 8-10) -> star field (2x2-logical dots with halo, brightness from luminosity/distance, optional streaks blue-shifted near the centre and red-shifted outside) -> sun (B-308: `drawSun` draws by angle per pixel from the unit direction to the star and its angular radius: the disc where a pixel's ray is within the radius, two glow falloffs outside it, the box the projection of the cone or the whole frame when the cone reaches the camera plane, rows in parallel; the rays of compact stars and blue giants and the point of a tiny sun stay screen-space while the centre is in front; the disc is never dimmer than the glow-lit sky round it; S-04: a protostar's dust disc is a second pass over the frame, cheap outside the strip along the orbital plane (`discUp`), see `04-galaxy-and-systems.md`; S-05: a Wolf-Rayet star's shell is a ring at five radii added in the glow's pass, whose cone is widened to reach it; S-06: a black hole is handed to `drawBlackHole` instead, which paints the shadow, bends the frame drawn so far (the band, the stars, the belts) round it through a lens map on a grid of one logical pixel, draws the accretion disc with its far half along the bent rays and a jet on some, see `04-galaxy-and-systems.md`; the lens flare is skipped for it; the old screen-space circle's radius `f tan(a) d/z` exploded as the centre neared the camera plane and flooded the frame with a companion star's colour) and lens flare (the sun radius clamped to a fifth of the frame) -> bodies far to near, each ray-traced per pixel (a globe is skipped only when its whole sphere lies behind the camera plane, O0-03/B-304: with its centre behind or beside the camera the rays are tested over the whole frame, since a huge parent's limb can fill the top of a moon's sky while its centre is behind you; B-312: the box of a globe in front is the exact screen extent of its sphere, `sphereBox`, the tangent planes through the camera's axes, united with the projection of the ring's outer circle, `ringBox`, so a parent whose centre has left the frame to the left or the right keeps its limb and its rings in view: the old box of radius `f tan(a) d/z` round the projected centre was empty with the centre ten degrees outside the frame, and the rings' box was never reached; sphere hit, normal -> body frame lat/lon -> `PlanetMap` albedo/material/cloud, relief shading from the height map's slope toward the star (M9-15), lighting, shadows of moons, the parent and the rings (M1-08), limb glow, rings with planet shadow and shepherd gaps; gas giants get zonal jets and night lightning; a globe whose map is still generating is drawn flat; far bodies are single logical pixels). A gas giant or a substellar object of a Wolf-Rayet star gets, before its globe, its envelope streaming away from the star (S-05, `drawStrippedTail`: forty glow discs of its own bank from the limb to four radii out, widening and thinning, curling back along the orbit and wavering slowly; in a sky it is drawn in the sun's bank, like the comet's tail). A black hole's companion gets, before its own disc, its gas streaming to the hole (S-06, `drawAccretionStream`: forty glow discs of the companion's bank 12 from the limb facing the hole to the disc's edge at nine radii, thin at the companion and flaring into the disc, bent back against its motion; in space only, since a sky draws its own companion sun). Asteroid belts are drawn as hashed points between the star field and the sun; after the globes, when the ship is inside a belt, `drawBeltRocks` (O3) draws the rocks within four cells: points under 1.6 px (bank 3, lit by phase), else lumpy meshes (a 6 x 3 or 12 x 6 sphere with fbm radii, tumbling, back faces culled, `0.06 + 0.94 lambert` shading, depth-tested), far to near. In cabin mode the cabin geometry is drawn after the space scene with depth (`Game::drawCabin`): floor, ceiling ring, wall bands and pillars, mullions, the cage bars and the capsule dome, the console and desk boxes with their screens, the bulb and the orb; a depolarised hull uses `BLEND_DARKEN` so the outside shows through tinted.

Surface: `setupPalette` -> `clearDepth` -> sky per pixel (direction LUT rotated by the camera; two cloud layers on a `4 x scale` grid, halo, twilight wedge) -> system bodies in sky mode (lit side mapped to the top of the sky ramp, dark side only writes depth; B-317: the depth in metres, the terrain's unit, since a moon 20,000 km away written in kilometres read as 20 km and stood in front of every hill beyond) -> stars masked where the sky is bright, twinkling through atmospheres -> the galactic band and the nebula patches (G-03: bank 22, from the night sky's colour to a pale star-grey, over the sky pixels whose light it beats, both read from the palette; hidden by the clouds; 20 shades at full brightness, the wings under two left to the sky; it used to be added to the sky's value, which the night ramp cannot show) -> the aurora (B-403: the curtain grid's shade over the sky-bank pixels darker than it, faded under the twilight glow, in bank 11 or, where the tops' share is the greater, 21 (R-402), and over the band's pixels (G-03); the stars and the lit discs of sky bodies stay, a body's night side shows it in front) -> meteors -> sun (depth-tested, so a body in front carves a partial eclipse; corona grows with the coverage) and lens flare -> the floor fan beyond the last ring at the minimum far height or sea level (curved; none on small bodies) -> the terrain rings, the near ring at 4 m (radius 30, O6-02), LOD0 (16 m cells, radius 40, inner 8 skipped under the near ring), LOD1 (64 m, radius 36, inner 10 skipped), LOD2 (512 m, radius 26, inner 5 skipped), LOD3 (2048 m, radius 26, inner 7 skipped; O1; B-313: a coarser ring skips the cells the finer covers up to a cell of overlap, `skipOf`, and each ring's outer band is morphed onto the coarser ring's surface, see 06 "Geomorphing"), with skirts and water quads at sea level (waves, foam); B-310: the vertex caches and the material tiles of every ring are filled first (`warmTerrainLOD`, the cells serially, the vertices in parallel by rows of cells; B-313: coarse to fine, since a ring's morph reads the coarser ring's vertices, three cells into its skip disc too), then every worker thread draws all rings near to far within its band of rows (`drawTerrainLOD` with `bandY0/bandY1` skips cells whose corners project wholly outside the band unless a skirt or water could reach in), reading only, so the pixels come out as the serial loop's -> reflections of the land into water pixels -> footprints and the explorer's shadow -> objects (with blob shadows) -> flora (band-parallel too: the trees are listed once per frame with the rows each can touch, a band thread places only those and skips a canopy blob wholly outside its rows before its points are placed) -> life -> capsule -> weather -> the veiling glare round the sun (B-309, `drawVeil`) -> `mush(2)` (3 on venusian worlds) -> `toRGB` -> HUD in RGB.

Terrain quads are shaded by the smooth gradient of their corner shades since B-311 (`shadingMode` 2, the default; the 4 m near ring had made the flat cells a mosaic); the flat cells of the original's `polymap` (average of the four corner shades, mode 0) and the 12-step gradient (mode 1, M10-12) remain in the settings. The grain texture (amplitude 9, 3 texels per metre at LOD0), the material meso tiles, the broad tone tile of the vertex shade and the mush provide the texture.

## HUD (M10-04)

Drawn after the mush in the RGB buffer with the 5x7 font (`core/font.*`): glyphs for ASCII 32..95 plus `CH_DEGREE`, arrows, block, dot. Lowercase maps to uppercase. Colours are constants in `game/ui.h` (`HUD_GREEN`, `HUD_AMBER`, ...). The HUD code lays everything out in logical 320x200 units (`UW`, `UH`); `RGBCanvas::scale` multiplies coordinates, glyphs, lines and rectangles by the resolution scale, so the HUD looks identical at every scale. Screen positions that come from the projection (`BodyScreenInfo`, `proj.f`) are framebuffer pixels and are divided by `FB_SCALE` before use in the HUD; pick radii (14 px stars, 18 px bodies) are multiplied by it. The landing map fits the 512x256 map into a 256x128 logical frame: one texel per pixel at 2x, every second texel at 1x.

## M5 additions (2026-09-26)

* Bank 12 is the companion star's ramp (space `setupPalette` when `sys.companion >= 0`; the surface when `env.hasSun2`), bank 13 the substellar glow ramp in a moon's sky. Space `bodyRamp` gives substellar bodies their own red ramp instead of the star-tinted one.
* `drawGlobe` draws a `PT_COMPANION` body with `drawSun` (depth-tested, bank 12) and returns; in sky mode the surface draws it itself. Every other globe is lit by `sys.lightAt(pos, t)`: the brighter of the two suns. Substellar globes take `shade = max(shade, selfGlow (0.3 + 0.7 albedo))`.
* N5-01 nebula patches (G-03: of the star-forming complexes within 42 ly): the backdrop loop of `SpaceRenderer::render` asks `nebulaPatches(obs)` for the patches and paints `nebulaGlow` (0..1) as bank 4 (blue), 5 (red) or 0 (white) at up to 17 shades where it beats the band. N5-03: gas giant globes read the storm term from the map's veg channel and replace the baked storm with `storm x (0.55 + 0.45 sin(t / 86400 x 0.9 + 4 lat + seed))`, so storms grow and fade over about a day, band by band.
* `drawCometTail` (called before the nucleus point or globe): activity `clamp((1.6 ref / d_star)^2)` with `ref` the first planet's orbit; a coma of 60,000 km and 36 tail discs widening to 960,000 km along the anti-star direction, `glowDisc` adds 30 and `18 (1 - f)^2` scaled by activity and `skyDark`. N0-01 (B-201) animates it without touching the orbit: the coma breathes (`1 + 0.1 sin(1.7t) sin(0.61t + 1.3) + 0.05 sin(3.1t)`), five knots (`cometKnot(k, t)`: `k/5 + jitter + t/40`, wrapped, Gaussian width 0.06 along the tail) modulate the ion tail by 0.82-1.18, and a dust tail of 24 discs (`0.55 lenKm g` along the anti-star direction plus `0.28 lenKm g^2` along `-velocity` projected off it, 100,000-1,200,000 km wide, adds `9 (1 - g)^2`) curves back along the orbit. When the nucleus is at least 3 px across, `2 + seed % 3` jets vent from the sunlit hemisphere (directions hashed from the seed, pushed toward the star when they face away), each 14 discs from 1.02 to 4 radii bending `2.2 R s^2` anti-starward, radius `0.1-0.45 R`, three knots per jet streaming out every 8 s; they turn with the nucleus frame.

## Presentation options (M6-01)

`Framebuffer::toRGB(out, gain, dither)`: with `dither` the 12-bit intensity is nudged by a 2x2 Bayer pattern (-24, +8, +24, -8 in intensity units, one cell per logical pixel) and snapped to whole palette stops, so ramps show the original's chequered steps instead of the 256-entry interpolation. `platformPresent(..., aspect)`: aspect 1 scales the picture 1.2x vertically inside the window (letterboxed), the way 320x200 filled a 4:3 monitor; scanlines and the CRT shader follow the drawn rectangle.

## Map cache (M7-02)

`SpaceRenderer` keeps at most `mapCacheCap` (24) planet maps, about 19 MB: `mapIfReady` stamps every use, `startMap` evicts the least recently used map that is not being generated before adding one. `mapStats` counts generated maps, hits and evictions; `genMicros` sums the worker generation time (`bench` prints the average: about 113 ms per 512x256 map on an M4).
