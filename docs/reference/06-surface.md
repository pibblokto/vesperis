# Surface

## Movement (M8, 2026-09-26)

`Player` carries the walking state; `SurfaceView::updateWalking` runs it when on foot, `updateBuggy` when driving, `updateDrone` when flying (R-403).

* Walk 4.2 m/s (x0.85 above 1.5 g, x0.5 crouched); sprint with `Shift` (hold, or toggle by setting) multiplies by `clamp(3.2 * (9.8/g)^0.44, 1.5, 4)` through a 0.6 s ramp and adds a 4-degree FOV kick (the sky table is rebuilt only when the focal length moves more than 1.2%). Stamina 0-100 drains at 12/s x max(0.6, g/9.8) while sprinting, recovers at 8/s walking and 16/s standing; below 1 you are winded until 25. The HUD shows the bar and a pulse (60 + 80 x fatigue + 30 x sprint); `AudioState::breath` breathes.
* Head bob from a stride phase (1.6 m stride, 2.4 m sprinting; 3.5-6.5 cm vertical, 2 cm sway), landing dip 4.5 cm per m/s of fall speed above 3 m/s (x1.4 on heavy worlds), postures `C` crouch (eye 0.9 m) and `Z` held stands tall (2.1 m, no walking).
* Slopes: the ground gradient (0.5 m finite differences) blocks climbing above rise/run 1.35 (only the along-slope motion remains) and slides you down at `0.8 g sin(slope)`.
* Jump apex `clamp(1.1 sqrt(9.8/g), 0.6, 6)` m (M8-09); air control 4 m/s^2 below 3 m/s^2 gravity else 2; terminal velocity 55 m/s with atmosphere, 80 without.
* Jetpack: hold `Space` in the air: +3 m/s^2 net, capped at 12 m/s up, heat +5/s (100 = 20 s), cools 8/s, ceiling 300 m above ground; the HUD shows altitude, vertical speed and heat while airborne. B-406 (2026-10-02): deep water (the ground 0.4 m or more under the water) is swum once the feet are within 0.3 m of the float level (0.6 m while already swimming); above it the air is the air, so the jet flies over a lake, and it fires from the water's surface too (afloat, not diving), lifting the explorer out; the wading slowdown wants the feet on the ground.
* Microgravity (O4, R-303; `SurfaceSite::escapeVelocity = sqrt(2 g R)` under 30 m/s, i.e. comets): the ground speed is capped at 0.3 of the escape velocity (walking or sprinting, 1-3 m/s), the jet's climb at 0.35 of it, `Ctrl` in the air thrusts down at 3 m/s^2 (on any world under 1 m/s^2 of gravity), the buggy refuses to unfold. A jump keeps its 6 m apex and floats for minutes (KI-302). The HUD prints `GRAV 0.0005 G  ESCAPE 9.2 M/S` and `CTRL DESCENDS` while airborne.
* Autowalk `1`-`9` holds a forward speed of n/4 walk speeds (up to 2.25x), `0` or any backward input stops it.
* Collision (`collectColliders`): rocks larger than 0.6 m (radius 0.8 x size) and tree trunks in the 3x3 cells around you, as cylinders; you are pushed out along the normal. The placement formulas mirror `drawObjects`. B-405: every collider carries the span it blocks in absolute metres (`y0..y1`: a rock to 1.15 sizes over the ground, a trunk to the tree's height, a log to a diameter, a monolith to its drawn height, a settlement's piece from its drawn foot to its top); the explorer skips what ends under the feet (the jetpack over a wall, a slope's buried wall) or starts over the head, the buggy what lies under its wheels or over its roll bar.
* Water levels are local (M9-04): `SurfaceSite::waterAt(x, z)` reads the per-vertex water surface (sea 0, lakes and rivers above it) and `surfaceHeight` is the higher of ground and water; the player, capsule, buggy, critters, trees and rocks all use it, water quads take their cell's level, reflections mirror about it. Wading slows you by up to 55% over 1.2 m of depth, swimming at 45% speed, `Ctrl` dives (down to 3.5 m, buoyancy returns you), underwater the palette goes blue-green and the fog closes to 22 m; swim stamina halves the speed when spent.
* Footprints and the explorer's blob shadow as before; the site re-anchors at 50 km (0.35 radii on a small body).
* Marks of a landing (B-303, 2026-09-27): `SurfaceView::init` clears the trail, the waypoint, the eruptions, the footprints and the buggy, and bumps `siteEpoch`; `reanchor` carries the trail's 600 points and the waypoint into the new frame through their unit vectors (like the capsule and the buggy) and bumps `siteEpoch` too, which tells the game's cached sector-map image to rebuild.
* Rangefinder (O1, B-302): `SurfaceView::rangeToGround(maxDist, hitX, hitZ, water)` marches the camera's line of sight in steps of 0.6 m growing 3% per step, comparing the ray's height with the ground under it (`heightNear`: bilinear lod0 within 0.9 of its ring, then the lod1/lod2/lod3 cell heights by distance, with the cell's water level; the curvature applied as in `toView`, the exact sphere on small bodies), and bisects the hit to a few centimetres; `render` stores `lastRange`, `lastRangeX/Z`, `lastRangeWater` for the HUD (`8.4 KM  33 MIN WALK`, `WATER`) and for `M`, which sets the waypoint on the spot under the crosshair. Beyond the drawn rings it reports the sky (-1).

The buggy (`Buggy`, M8-06, rebuilt in N4, closed in N4-06, `surface/buggy.cpp`): `B` within 10 m of the capsule unfolds a buggy beside the capsule over two seconds (the hull grows out of its folded state, the wheels drop); a buggy already out, wherever it was left, is scrapped by the new one (R-204); a new landing (`SurfaceView::init`) starts with no buggy and `reanchor` moves the buggy with the frame like the capsule (B-205). `E` within 3.5 m gets in, `E` again gets out (also boards the capsule when next to it) and reports the trip and top speed. Driving per frame: a steering lock of 30 deg at rest shrinking to 9 at the top speed with a 6/s response; a torque curve (O1-05, 2026-09-27) of 6 m/s^2 up to 18 m/s tapering to 3.4 at 50 m/s (180 km/h; before: 5 to 15 m/s tapering to 1.2 at 32 m/s, 115 km/h), 3 m/s^2 in reverse to 8 m/s, x2.5 against the motion, scaled by `0.55 + 0.45 traction`; drag `0.0011 v|v|` plus rolling resistance by material (sand 1.6, snow 1.5, dust 1.1, ice 0.5, else 1) x0.3; the slope term `0.8 g sin(slope)` over 3 m and the stall above 30 deg; `Space` handbrake (grip x0.35); traction = material (ice 0.35, snow 0.6, sand 0.8, grass and forest 0.9, dust 0.85, rock 1) x `clamp(g/9.8, 0.3, 1)`; turn rate `v tan(steer) / 2.2` x `(0.4 + 0.6 grip)` with a lateral slide `turn v 0.25 (1 - grip)` that feeds `skid`; collisions with trunks, logs, ruins and rocks over 0.9 m cost 60% of the speed and push back (a thump), rocks under 0.9 m are bumps (speed x0.88, a shake, at most every 0.6 s); water deeper than 0.4 m ahead stops it; the chassis follows the mean of the four wheel contacts (1.1 m half wheelbase, 0.85 m half track) and each wheel keeps its travel (`wheelY`, -0.35..0.35 m, 12/s); B-313 (2026-09-28): the hull rides on a suspension: its reference height is the ground averaged over 16 m along the way (five samples 4 m apart), kept within 0.3 m of the contact mean, so the 4 m ring's bumps of 4-24 m are soaked up by the wheels and the hull follows the ground's larger shape (a straight slope averages to itself, so the descent rule below sees what it did); airborne (B-307, 2026-09-27) only when the ground falls away faster than gravity and the suspension can follow, i.e. the vertical acceleration the ground asks for, `(vyGround - vy) / dt`, exceeds four g and at least 40 m/s^2 at speed (`Buggy::groundY` keeps the previous frame's contact height, so the slope's vertical speed is known in the air too); a launch keeps the current vertical speed (a crest throws it up, a lip lets it drop), and a landing is judged by the speed relative to the slope (a thump and up to 15% of the speed only above 1 m/s of relative fall); it used to launch, horizontally, as soon as the ground dropped faster than 3 m/s, which a 30% grade does above 10 m/s, and descended in a ladder of hops (`vesperis_test descent`); `vibration` grows with speed and roughness and `jolt` with thumps (both decay, 4/s and 5/s); gears 0-12, 12-28, 28-50 m/s set the engine pitch (0-9, 9-20, 20-32 before O1-05); dust darkens the body on sand and dust and rain washes it; tracks are a 400-entry ring of rear-wheel contact points drawn as 0.3 m wide darkened quads with alternating knobs, spray puffs come from the rear wheels by material (dust and sand tan, snow white, mud dark on wet or watery grass) and live 1.5 s; headlights come on below 4.6 deg sun altitude and light a 25 m ground cone in `vertexOf` (trees and rocks stay dark, KI-210). Views (N4-06, R-204): from inside, the nose camera. The eye is the lens of the camera pod (`buggyCameraMount`: 1.58 m ahead of the centre, 0.84 m up, in the hull's frame `buggyFrame` with its pitch and roll); the mouse pans it within `CAM_PAN` (+-70 deg, kept relative to the hull as it turns) and tilts it within -35..+25 deg (`CAM_TILT_DOWN/UP`), so you can never look back (KI-215); the picture takes the chassis' pitch (x cos pan) and roll, a smooth tremor of at most 1.5 cm and 0.4 deg (sines at 7.3, 11.9 and 9.1 Hz scaled by `vibration`; B-203 was per-frame random offsets) and a 5 cm dip with `jolt`. `cameraFeed` (called by `Game::renderSurfaceScene` after the mush, and by the `drive` and `scene` harnesses) gives it the CCTV look: palette blacks lifted to 16/255 and saturation x0.45 with a cold cast, then per pixel scanlines (x0.82 on odd logical rows), a corner vignette (down to 30%), +-1.5 shades of noise and a 5% brighter band rolling down every 4.5 s; the HUD draws the camera's interface (07). The hull is never drawn from inside. `V` is the outside chase camera 4.5 m behind and 2.2 m up. The model (`drawBuggy`, parked and chase views): a wedge hull on bank 6 (the ship's grey-blue) with stations nose 1.62 m (0.84 m wide, 0.44-0.74 m high), shoulders 1.0 m (1.72 m wide, belt at 0.92 m), cabin 0.5..-0.8 m (1.8 m wide, roof 1.5 m) and tail -1.55 m (1.44 m wide, 0.4-1.02 m): nose face, hood, a blacked-out sensor band (bank 0, tint 0.3) across the cabin's front slope and round its sides, roof, rear slope, tail, nose flanks, belts and five-sided cabin sides; wheel pods (five arc strips from the hull side out to 1.06 m over each wheel, plus a skirt); four 12-gon wheels (rubber bank 0) with tread knobs, chrome hubs and spokes, two suspension arms each; the camera pod on the nose (a box 1.44-1.62 m, 0.74-0.94 m high with a dark lens face, a bright lens dot and a red LED blinking while the buggy is live), a chrome lidar puck on the roof, a sensor mast with a blinking bank-14 light, chrome shoulder and roof lines (bank 7), a headlight bar across the nose (bank 1 when on), a tail light bar (bank 14, 58 under braking, 30 with the lights, 12 off); faces shaded per normal with a floor of 0.16 so they never go black. Saved as `buggy <deployed> <x> <z> <heading> <odometer> <in>`; `K` always recalls the capsule next to you (the status names a buggy left over 50 m away; B-205). `M` sets or clears a waypoint (saved), shown on the HUD and as a dotted ground line; the same line points at the capsule when it is more than 40 m away.

The drone (`Drone`, R-403, 2026-10-02, `surface/drone.cpp`): the buggy's hull shortened on two skids with four ducted thrust pods on arms, the second vehicle the capsule carries. `F` within 10 m of the capsule unfolds one on the capsule's other side from the buggy's over two seconds (the arms unfold outward with the hull; any drone already out is scrapped; none on a comet; a new landing starts with none; `reanchor` moves it with the frame). `E` within 3.5 m gets in once unfolded, `E` gets out on the ground only. Flying per frame: on the skids the pods idle at 0.3 and Space spins them up (1.6/s) to lift off past 0.7; thrust 9 m/s^2 forward, 5 back, x1.8 against the motion, drag `0.0008 v|v| + 0.5`, the clamp at 88.9 m/s (320 km/h, `DRONE_TOP_SPEED`) and 12 backwards; the turn 70 deg/s at rest shrinking to 24 at the top with a 4/s response, the hull banking `atan(steer v / g)` (at most 50 deg) and dipping its nose `-(0.02 accel + 0.14 v/top)`, both in the outside view only (the nose camera's gimbal levels the picture); Space asks +15 m/s, Shift -12, neither 0, the vertical speed following at 1.8/s; the ceiling 400 m over the ground (`DRONE_CEILING`); colliders at the hull's height (0.2..1.5 m over the skids) push it out with 30% of the speed reversed and a thump, small rocks ignored; the skids never go under `surfaceHeight` (water included): an arrival over 2.5 m/s down or 8 m/s along is a thump and halves the speed, a scrape decays it at 2/s, and touching with the speed under 3 m/s and Space released lands it; within 8 m of soft ground or water the downwash spawns three puffs every 0.08 s (dust, snow, spray or mud by the material; `drawPuffs` draws the buggy's and the drone's); the pods' spin `0.5 + 0.3 climb + 0.15 thrust + 0.1 speed` drives the fans' blur, the sound and a buzz of the camera (a faster, smaller tremor than the buggy's road). The camera pod's gimbal pans +-70 deg and tilts -60..+25 (`DRONE_TILT_DOWN`), the chase view sits 7 m behind and 2.8 m above. ## Lighting and textures (M10, 2026-09-26)

Per terrain vertex (`SurfaceView::vertexOf`): normal from the four cache neighbours; hemisphere ambient (`0.08 + 0.2 skyBrightness` with atmosphere, 0.11 without, times `0.55 + 0.45 n.y`, times `1 + 0.6 cloudCover`); sun term = Lambert x sunUp x light factor x (1 - 0.6 cloud) x cast shadow (`castShadowLit`: nine samples marched toward the sun over the same cache, inside the ring, soft edge, floor 0.4) x cloud shadow (the sky's cumulus layer projected along the sun ray); planetshine from `env.moonLight` at night; capsule beacon pool within 14 m; wet shore albedo x0.8; lava glow within two cells on molten worlds (+16 shades max); three-step Blinn specular on ice/quartz/snow (+6/+12). Fog toward the ramp top uses a haze distance that shortens toward the sun and lengthens away from it, thicker when cloudy (scattering lite).

Palette (`setupPalette`, N1-03): `lookFor(gen, star)` derives `ground`, `sand`, `snow`, `grass`, `secondary` and `tertiary` from `BodyGen::matColor` (times `lerp(star, white, 0.5)`, the same tint as the globe) and chooses only sky, fog distance, night colours and the cloud layer per type. `matRamp(bank, c)` = the shared `materialRamp(c, skyLight, day)` (stops 0/14/34/47/56/63, `05-planet-function.md`) with the surface's extras: the dark end blends toward the moon colour at night (on airless worlds "night" follows the sun's altitude, since the sky brightness there is always 0; before N1 planetshine washed out the ground by day), `lit` is warmed by the sunset tint and flattened by 12% per unit of overcast, stop 63 is the fog colour. Banks: 0 rock family, 2 water/lava (S-03: the glass of a glassed world, `L.secondary = matColor[MAT_GLASS]`), 3 forest, 8 snow/ice, 9 sand family, 10 grass (bank table in `02-rendering.md`). S-03: the quantised specular of ice, quartz, snow and iron applies to glass as well, brighter (18 and 9 shades), and the glass has its own meso tile (`RIPPLED`: a melt frozen in ripples, bubble pits, hairline cracks). The vertex shade is `exposureStop(albedo, light, atmosphere)` (plus emissive and speculars), the rule the globe and the landing map use as well. Bank 4 = stars.

Textures (`surface/textures.*`): per material a periodic 128x128 tile of signed shade offsets (a texel is 0.25 m, a tile 32 m), generated from periodic value and Worley noise per planet seed: sand ripples across the wind, dust pebbles and craterlets, rock cracks and strata, basalt plates, ice facets and fractures, snow mottling with sparkle texels, grass tufts and bare patches, forest litter, quartz facets with glints. Added in the rasteriser as `grain2` (world-mapped, perspective-correct) on top of the 64x64 micro grain; both fade with distance (`grainWeight`, full within 120 m, 20% beyond 620 m). Rocks use the rock (or, on felisian worlds, litter) tile with world-mapped facet coordinates. Blob shadows (`drawBlobShadow`, `BLEND_DARKEN`) sit under rocks, trees, creatures, the capsule and the explorer; footprints are a 32-entry ring of small darkened quads left on sand, snow, dust and grass. B-311 (2026-09-27): every material has two or three looks chosen per planet and material by hash (`mesoVariant`, named by `mesoVariantName`: rock cracked / boulders / layered, sand ripples / playa / pebbly, dust pebbles / regolith / rubble, grass tufts / meadow / scrub, snow mottled / sastrugi / crusted, ice faceted / rippled / shattered, basalt plates / ropy / blocky, metal hammered / dendritic, sulphur crusty / lobes, graphite flaky / sooty, forest litter / roots, quartz faceted / drusy) with a strength of 0.8-1.3 per planet; `buildMacroTile` makes the broad tone tile (three octaves of periodic gradient noise, +-10, a texel every two metres, patches of 23-85 m) that `vertexOf` adds to every vertex's shade at 0.4 (0.25 on the far rings, and fading with the haze) so one material is not one flat tone from cell to cell; the far rings carry the meso tiles too, stretched to two cells; the rasteriser reads texels bilinearly where they span more than about two and a half pixels (02); and a cell whose corners differ in family is drawn with two banks and a noisy contour between them (`grainEdge`, 64 x 64 at four texels a cell) instead of the majority bank over the whole square (02, `RasterParams::bank2`). The terrain's shading mode is `SurfaceView::shadingMode` (0 flat cells, 1 stepped, 2 smooth, the default since B-311).

Foliage (M1-01, "greenmush", rebuilt in N2): a tree is a trunk with branches and 8-20 canopy blobs; each blob is a spray of hashed points inside an ellipsoid whose count follows the on-screen radius, points of 1 or 2 logical pixels in three stepped tones, solid diamonds when small; blobs under 1.2 px are a single dot. Bushes are sprays too. The mush melts the sprays into soft rounded canopies. Details in "Vegetation (N2)".

Water (M1-04): water quads at sea level get wind-driven wave rings (2.5 shades, stronger with wind), foam sparkle points where the shore is within 0.6 m of sea level and 90 m of the camera, and the sun glitter. `drawReflections` mirrors lod0/lod1 cells (within 20 cells) about sea level and blends them into bank-2 pixels only (`BLEND_REFLECT`, 45% reflected), sorted far to near, no depth test. Everything drops by d^2 / 2R with distance from the camera (the horizon curves, M1-07); since O1 the drop lives in `toView` (`03-coordinates-and-units.md`), so objects curve with the ground and small bodies use the exact sphere.

Sky (`drawSky`): two cloud layers (cumulus at 2600 m with lit tops and shaded bases judged by the density toward the sun; a thin veil at 6000 m), evaluated on a `4 x scale` pixel grid; 22-degree halo below -5 C with clear air; a darker twilight wedge opposite a low sun; the galactic band (`buildBandMap`: `buildGalaxyBand`'s 128 x 64 map per landing; since G-03 laid over the sky after the stars in bank 22, from the night sky's colour to a pale star-grey, where its light beats the sky pixel's, hidden by the clouds; 20 shades at full brightness, the bulge 17 on a dark night, the plane 6-8, fading with the day and the rain); stars twinkle through atmospheres; meteors at night; rows in parallel.

M0-07 additions (2026-09-26): every LOD ring hangs skirt walls 1.5 cells deep from its outer edge (KI-006); descent renders no 16 m cells above 800 m and `SurfaceView::prefetch(ms)` fills lod2, lod1, lod0 rings around the origin within a per-frame budget (KI-009); rain drops are world-space lines around the camera, depth-tested (KI-017); beyond 50 km from the origin `SurfaceView::reanchor()` re-initialises the site at the player's position, re-projects the capsule with `SurfaceSite::localAt` (the inverse azimuthal-equidistant mapping) and corrects headings by the meridian convergence (KI-007). exploration

## Site and caches (`surface/site.*`)

`SurfaceSite::init(sys, body, lat, lon)` builds the site basis (see 03), sets `R` in metres, `gravity`, `atmosphere`, `hasWater` (felisian), `seaLevel = 0`, `lavaLevel`, and three toroidal caches:

| Cache | Cell | Size | Detail passed to the planet function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lodN (O6-02) | 4 m | 256 x 256 | 4 m |
| lod0 | 16 m | 128 x 128 | 16 m |
| lod1 | 64 m | 128 x 128 | 64 m |
| lod2 | 512 m | 64 x 64 | 512 m |
| lod3 (O1) | 2048 m | 64 x 64 | 2048 m |

Rings drawn (`SurfaceView::ringR`, `computeRings`): the near ring (`ringN`, O6-02) 30 cells of 4 m (120 m), lod0 40 cells (640 m), lod1 36 (2.3 km), lod2 26 (13.3 km), lod3 26 (53 km); on a small body (`smallBody`, R under 300 km) each ring stops at 1.15 radii and a ring under four cells is dropped (a 5 km comet: 640 m, 2.3 km, 5.6 km, no far ring). `drawRadiusM()` is the outermost radius; `prefetch` fills the rings coarse to fine. The floor beyond the last ring (`drawFloor`) is a fan of 24 x 6 curved quads from 0.92 of the far radius out to 10.5 times it, at the sea level or 300 m under the lowest far cell (KI-301); small bodies have none, their terrain disc reaching past their own horizon. `SurfaceSite` also carries `escapeVelocity` and, on ringed worlds, `ringProf` (the shared ring profile, O0-01).

`TerrainCache::at(cx, cz)` computes a vertex on demand (`site.sampleAt`) and tags it with its lattice coordinates, so moving simply overwrites stale entries. `groundHeight(x, z)` interpolates lod0 with the same two-triangle split the rasteriser uses, and inside the near ring the near cache blended by `nearBlend` (below); `groundHeightCoarse` is lod0 alone.

## Player (`SurfaceView::update`)

Mouse look (0.0032 rad per pixel), arrows also turn, pitch clamped to +-85 deg. Walk 4.2 m/s, run x2.3 (Shift), swimming x0.45. Acceleration constant 10/s on the ground, 2/s in the air. Jump 4.6 m/s; gravity is the body's. Slopes with rise/run above 1.35 stop you. Swimming when the ground is more than 0.4 m below sea level (you float 1.15 m below the surface, eye at 1.65 m). Terrain is unbounded; there is no walking limit.

## Environment model (`computeEnvironment`)

* `skyBrightness = smoothstep(-0.12, 0.2, sin(alt))` with atmosphere, else 0.
* Cloud cover: map pattern at (lon + drift, lat), `drift = t * 2pi / (|rotPeriod| * 3.7) + 1`, x1.3 plus slow noise; venusian = 1.
* Rain (felisian only): `(cloud - 0.72) / 0.25` clamped.
* Wind: base 10 kt (25 thin atmosphere, 4 venusian, 0 airless) x gusts (noise over 400 s) x (1 + 1.5 rain), direction from noise over 2000 s; refreshed every 2 s of game time.
* Temperature (C): from the body's `tempK`; with atmosphere `0.75 base + offset` (venusian +420, thin -25, felisian +30, quartz +6) `+ 12 sin(alt) - 0.4 |lat|`; airless `1.3 base - 60 + (110 sin(alt) or -70 at night) - 0.5 |lat|`; minus 6.5 C per km of altitude; molten +180; floor -270.
* Pressure per type (felisian 0.7-1.3 atm, venusian 60-95, thin 0.006-0.046, quartz 0.4-0.8) scaled by `exp(-alt/8000)`.
* Fog distance from the look table (O1: felisian 12 km, ocean 9, quartz 5, thin air 14, comet 16, molten and volcanic 30, the airless worlds 45, venusian 0.7 km) x (1 - 0.7 rain) etc.; on a comet x (1 - 0.85 activity).
* Ring shadow (O0-01): `SunInfo::ringShadow` from `SurfaceSite::sun` (the sun ray from the site crossing the ring plane inside the ring: `0.85 x` the profile's density) cuts the light factor by 80% of it and the sky brightness by 45%; `env.ringShadow` feeds the HUD's `RING SHADOW`.
* Comet activity (O4): `env.cometActivity = clamp((1.6 ref / dStar)^2, 0, 1)` with the reference orbit of the tail's formula; drives the vents, the coma, the tail and the haze; the HUD prints `VENTING 40%`.
* Capsule distance/bearing; `nearCapsule` within 7 m.

## Lighting

Per vertex: normal from the four neighbours; `lambert = max(0, n . sun)`; `sunUp = smoothstep(-0.03, 0.06, sun.y)`; ambient `0.08 + 0.2 sky` with atmosphere, `0.11` airless; `light = ambient + (1 - ambient) lambert sunUp lightFactor`; `shade = 50 light^(0.65 | 0.55 airless) clamp(0.45 + 0.75 albedo, 0.5, 1.2)`; fog toward 63. Lava vertices ignore light: worley veins (11 m cells) bright, crust dark, animated with the day fraction. Quads are flat shaded (corner average).

Water quads (felisian, any corner below sea level): shade `20 + 14 sky + shallowness*9 + glint` where glint is `pow(max(0, reflect . sun), 28) * 24 * sunUp * lightFactor`, fogged, with an animated fine grain.

## Sky (`drawSky`)

See 02 for the order. Notable formulas: airless glow `pow(cos, 300) * 22`; atmosphere base `day (15 + 25 hz) + (1 - day)(2 + 5 hz)` with `hz = (1 - el)^2.2`, density from the look table (venusian 1.3, thin 0.5, quartz 1.15); sun glow `(cos^6 * 9 + cos^50 * 14) * sunUp`; clouds on a 2600 m plane with `fbm2` (4 octaves, 1800 m wavelength) thresholded at `1 - 0.75 cover`, lit by the sun direction, drifting with the wind (`windDriftX/Z`, the integral of the wind over game time at 0.5 m per knot-second, shared with the ground cloud shadows; B-206: the old `wind x 0.5 x t` jumped kilometres at every wind change); stars are hidden where the sky index exceeds 22 and scaled by `(1 - 0.9 day)(1 - rain)`.

Sun colour and sky ramps come from `setupPalette` (sunset tint when the sun is within ~12 deg of the horizon, rain dims, lightning flashes the palette).

On a comet (O4) the airless sky adds the coma, `activity x (6 + 14 cos^3)` toward the sun, and the ion tail: `pow(max(0, -cos), 18) x activity` (a cone some 15 degrees wide round the anti-solar point) drawn in bank 15 (a blue ramp set for comets) at `6 + 26 tail (0.8 + 0.3 knots)` with the five streaming knots of `SpaceRenderer::cometKnot`; by day the tail is under the horizon, at night overhead.

## Objects (`drawObjects`)

Per lod0 cell within 22 cells, hashed by cell coordinates:

* Rocks: density per type (cratered 1.1, rocky 1.6, thin 1.2, molten 0.7, icy 0.3, felisian 0.35, venusian 0.6, quartz 0.5, comet 1.4), none on sand, water or lava, a third on glass (S-03); size `scale * (0.35 + 2 u^2.5)` m; pyramid of three lit triangles, bank 3; culled below 0.8 px.
* Quartz crystals on quartz worlds: 1-3 per cell (50%), 1-4 m spikes, bank 2.

## Vegetation (N2, 2026-09-26, `surface/flora.cpp`)

One enumerator places everything: `forTrees(cx, cz, fn)` gives the trees of a 16 m cell (felisian forest/grass cells above the water, veg over 0.2) from the cell hash `hash2i(cx, cz, seed ^ 0xB0B)`; the count is `dens x per x (0.6..1.4)` with a fractional part as a probability (scattered trees on grass), `dens = canopyDensityAt(x, z, veg) = veg x (0.12 + 0.88 smoothstep(0.30, 0.55, fbm2(x/110, z/110, seed ^ 0xC1EA, 2)))` (the clearing mask: about a third of a forest is clearings with an eighth of the trees), `per` 6.5 on forest, 2.2 on grass, 3.0 on savanna (veg-based), 1.5 on tundra. Per tree: silhouette (70% family 1, 30% family 2; taiga and tundra cones, savanna umbrellas, wetland 60% fern trees, tropical 25% tiered giants), height `H0..H1 x sqrt(9.8/g) x (0.6 + 0.4 dens)` (dome 6-12 m, cone 8-16, umbrella 7-13, giant 18-28, fibrous 4-8, fern 4-7, mushroom 3-6), canopy radius 0.22-0.45 of the height, canopy centre 0.62-0.90, 3% dead (forest only), bare when deciduous (dome, umbrella, giant, mushroom) and `seasonPhaseLocal() < -0.6`, leaf bank 5 for family 2 or deciduous trees in the cold shoulder seasons (phase in [-0.6, 0)), 10 for 15% (the straw variant), else 3. `forLogs`: 12% x dens/0.5 of forest cells hold a fallen log (4-9 m, radius 0.3-0.5) that `collectColliders` turns into three circles; trunks are colliders of radius `0.12 + 0.02 h`.

Drawing (`drawFlora`): cells sorted near to far within 24 cells and a 70-degree view cone (dot over 0.45 beyond 3 cells). A coverage grid of 8x8 (1x) tiles counts drawn spray points (weighted by point area); a tree farther than 40 m whose projected canopy lies entirely on tiles at or above 64 is skipped (`lastTreesHidden`; a forest is many trees deep and the hidden ones cost the most). Tiers by the canopy radius in 1x pixels `rPx`: far (`rPx < 3.5`): a trunk line plus 2-3 blob points; mid: a trunk quad within 100 m else a line, 2-3 blobs drawn solid (a view-facing diamond) up to 9 px, a point budget `0.2 rPx^2` (8..60) split 3 ways, no branches; near (`rPx > 16` and within 80 m): the trunk quad (half-width 2% of the height, 4% giants, 12% mushroom stems; Gouraud lit by the side facing the sun; bark at stops 7-12, the near-black end, since the rock ramp's dark stop is sky-lit), 2-3 branch lines to the first blobs, the full silhouette (dome 8-14 blobs in an ellipsoid; cone 6-9 stacked and narrowing; umbrella 7-11 in a flat crown; giant three layers of 5-8; fibrous 7-11 tufts on stalk lines; fern tree 7-10 arching two-segment fronds with small sprays; mushroom 6-9 flat blobs plus twelve dark gill points), a point budget `0.35 rPx^2` (30..500) split over ten blobs. A blob (`blob`) is a spray of `0.22 rbPx^2` (`0.11` with size-2 points, used above 3.5 px) hashed points on an ellipsoid with three stepped tones (lit crown 1.0 where the direction from the blob centre faces the sun, mid 0.72, dark underside 0.46, and everything below 35% of the blob's half-height dark), shade `48 light^0.7 x tone x (0.9..1.1)` plus fog computed once per blob. Molten worlds erupt (`eruptions`, `nextEruption`): a plume of lit points with rumble, cued to the audio through `eruptionCue`.

## Sound per world (M4-09)

`Game::updateSurface` sets `AudioState::windTone` (0.25 near water: low slow surges; 0.6 on sand and dust; 1.0 on relief over 12 m across 80 m: a whistle at 14 Hz over the noise; 0.5 elsewhere), `surf` (0.6 when water lies within 30 m), `birds` (0.6 by day with flocks present, on foot), N3-05 `call`/`callPitch` (a creature call: chirp = a rising sine 0.18 s, low = a 0.7 s growl with vibrato and harmonics, hoot = two soft notes, click = four noise ticks), `hoofs` (soft thumps whose pace quickens with the level) and `insects` (a thin amplitude-modulated buzz), N2-06 `leaves`, N4-04 `engineRpm` (a tone at 9-21x the drive's base pitch within the gear), `skid` (low-passed noise) and `thud` (a one-shot 55 Hz thump), and one `step` per integer stride of `Player::stridePhase` on the ground: 1 soft (sand, snow, grass, dust, forest), 2 hard (rock and the rest), 3 splash when swimming; `stepGain` 0.8 while sprinting. `AudioSynth` renders the wind as low-pass noise whose cutoff follows the tone, surf as slow cubed surges of the same noise, bird calls as 90 ms sine sweeps at 1.8-4 kHz spaced 0.4-3 s over the level, and footsteps as 50 ms (160 ms for splashes) filtered bursts. `vesperis_test audio` renders six stages (ship, vimana, shore, desert storm, mountain rain, lava) to `shots/audio_test.wav` and fails on a non-finite or clipped sample.

## Descent and ascent

`cameraOverrideAlt` and `cameraOverridePitch` replace the eye height and pitch during transitions: descent from 1800 m at -60 deg to ground level at 0 deg over 7 s (ease-out), ascent the reverse with a fade in the last 30%.

## Two suns, rings and glowing parents in the sky (M5, 2026-09-26)

* **Second sun (M5-01).** `SurfaceSite::sun2(t, out)` fills a `SunInfo` for the companion (direction, altitude, angular radius, `starLightFactor`, its colour). `computeEnvironment` keeps the primary in `env.sunDisc`, the companion in `env.sun2`, and makes `env.sun` the light used by every shading path: the two directions blended by `lightFactor x up-ness`, the factor summed, the eclipse of the primary scaled by its share, `env.lightColor` mixed by `sun2Share`. The sky brightness takes the brighter sun, so a companion keeps the day alive after the primary sets. `drawSky` adds the companion's own glow term, draws its disc first in bank 12 (its colour), then the primary from `sunDisc` (flares and eclipse from the primary only). Blob shadows (`drawBlobShadowFrom`) are drawn once per sun when both matter (share 5-95%); terrain cast shadows follow the blended direction (KI-020).
* **Ring arcs (M5-04, brightened in O0-01, B-301).** On a ringed world, each sky pixel's world direction is intersected with the ring plane (normal `spinAxis` through the centre, the site's offset from the centre in km): a hit between `ringInner` and `ringOuter` radii takes the ring profile density (`SurfaceSite::ringProf`, from `StarSystem::ringProfileOf`) and is drawn at `36 + 24 pow(dens 1.3, 0.35) lit` on the sky bank, whose bright part starts at 36 (the old `63 dens^0.35 ...` peaked at 39, near black there): `lit = (0.55 + 0.45 |axis . sun|) x face x max(lightFactor, 0.5)`, `face` 1 when the sun is on the observer's side of the ring plane, 0.5 for the unlit face seen by transmitted light; the part in the world's own shadow is not drawn; with an atmosphere `rv (1 - 0.3 day) + v 0.45 day` washes it like the day moon. From a moon, the parent's rings come with `drawGlobe` in sky mode at `36 + 27 ...` (same reason), so they read like a moon.
* **Glowing parents (M5-02).** A substellar parent in the sky is drawn whole in bank 13 (its own red ramp, set in `setupPalette` when `env.moonBody` is substellar); `moonLight` takes `0.3 selfGlow (angR / 4 deg)^2` when that beats the reflected light, with a warm `moonColor`, so its moons have warm night skies and dim red ground light.
* **Comets and wandering stars (M5-07, M5-09).** `SpaceContext::skyDark` (1 at night, 0 by day) fades comet tails and far-body points in a bright sky; far bodies are `scale + 1` squares at 30-60 shade with phase, albedo and closeness, so they survive the mush; substellar bodies never fall below 40, comets 36 at night.

## Prefetch worker (M7-01, 2026-09-26)

`SurfaceView::prefetchAhead` runs every frame after the movement update. When a previous job has finished (an atomic flag), its samples are copied into the cache slots that are still empty; then, if the explorer or the buggy moves faster than 1 m/s, the uncached cells between 24 and 46 cells ahead within 55 degrees of the velocity (the near ring every other job with up to 640 cells 22-50 cells ahead, since it sweeps fastest, then lod0 and lod1 in turn with 320; O6-02) are handed to a new worker thread that calls `SurfaceSite::sampleAt` (pure, thread-safe) into a staging vector. `init` and `reanchor` join and discard any pending job, the destructor joins. The result is bit-identical to on-demand sampling, so determinism is untouched; the benchmark's sprint worst frame is 1.6 ms.

## The near ring (O6-02, 2026-09-27)

Within 120 m the ground is drawn at 4 m cells (`SurfaceSite::lodN`, a 256 x 256 torus one kilometre across; `SurfaceView::ringN` 30 cells, `vcN`), the last stage of `prefetch` after lod0 and the first customer of the ahead worker. It shows the two finest octaves of the relief spectrum (64 and 32 m wavelengths, 1-4 m of relief in the mountains) that 16 m cells cannot carry, and gives the near ground the fine facets the original's polymap had; lod0 skips its inner six cells under it. The ring fades with speed (`updateNearRing`, called before the movement update): on foot and in a buggy under 12 m/s `nearBlend` is 1, above 20 m/s it is 0 with a quarter-second time constant between (a buggy at 20 m/s would sweep 750 new cells a frame). While it fades, the near vertices are drawn at `lod0 + (near - lod0) nearBlend` (`vertexOf`) and `groundHeight` blends the same way inside the ring (`SurfaceSite::nearBlend`, `nearCx/Cz`, `nearR`, set by the view each frame), so the feet and the wheels always sit on the ground that is drawn and nothing jolts when the relief melts into the 16 m cells; during the descent it is drawn only under 60 m. Foam sparkles come from one near cell in sixteen so the shore keeps its density; cast shadows, meso tiles and the grain are the lod0 rules. On a body under 16 m radius the ring is dropped. Cost: surface 2x 4.3 ms (3.9 before), the sprint's worst frame 2.7 ms.

B-313 (2026-09-28): the speed fade is gone; the near ring is drawn at every speed (`nearBlend` is 1 whenever the ring is drawn, 0 when the capsule is high) and the buggy's suspension (above) takes its bumps; braking to get out of the buggy, the 4 m relief used to rise over half a second in front of you. `SurfaceSite::nearX/nearZ/nearR/nearBand` and `nearMorph(x, z)` carry the ring's centre and morph band, so `groundHeight` returns the ground as drawn: the near height in the core, sliding onto lod0 across the band.

## Geomorphing (B-313, 2026-09-28)

Each ring samples the planet function at its own detail (`lodFade` in `sampleSurface`), so a coarser ring is a smoother terrain, and at the pinned mountain site the vertices of a ring's edge differed from the coarser ring's surface by 3 m (the near ring at 120 m), 17 m (lod0 at 640 m), 84 m (lod1 at 2.3 km) and 1015 m (lod2 at 13 km): as an edge swept over the ground with the camera, every cell crossing it jumped to the other ring's height (KI-301's "a ridge shifts by a cell", the user's "hills morphing"). `vertexOf` now morphs: within a ring's band a vertex takes the coarser ring's drawn surface at its spot (`coarseAt`: the height of the coarser cell's triangle with the same diagonal split, water vertices half a metre under their water as drawn) and, when the coarser vertices were computed this frame, their Gouraud shade, in proportion to `smoothstep(radius - band, radius - 0.5, distance from the camera in cells)`, so the two surfaces coincide at the edge and the finer relief rises as the edge approaches instead of popping. The band reaches from the finer ring's cover plus two cells to the edge (`render`: 20 cells of the near ring beyond a 40 m core, 14 of lod0, 24 of lod1, 20 of lod2): a six-cell band turned the far edge's gap of hundreds of metres into twisted cells and a sawtooth skyline. The rings are warmed coarse to fine (`warmTerrainLOD` takes the coarser ring; its vertices are warmed three cells into its skip disc, since the finer ring's edge lies there), a coarser ring skips the cells the finer one covers up to a cell of overlap (`skipOf`: 8, 10, 5, 7 cells; it used to overlap by two or three, 4 km of both rings drawn at the far edge) and draws with `zbias` 0.999 so the finer ring wins where the two coincide. `SurfaceView::testRingSeams` (tests) inverts the view transform of the edge vertices and reports the largest gap to the coarser surface per edge, and what it would have been unmorphed: `unit` requires 0.

B-404 (2026-10-01): the materials are the same in every ring. The planet function decides them on scale-free terms (`matVisible`, the material chain of the relief spectrum: `05-planet-function.md`), so a patch of ground never ends at a ring's edge in front of the explorer (the europan stain did, at the near ring's 120 m, and the lava tongues, the cliffs' rock and every landform's walls at one ring or another); what still differs between the rings is KI-341, and `vesperis_test matlod` measures it.

## The rings' cover, the water's drawing, the life sensor and the clutter (O10, 2026-09-28)

* **Cover** (B-318): `SurfaceView::cellCovered(cs, cx, cz, coverM)` skips a coarse cell only when its farthest corner from the camera lies within `coverM`, the finer ring's guaranteed cover (`coverOf(rf, csf) = (rf - 0.71) csf` metres: a ring of rf cells draws the cells whose centre is within rf, so every point within rf - 0.71 cells lies in one of them). The warm pass warms three cells further in (`coverM - 3 cs`). The old scalar skip on cell centres left the skipped cells' outer corners bare (sawtooth holes at every edge, `vesperis_test capsule`).
* **Objects on the drawn ground** (B-319): `SurfaceSite::groundHeight` blends lod0's height onto lod1's across lod0's outer morph band (`lod0R`, `lod0Band`, `camX/camZ`, set by `render` with the ring table), as it blends the near ring's; a far canopy's cutout fades out under 8 logical pixels of radius and its tile under 6.
* **Water** (B-320): `VtxCache::wy` is the vertex's water level as drawn (`vertexOf` morphs it onto the coarser ring's `coarseWaterAt`, bilinear over the wet corners, with the height); `drawTerrainLOD` builds the water quad from the four corners' `wy` (a dry corner takes the mean of the wet ones), wet when any corner has water and the lowest ground lies under the highest water plus half a metre, the foam on corners within 0.6 m of their own level. The shade: `body = 24 + 8 (1 - depth/8)` from above, `55` (the ramp's horizon stop) at grazing angles through a Fresnel term `0.04 + 0.96 (1 - cos)^4`, plus the sun's glint and the wind's waves; the felisian and ocean water ramp is scaled by `0.25 + 0.75 day`. `drawReflections` mirrors a cell round the sea, and round its own water level when its vertices carry a lake or river level over the sea (`Cell::level`). `SurfaceSite::waterAt` is bilinear over the wet corners.
* **Life sensor** (B-321): the HUD (on foot, not in the buggy's seat) prints `LIFE <distance> <bearing>` for the nearest herd or flock within 1.5 km (`LIFE HERE` under 25 m, `(FLYERS)` for a flock), right-aligned above the capsule line. The habitat chances: grassland and savanna 0.72, temperate, tropical and wetland 0.52, taiga 0.4, desert and tundra 0.26, alpine 0.14.
* **Clutter** (R-306, `drawFloraBand`, `drawObjects`): bushes `(0.5 + 3.5 veg) per (0.6..1.6)` a cell on grass to 320 m (savanna 1.6, tundra 0.7 and small, desert 0.35 on sand, thin forest 0.6), banks 3/5/10, the big ones with a second cluster within 120 m; mounds (1-3 tapered cones 0.8-2.5 m) on one warm open cell in six to 260 m; tufts to 120 m (3 + 4 veg beyond 60 m); boulders at density 0.7 on open felisian ground and an outcrop of three on one cell in sixteen. Trait hooks: `treeScale x 1.9` (giant flora), 60% dead trees (dead forests), `leafBase` held at 40 x the dark and bank 3 re-ramped toward the flora colour at night (luminous flora), `fogDistance x 0.3` (haze).

## The water's edge, the eye at the surface, the new types' events (O11, 2026-09-28)

* **The shore cut** (B-322): a cell whose corners lie on both sides of a water's edge is drawn as pieces cut along the edge (`shoreSplitTriangle` in `site.cpp`: each of the cell's two triangles clipped by the sign of the corners' shore value, the crossing points at the water level less 3 cm, the wet and dry polygons in fan order), the edge being the contour of the shore distance across the cell (`vertexShore`: the sample's `shore` where a river or a lake set it, else its height over its water, else its height over its neighbours' plane), not the place where the two triangles cross the plane. It used to be the latter: a bank drops metres within a cell, so the crossing hugged the bank vertices, a stair of cells from the air (KI-321) and a sawtooth of triangles when walking. `cacheHeightAt` reads the same pieces (`groundHeight`, the objects, the morph target), `VtxCache::hy` and `sh` carry the drawn height and shore, both morphed onto the coarser ring (`coarseShoreAt`, `coarseWaterAt`: linear over the coarse cell's triangle, a corner without water taking the mean, exactly as the cut reads them, so a morphed vertex lands on the same side of the same edge; `unit` keeps the seams at 0.0000 m), and a drawn vertex is clamped to its side (the margins 0.3 m wet / 0.02 dry, closing to the cut's 3 cm lip at the edge). The water sheet is drawn where a corner is wet by its edge (it was drawn wherever a corner lay within half a metre of the level: a moat of cells beyond a river's plain); its depth shade reads the drawn ground; it carries its ring's `zbias` (a coarse ring pushed its ground back 0.1% and not its water, so from a kilometre a metre-high shore lost to the plane in every wet cell). The reflection pass mirrors only the part of a cell above the water (the dry pieces, then clipped by the level), so from a low eye the mirrored shore cells no longer pass under the camera as slabs and stand in the water as bars. The inner rings hang no skirts (their edges lie on the coarser surface since B-313; a skirt peeked through as a bright line). KI-323/324 note the limits: a lake the coarser ring cannot see fades across the band, the 512 m ring's outlines are 512 m polygons.
* **The eye at the water** (B-322): the Fresnel term's grazing angle is clamped at cos 0.04 (the water stays water from an eye at the surface); `viewUnderwater` (`render`) tints the palette and closes the fog to 22 m whenever the eye is under the drawn water surface, whatever the body does (a dive's first frames, the free camera); crouching afloat keeps the eye at 1.65 m. The capsule (and its beacon beam) is not drawn while the camera flies with it (`cameraOverrideAlt >= 0`): from the air the beam was a bright line across the ground.
* **Geysers and fountains** (R-307, `drawGeysers`, `drawJet`, `jetStrength`; `drawWeather` calls the geysers on europan and tectonic worlds and wherever `TR_GEYSERS` is a trait). A jet is a dense core column of eight camera-facing segments, each two quads round the axis (the vertex weight 1.15 at the centre and 0.2 at the sides, so the boundary tile's cutout cuts the sides ragged; the fine grain scrolls up it at the jet's ejection speed `sqrt(2 g H)`; the throat narrow, the top twice as wide and fraying as the centre weight falls), a crown of 130-340 spray points thrown out where the column breaks and falling from it for three seconds, a veil of 110-300 drops on full ballistic arcs from the throat, and a bright pool; steam and ice take bank 8 at `38 + 22 sunUp`, lava bank 2 at 61. Its height and width are the vent's class times its strength this frame: `jetStrength` returns a steady vent's `0.82 + 0.18 sin(0.9 t)` pulse or, for a periodic one, a cycle's `smoothstep(0, 0.035)` build with a `1 + 0.35 exp(-phase/0.05)` burst (a third over full height in the first seconds) dying over the cycle's last tenth; the column's height follows `0.55 + 0.45 strength`. Three vent sets: europan cracks (hashed on 300 m cells where the 16 m ring is stained, within 2.5 km; small 160 m x 7 m, tall 400 x 15, huge 800 x 30 by hash, 35% steady, cycles 90-180 s at 25-55% duty), geyser basins (`geyserVents`: the sinter mounds' centres within 2.5 km on sinter; 30 / 80 / 180 m by 2.5 / 5 / 10 m, 30% steady, cycles 60-180 s at 20-50%; `basinJetStrength` is the one rule for the drawing and the tests), tectonic crusts and lava (hashed on 200 m cells within 1.1 km: on sulphur three vents in four steam steadily at 15-40 m and one is a periodic hot spring of 60-150 m; on lava a steady fountain of 30-90 m).
* **Lava fountains, quakes, strikes, devils** (R-307): `Eruption::kind` 1 is a tectonic world's great fountain, drawn with the same jet (from a lava cell of the 16 m ring within 600 m, every 15-40 s, 250-450 m by 14-26 m, a 2.5 s build with the burst, dying over the last 8 s of 30), kind 2 a meteorite strike on a bombarded world (within 1.2 km every 20-60 s: a white flash for a third of a second, eight seconds of dark ejecta with glowing chips, a shake); `quake` (0..1, decaying over 4 s) heaves and trembles the camera (up to 1.1 degrees of pitch, 0.8 of roll, 10 cm of eye) and swells the rumble, every 60-180 s on a tectonic world for ten seconds; `devils` are dust devils on desert worlds (and thin-air worlds in a storm): up to four within 500 m, born every 20-50 s, 30-110 m tall, radius 5-17 m, walking with the wind for 2-5 minutes, 160-420 points spiralling up. Per type the environment sets the temperature offsets and swings (desert +22 / 32 degrees, hydrocarbon -75 / 3, acidic +55 / 6, tectonic +65 / 14), the pressures (0.3-1, 1.4-1.7, 2-5, 0.08-0.38 atm), acid rain over cloud 0.6, methane drizzle by a slow noise, desert dust storms (threshold 0.45), the wind bases (18 and 3 knots). `hasOpaqueDeck` replaces every venusian branch (no sun disc, halo, pillar or wedge; cloud 1; no cast shadows; the picture blurred; the aurora off) and `isLavaWorld` every molten-or-volcanic one (bank 2's lava ramp, the ground glow, the infrared view, the eruptions, the rumble).

## Drainage on the surface, landmarks, scree, the far floor (O6-03..O6-07, 2026-09-28)

**The drainage tiles and the rings.** `SurfaceView::init` and `reanchor` prefetch the tiles within `drawRadiusM() + 3 km` synchronously before the caches fill (the range includes the blend neighbours, see `05-planet-function.md`); `update` checks every 3 s that the tiles within `drawRadiusM() + 9 km` of the player are built and asks for the missing ones on a worker. The landing map prefetches the site under the cursor (65 km) on a worker at every cursor move (a later prefetch supersedes the earlier worker's remaining tiles), so a landing a few seconds later finds its tiles in the cache; `Game::testDrainageReady` tells the bench how long that took (about a second for nine tiles on a 6400 km world). The site probe of the landing map (`siteSlope`, the material line) samples without the drainage until the site's tiles are built (`DrainageOff(bool)`): a probe that built them froze the map for half a second at every move of the cursor. The zoom (`Z`) reads the drainage from tiles already built: a window whose tiles are missing is drawn without it at once, its tiles are asked for, and it is drawn again when they are there (`zoomDry`, `zoomPrefetchT`; the request is repeated every half second while the cursor's own prefetch supersedes it). The first descent frame fills the four rings (12,000 samples, a fifth of them drainage) in about 50-80 ms, under the fade-in; `bench check` allows 80.

**The far floor and the warm pass sample a disc**, not a square: the square's corners lay 75 km out on the far ring, beyond the tiles a landing prefetches, and the first frame computed those tiles itself (270 ms). `drawFloor` reads only the far cells within the ring's radius; `warmTerrainLOD` fills cells within `radius + 2`.

**Scree** (`TerrainVertex::scree`, from `SurfaceSample::scree`): the talus of the cliff bands and the boulder fields raise the rock count of a cell (`x (1 + 5 scree/255)`) and the rock size (`x (1 + 0.6 scree/255)`) in `drawObjects` and `collectColliders`.

**Landmarks** (O6-06): `SurfaceView::findLandmarks` searches the grid cells within `drawRadiusM() + 8 km` of the site on a thread of its own at every landing (the sync prefetch has built the tiles it reads; a synchronous search cost the first frame 1.2 s on lake country); `collectLandmarks` takes the result when it is ready (`SiteLandmark`: the landmark and its local x/z), `joinLandmarks` on a new site and in the destructor; `landmarkAt(x, z)` gives the landmark whose radius (at least 60 m) covers a point, `nearestLandmark` the nearest. The rangefinder's second line names what it hits ("MOUNT KEIRA - PEAK"); the sector map marks every landmark at its zoom levels 1-3 with the kind's glyph (`^ = V O * + ~ #`) and a truncated name, white once seen, amber before; the landing zoom marks the cells' landmarks with the glyph (the ruins only once seen), names the eight most prominent (ruins count at a twentieth), and lays no name over another's or over the map's own text. `Game::noteLandmarks` (in `updateSurface`) logs the first sight of a landmark within its radius plus 2.5 km, or within a kilometre of its edge for the big ones ("LANDMARK: LAKE LYOOTRU, A LAKE 6.8 KM ACROSS AT 996 M, 3774 M AWAY ON SKEATOLTDOS III"), marks it seen in the guide (`Guide::landmarksSeen`, `landmark <key>` lines) and shows a status line; `landmarkKey` is the body key plus `/L<id>`, so `Guide::names` renames a landmark like a star or a body ("RENAME THE NEAREST LANDMARK" in the guide menu, text kind 6).

**Harness traces** (`VESPERIS_TRACE=1`): `SurfaceView::init` prints its stages, the first three renders print their sections and the warm pass's misses and fill times per ring, the descent update its time; the drainage prints every tile built (with the thread) and every prefetch and readiness check with a millisecond stamp, and the first drainage call of every thread with its detail (a caller sampling finer than 2100 m builds tiles). `VESPERIS_SKIP=cut|wq|refl` skips the shore cut, the water quads or the reflections (`08-testing-and-tools.md`).
