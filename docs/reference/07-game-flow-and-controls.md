# Game flow, controls, persistence

## States (`GameState`)

```
TITLE -> SPACE (Enter, or SURFACE when a loaded save was on the surface) ; N starts a new expedition (G-02: parked at the first living world of the pinned home star, `HOME_SX/SY/SZ` in `galaxy/starfield.h`, Heilya at sector 195 0 46; the search of sectors 176..196 x 36..56 for a yellow or orange star with a living world is the fallback when a generation change takes it away)
SPACE -> LANDING_MAP (C, parked at a landable body) -> DESCENT (Enter) -> SURFACE -> ASCENT (Q/E at the capsule) -> SPACE
SPACE/SURFACE -> HELP (H, ?, F1), MENU (Esc), DATA (I, F2); SPACE -> SYSTEM_LIST (Tab); MENU -> SETTINGS, SLOTS (save/load pickers)
SPACE -> SHARDS (C-06: the shard decoder, from the cabin's back wall, the flight computer's preferences page or the guide menu; the menu refuses it on the surface; C-04: a piece of music plays there and `N` names it through TEXT_ENTRY, which returns to the decoder; C-05: a text shard speaks there)
SPACE/SURFACE/LANDING_MAP -> ALMANAC (W-01: Ctrl+T; DATA -> ALMANAC with T; the flight computer's devices page); Esc returns where it came from, Enter starts a run to the row and returns to the scene
SPACE -> PROBE (X-01: C parked at a giant launches a probe and shows its camera; C, E at the landing screen or the cage watch a probe that falls; Esc, E or C go back to the cabin, the probe falling on; at its end Enter or Esc let it go)
GALLERY -> RECORDING (X-04: Enter on a probe's recording plays it on the ship's screen; Esc or Enter back to the gallery; on the ground the gallery says the recordings play on the ship)
```

`returnState` remembers where an overlay came from and is set explicitly by every overlay entry (B-006). Only SPACE, SURFACE, DESCENT, ASCENT and LANDING_MAP advance game time (and the ship's screens, the decoder and, since X-01, the probe's camera).

## Ship (`ShipState`)

Modes: `STANDBY` (free in a system or deep space), `VIMANA` (interstellar flight), `APPROACH` (fine approach), `PARKED` (following a body, optionally orbiting).

* Remote target: `R` toggles crosshair mode; the star nearest to the crosshair within 14 px is named live; Enter/click locks it (`lockRemoteTarget`). The current star is refused. `N` turns the ship to the next nearest star and (G-04) faces the cabin view forward as `X` does, so the lock finds it: in the cabin the view kept its own yaw and `Enter` missed the star the ship pointed at. While aiming the star field is amplified x1.5, the six nearest stars carry diamond markers with their distance (`4.2 LY`; R-407: the name only where the star has one, so no row of `UNKNOWN`s, and `VISITED` in amber where the ship has been: `aimStarLabel`), and `N` cycles the crosshair through them (`cycleTargetStar`); the star under the crosshair, the remote target's line and the radar's lock say `VISITED` too, and a key line `N NEXT STAR  ENTER LOCKS  DIGITS JUMP  R CANCELS` holds the bottom (the local target's label is left out meanwhile). **The jump** (R-407, 2026-10-07): the digits typed in the aim are a jump's light years (`jumpDigits`, 1 to 100,000; Backspace takes one back); a panel shows `JUMP 400 LY COREWARD` (`galacticHeading`: COREWARD, RIMWARD, ALONG THE DISC, NORTH or SOUTH OUT OF THE DISC), where the line's end lies (`END: SPIRAL ARM - 13104 LY FROM THE CORE`) and `ENTER JUMPS  BACKSPACE  R CANCELS`; Enter (`startJump`) makes the star nearest the end of the line along the crosshair (`aimDirection`, the view's forward) the remote target (`jumpTarget` in `galaxy/starfield.h`, `04-galaxy-and-systems.md`) and flies at once (`VIMANA JUMP OF 400 LY: A STAR 402 LY AWAY`; where the end lies in the void, `NO STARS OUT THERE - THE JUMP ENDS 580 LY SHORT`), logging `JUMP 400 LY COREWARD FROM <star> TO PARSIS ..., SPIRAL ARM`. `F` toggles the field amplificator at any time (M1-10); arrival from a Vimana flight flashes the palette (`arrivalFlash`).
* Vimana: `V` starts a flight (`flightFrom`, `flightTo`, real-time duration `vimanaSeconds`: `7 + 2 sqrt(LY)` to 100 LY, then 6 s more for every doubling since R-407: 400 LY in 39 s, 10,000 in 67, 100,000 in 87); the system is unloaded (`sys.valid = false`) during flight; arrival regenerates the target system and parks the ship in standby facing the star. `V` again aborts and reloads the nearest star's system.
* Local target: `L` picks the body under the crosshair (within 18 px of its disc) or cycles; click also picks; `Tab` opens the list (`Enter` approaches, `L` targets). `X` centres the view on it.
* Approach: `startApproach(body)` chooses a parking direction on the lit side, offset sideways and slightly above the orbital plane; `updateShipMotion` moves with smoothstep easing and turns the view.
* Parked: position = body position + `parkDir * parkDist`; `O` toggles orbiting (slow rotation of `parkDir` about the spin axis). R-405 (2026-10-06): with `Shift` held the arrows carry the parked ship round its world (`Game::orbitShip`: Left/Right east and west along its parallel, about the spin axis; Up/Down north and south along its meridian, within 85 degrees of the poles; twenty degrees a second, a lap in eighteen seconds), the ship's attitude turning with it so the world stays where it was in the window and its ground streams past, the status reading the ground under the ship (`OVER 12.3° N  134.5° E`); the lap of the orbit mode goes on meanwhile. While the eye is at the telescope the stabiliser holds the parking in the body's frame (the telescope bullet below). `parkDist` comes from `Game::parkDistanceFor`: 3.5 radii, outside the rings for ringed worlds, 12 nucleus radii for a comet (N0-01: the nucleus is 76 px across at 2x with its jets around it).
* Belts (O3, R-302): `ShipState::targetBelt` is the local target when it is a belt (`localTarget` is then -1), `parkedBelt` the belt the ship is parked in, and `rockIr/rockIa/rockIy/rockM` + `rockDist` name the rock it parks beside (`04-galaxy-and-systems.md`). `L` cycles the bodies and then the belts, the analyzer lists the belts after the bodies (selectable), `Enter` calls `startApproachBelt`: `pickBeltRock` keeps the ship's angle round the star, clamps its radius into the belt and takes the biggest rock within two cells of that anchor; the parking point is six rock radii (at least 2 km) off on the sunlit side, a little above (`beltParkPos`), and the parked ship follows it (mode name `IN THE BELT`). `X` aims at the rock from this frame's parking point (a parked ship moves a hundred kilometres per frame at x10). Arrival logs a `BELT` entry. `C` in a belt says there is no world under the ship. `localTargetLabel()` names either kind of target; a body's approach (`startApproach`) leaves the belt, both its target and its parking (B-410: the belt's parking won at the approach's end and put the ship back beside its rock), and the flight computer's next body clears the belt target. Saved as `belt <target> <parked> <ir> <ia> <iy> <m> <rockDist>` (a save with a body's and a belt's parking loads at the body, B-410).
* Signal radar (C-07, 2026-10-02, `game/radar.cpp`, `galaxy/signals.h`, `12-civilisations.md`): `B` in space (the radar set beside the decoder on the cabin's back wall, `facing` 8, or the flight computer's devices page) enters the radar camera, the ship's own instrument seen through like the vehicles' nose cameras: the cabin is left behind (the head faces forward, `drawCabin` is skipped), the mouse turns the ship (`updateCabin` is not run), the picture is the camera's (`radarCameraFeed`: a cold blue-green phosphor, scanlines, a rolling band, a vignette, static on every pixel and snow in flecks that thin with the clarity), and `B`/`Esc` leave it. It listens where the camera looks (`viewBasis()`). The far signals are scanned once per sector of the ship (`signalsNear`: the transmitter sectors within 200 ly and the loud pulsars within 25, under a millisecond, the current star's own left to the local list), the system's own every frame (`localSignals`: a comet within 1.5 AU of its sun, a giant's or a strong big world's magnetosphere, and the people's world of a transmitter system). The meter reads the sum of the beam's gains over the static (`beamGain`: a wide lobe within forty degrees, a middle within ten, a narrow within three, times the signal's strength by its distance; the static a slow wander plus a jitter of about 0.07); a hold within three degrees of the strongest for `2.5 + 3 (1 - strength)` seconds locks (`radarLock`: a `SIGNAL` log entry for a new source naming only the system (the star, its class code, the distance and age, the sector; a body's name for a signal of this system), `Guide::signals`, a beep), the lock holds while the beam stays within eight degrees (lost after 1.5 s outside, `LOCK LOST`), and `Enter` on a lock locks the star and flies (`radarAccept`: `setRemoteStar` then `toggleVimana`, which leaves the camera; a body of this system: the camera left and `startApproach`). What sent the signal is never told: it is heard, and found on arrival. A people's world within the middle lobe is heard before the lock: its slot on the air (`transmittedShard`: the fifty walked in a stride of the world's own, one a slot of 75 s of game time; the next when it ends; C-13: on a world of two peoples the slot's people is a coin of the slot's, the world built again when the air changes hands) is built as a `ShardWorld` of the radar's own (`buildShardWorld`, shared with the decoder) and put on the synth through the receiver (`AudioState::radio`) as the slot's programme (`programmeFor`: the recording spoken, whispered, chanted or looped, a numbers station, a machine of the world's, or rarely its piece slowed; a loop's and a numbers station's repeats run on the game's clock, `radarRepeatContent`); what is heard while locked goes into `Guide::heard` when the programme carried the recording, and a shard taken later that the radar caught says so. The radar is off during a Vimana flight, at an arrival (the scan is fresh there), on the decoder (which takes the synth), on the surface and at a load; the other screens pause the sweep and keep the sound. `testAimAtSignal`, `testRadarInfo` for the harness.
* Telescope (W-06, 2026-10-06, `game/telescope.cpp`): `Z` in space (the devices page too) puts the eye to the telescope: the view is the ship's own attitude magnified by a power of two, x2 to x1024 (`Game::telescopeProj`: the setting's focal length multiplied; the field `2 atan(tan(fov / 2) / power)`, 38.6 deg at x2 and 0.08 at x1024 for the 70 deg setting), the cabin left behind as in the radar camera (`cabin.yaw/pitch` zeroed, `drawCabin` skipped, the stars gathered x1.5 as the field amplificator does); `+`/`-`, the wheel and PgUp/PgDn step the power; the mouse and the arrows steer the ship at the rate divided by the power. The reticle names what it rests on (`telescopeFindTarget`, every frame from the attitude: a body or the sun within 18 px of its disc, else a star of the neighbourhood within 14 px, the local target's and the aim's own picks, so the pick shrinks in angle as the power grows): `<name>  <type>  <range>  <width>` (the explorer's name or UNKNOWN, `ROCKY MOON`, the range to the surface, the apparent width in degrees, minutes or seconds of arc; the sun by its class, a star with its light years). Enter tracks the body under the reticle (`telescopeAim`: the ship re-aimed at it every frame, the mouse moving the view's offset from its centre) and lets go again; from an orbiting parking everything but the parked body sweeps the field in a second, so the telescope tracks what it is pointed at from the moment it opens (`TRACKING <name>`). `N` names the target through the guide's text entry (`NAME THIS WORLD` / `NAME THIS MOON` / `NAME THE COMET` / `NAME THE SECOND SUN`, kind 2, the status and the log naming the kind; `NAME THIS STAR`, kind 1; a star of the neighbourhood `NAME THE STAR`, kind 8 through `textStar`); `P` photographs it through the usual screenshot path with `THROUGH THE TELESCOPE X64: <the reticle's line> - IN ORBIT OF ...` as the caption; `Z` or Esc stow it. It is stowed on a load (not saved), when a Vimana flight starts, off the window (the surface, the landing map, the decoder, the sector map) and when the radar comes on (one instrument at the window at a time; the telescope switches the radar off likewise). The telescope's target (tracked, else under the reticle) takes palette bank 2 whatever its size (`choosePaletteBodies`), so a planet across the system is drawn in its colours; Enter does not start an approach while the telescope is out; the local target's bracket is not drawn in the eyepiece (the reticle names things), its line stays; the flight computer's devices page shows `X64` or `STOWED` beside `THE TELESCOPE`. **The plate** (the user's review of 2026-10-06, "a poor zoom in and that's it": `DetailPlate` in `space/space_view.h`, `02-rendering.md`): the eyepiece's body (tracked, else under the reticle: `telescopePlateBody`) is drawn from the planet function sampled at the eyepiece's own footprint, not from the world map's 512 texels: a body-fixed patch round the aim point, built in blocks of 8, 4, 2 and 1 texels within 8000 samples a frame (`TELE_PLATE_BUDGET`: a few milliseconds on the workers; coarse at once, whole within a second or two), read by the globe wherever a cell is filled with the map standing in elsewhere, the previous plate kept as the fallback while a zoom step or a drift begins a new one; the rivers' and lakes' tiles are fetched in the background for the steps under 2 km and the plate begun again when they are in. The frame says `EXPOSING 46%` while it builds; `P` reads it out whole before the photo (`telePlateFinish`, a short stall at most). **The stabiliser** (R-405, the user's review of 2026-10-06, "planet still moves and it creates shaking ... it should feel like it's not moving at all": `tele.frameBody`, `parkBF`, `viewBF`, `Game::telescopeHoldFrame`/`telescopeHoldView`/`telescopeStabilised`): while the eye is at the eyepiece a ship parked at a body holds its parking direction and the view's axis in the body's frame (both taken when the telescope opens, or when the ship arrives at a parking with it out; the view's axis taken again after every input), so the lap of the orbit mode is held off and the world's turn carries the ship with it: at any power the ground under the reticle stays where it is until the mouse or the arrows move it, and the plate is never begun again by a drift. Opening the telescope keeps the attitude (the side faced is the side held: `STABILISED ON <name>` on the frame and the status), Enter on the parked body brings its centre under the reticle and holds again; another body or the sun is tracked on its centre as before (`TRACKING <name>`: a moon moves by its own orbit, slowly); off a parking (standby, the approach) nothing is held. Stowing lets the lap go on from where the ship is. (It replaced the rework's ground hold of a deep power the same day.) The telescope's own set stands on the cabin's back wall (`facing` 9, x 1.0..2.1, the radar's twin; `E` looks through it, `TELESCOPE - E STOWS IT` while it is out) and the fuel orb moved to the front-left corner.
* The probe (X-01, 2026-10-07, `game/probe.cpp`, `galaxy/probe.h`, `space/probe_view.h`): parked at a gas giant or a brown dwarf, `C` (the landing screen on the right wall and the capsule cage, `PROBE SCREEN - E TO LAUNCH A PROBE` / `PROBE BAY - ...` there; the flight computer's `DEPLOY THE CAPSULE OR A PROBE`) sends a probe to the ground under the telescope's reticle when the telescope is out (`C SENDS A PROBE TO 5.1° N 97.8° W` on its frame), else to the ground under the ship (`PROBE AWAY, UNDER THE SHIP (THE TELESCOPE AIMS IT)`), and the screen becomes its camera (`GameState::PROBE`): the fall from the ship (36 s, the giant growing, the camera on the aim point and never steeper than a little under the limb), the entry (14 s of plasma, the speed and the g falling), then the descent through the stages of `galaxy/probe.h` (above the clouds, the upper haze, then the giant's one to three decks named by their clouds with the clear bands between them, the deep) to the end, the hull's limit (18-32 bar, per probe) or the heat (750 K), seven or eight minutes falling free. The relay's clock is real time: the warp turns the sky (the sun crosses the deck) and not the fall; `Shift` held on the probe's screen runs it eight times as fast (R-406, `FAST` in amber after the stage's name; let go, or away from the screen, it runs at one again). The mouse and the arrows turn the camera from its default (toward the aim in the fall, then a little aside of the sun's azimuth, down at the deck from high over it (22 degrees), less steeply in the haze, level in the decks and between them), `X` centres it; `Space` opens the chute once after the entry (the pressure grows four and a half times under it at a quarter of the pace: a layer to linger in, about four minutes; `Space` again cuts it), `P` photographs (`THE PROBE IN <giant>: THE UPPER HAZE, 0.32 ATM, ALT 53.4 KM - ...`), `Ctrl+R` records the feed, `Ctrl+S` saves; `Esc`, `E` or `C` go back to the cabin with the probe falling on (`PROBE: THE UPPER HAZE  0.32 ATM - C WATCHES` on the space HUD, the landing screen breathing, the relay murmuring), `C` or `E` at the landing screen or the cage come back to it. At the end the last frame is held under the loss-of-signal hiss (`SIGNAL LOST`, the depth, the pressure, the temperature, the minutes since the launch, `THE HULL GAVE WAY` or `THE HEAT TOOK IT`); away from the screen the status says `PROBE LOST - ITS LAST FRAME IS ON THE SCREEN (C)` and the frame waits until it is seen; `Enter` or `Esc` let it go. The ship holds its parking meanwhile: leaving it (an approach, a Vimana flight, another star) cuts the relay (`THE PROBE'S RELAY IS CUT: THE SHIP LEFT ITS PARKING`). Unlimited probes (the user's answer at X-01's start), one falling at a time (`C` at the giant watches the falling one). Log: `PROBE` at the launch (`INTO <giant> AT <lat lon>`), at the end (`LOST IN <giant>: 285 KM DOWN, 23.6 ATM, +477 C, 11 MIN - THE HEAT TOOK IT`) and at a cut (`RELAY TO THE PROBE IN <giant> CUT AT 0.32 ATM: THE SHIP LEFT ITS PARKING`), each within the log screen's two wrapped lines of 44 with a name of twenty. X-02 renders the stages (`space/probe_view.h`, 02-rendering): over the clouds the ammonia deck to its curved horizon under a dark sky with the thin bright limb, the stars, the sun, the moons and the rings' arc over it, its towers casting their shadows, the terminator crossing it at dusk; the upper haze's amber with the cirrus racing along the jet in the band's colour and the towers rising round the probe; inside the ammonia deck the fog with the light coming down through it, its caverns and layers; between the decks the ceiling's pouches over the water deck's towers, the lightning lighting a tower from within (now and then a channel up through the clear air) and the whole band for an instant; the water deck's dark with the rain and the lamp's cone; the deep's motes in the lamp and its dull red heat near the end. X-03 gives each giant its own (`galaxy/probe.h`'s character, 04 and 10-decisions): its decks by condensation, named on the screen by their clouds (`THE METHANE DECK`, `THE SULPHIDE DECK`, `THE AMMONIA DECK`, `THE HYDROSULPHIDE DECK`, `THE WATER DECK`, `THE SALT DECK`), the clear band coming twice under three decks; its storms, lightning (in the convective decks; an ice giant's fall the quieter, its wind's sound softer too), the deep's glow (a young giant's dull red, a brown dwarf's clouds lit from under them, on the night side too), the polar aurora's curtains over the clouds, a great storm's wall, a clear-air hole, diamond hail in the lamp, and on one rare giant something seen once in the deep that the screen never names. The camera's default turns to what was aimed at: a great storm's wall when the aim is within 2,500 km of its rim, else the aurora's curtains within 2,800 km of the oval; falling into a clear-air hole it looks down into it and further down the shaft. The probe's HUD names the layer its picture shows (inside a great storm's dome the deck's name though the pressure says the haze; `A CLEAR-AIR HOLE` in one); the cabin's line and the photograph's caption name the pressure's. X-04 keeps the record. The screen's last line: `MOUSE LOOKS  SHIFT FAST  P PHOTO  ESC CABIN` (`X` still centres).
* What a probe sends back (X-04, 2026-10-07, `game/probe.cpp`, `game/guide.*`, `galaxy/probe.*`). Every probe has its number (`Guide::probesSent`, `PROBE 3` in its lines). At its end, or when the relay is cut, its record goes into the guide (`ProbeRecord` in `Guide::probes`): the launch's numbers (the giant's key, the aim, the seed, the ship's place, the headings, the chute's clocks, the hull's limit), the explorer's turns of its camera and the game's clock along the fall as keys (a turn of half a degree, a drift of half a second off the pace), the end (its clock, pressure, depth, temperature, cause: 1 the hull, 2 the heat, 3 the relay cut), the storm it fell into, and the profile it measured: a sample every quarter of an e-fold of the pressure from the clouds' top (the clock, the pressure, the temperature, the wind as the screen read it, the light: the sun's at the depth by its height over the aim's horizon and the deep's glow, the layer found there, the flashes since the sample over it) and the layers it found with their bounds (`giantFoundLayer` on `GiantDecks::layersAt`, the view's own: a deck by its cloud, a clear band, the deep, `A CLEAR-AIR HOLE`). Its final image is drawn then (the frame the screen holds) and written as `shots/probe_<n>.png` with a `.txt` caption. A probe ended again after a load replaces its own record (the seed). The log keeps what it crossed, once each with the depth: `PROBE 3 FELL INTO THE GREAT STORM OF <giant> AT 0.21 ATM, 35 KM UP` (or `A STORM`, or the storm's name), `SAW THE WALL OF THE GREAT STORM ..., 799 KM OFF`, `SAW THE AURORA'S CURTAINS OVER ..., 1200 KM OFF`, `FELL THROUGH A CLEAR-AIR HOLE IN <giant>'S AMMONIA DECK, ...`, `MET DIAMOND HAIL IN ... AT 9.86 ATM, 182 KM DOWN`; the rare sight is never logged. `N` on the probe's screen names the storm it falls into once it is in the clouds (text entry kind 9: the great storm's key `<body>/TG`, a storm cell's `<body>/T<id>` from the globe's own cell, `giantStormCell`, at the aim and the bands' time); the name shows under the giant's at the screen's right (`THE GREAT STORM` / `A STORM` unnamed) and the keys' line reads `N NAMES THE STORM  SHIFT FAST  P PHOTO  ESC CABIN`. The gallery holds the recordings beside the photographs in the order they came (a photograph by its file's time, a recording by its end's), framed amber (a lent one cyan, its last frame drawn when it is shown); `ENTER PLAYS`. The recording (`GameState::RECORDING`, not simulating: the game's clock and a falling probe wait) is the descent drawn again from the record by the probe's own functions (its probe, and another star's system and sky, swapped in for the call): `PROBE 3  T+04:31` with `PLAYING` or `PAUSED`, the stage and the readouts as the live screen's, the storm, and a depth bar on the right (the fall and the entry over its first tenth, then the pressure's logarithm, the layers found in their colours, the playhead); Space pauses (at the end plays again), Left/Right go up and down it (a press a fiftieth, held a quarter of it a second, Shift four times as far), Up/Down and Page Up/Down to the layers' tops, Home/End, Shift held plays it eight times as fast, the mouse looks round the recording's camera (`X` back to it), `N` names the storm, Esc or Enter back to the gallery; at its end the last frame under the hiss, `SIGNAL LOST` (or `RELAY CUT`). The giant's data sheet gains a page per probe (Right/Left, `T THE ALMANAC  RIGHT PROFILES  ANY OTHER KEY CLOSES`): `PROBE 3 INTO <giant>`, the aim or the storm and the date, the end; the pressure down the page from 0.001 atm to the deepest end of the giant's probes, the layers found (a strip in their colours, named where there is room), the temperature, the wind (east right of the dotted zero), the light (its logarithm, `DARK` to `SUN`) and the lightning's rate (ticks right of it); the giant's other probes dim under it on the same scales. The statistics: `PROBES SENT 3   DEEPEST DESCENT 412 KM` under the creatures' line.

## Landing map (`LANDING_MAP`)

Shows the body's `PlanetMap` through `buildLandingMapBase` (N1-04: `materialRampColor(ramp[material], exposureStop(albedo, light factor, atmosphere) x hillshade)` with the body's `matColor`, the star tint and the body's noon sky light, so the map is the ground seen from above at noon; north-west hillshade normalised by the map's RMS gradient; lava emissive; clouds at 55% on felisian/thin-atmosphere/quartz worlds, an opaque deck in the body's cloud colour on venusian worlds) times the day/night term from the current sub-solar direction and, since O0-04 (B-306), the shadows on the day side: the moons and the parent between a texel and the star and the world's own rings (`Game::buildShadowMap`, the globe's own rule, 90% dark inside the umbra; rebuilt for another body, every 20 s of game time and at most twice a second of real time; the zoom window gets the same), the cursor (arrows, Shift for 6 deg steps), `R` for a random lit land site, `C` toggles the cloud overlay (W-03: the fronts' bands with it, from the globe's own overlay `SpaceRenderer::frontsFor`, on the map and in the zoom window: a band is whitened at 60% of its cloud like the pattern's), `Enter` to land, `Esc` back. The readout (five lines at 8 px pitch since N1): name, coordinates and sector; `MATERIAL +height RELIEF` with a `GROUND` swatch showing the noon ground colour at the cursor from the same ramp (N1-04) and the relief class from `Game::siteSlope` (the mean grade over 150 m and 600 m around the probe scaled to a gradient, O6-02; the steepest rise before; `reliefClass`: FLAT under 0.05, HILLS under 0.2, else MOUNTAINS, N1-06); biome and season (M9-05/09) with the sun's altitude, azimuth and day/twilight/night; local time, day length, gravity, atmosphere; `SKY: ...`. The data sheet adds a generated description of the body and the star (M9-14) and, on the surface, the biome and season here; the analyzer lists asteroid belts after the bodies; the space HUD names the galaxy region. The readout uses a cached `SurfaceSite` probe at the cursor: exact terrain material and elevation at 64 m detail, sun altitude/azimuth, day/twilight/night, local time, day length (or "tidally locked"), gravity, atmosphere. Sector numbers follow the original's 360 x 120 convention.

`J` (M5-09) moves the cursor under the biggest body in this world's sky (the sub-body point, where it stands at the zenith); the fifth readout line says what hangs over the cursor now (`SKY: name, altitude, width, phase, +N PTS`); on a ringed world it starts with the ring's own arc (`RINGS 62 deg UP`, `RINGS EDGE-ON OVERHEAD` within 1.5 deg of the equator, `RINGS BELOW THE HORIZON`) and a ringed sky body reads `RINGED` in place of its phase (O0-01).

Sector zoom (O2, R-301): `Z` zooms into the cursor's sector (`Game::buildLandingZoom`): a window one sector (one degree of latitude) tall and two sectors' worth of ground wide (`zoomHalfLon = zoomHalfLat x 2 / cos(lat)`, the frame being 2:1), 256 x 128 texels sampled from the planet function at `clamp(0.6 texel, 64, 512)` m of detail on all threads (`parallelFor`), coloured like the whole-world map (`mapRampsFor`, `mapColor`: the body's material ramps under the star's light, `exposureStop`, a north-west hillshade normalised over the window and capped at a 3% grade) and lit by the day side per frame; the one-degree grid is drawn over it with the sectors' names at their centres, a scale bar (50, 20, 5 or 1 km) sits at the bottom right, the cursor steps a hundredth of a degree (five with Shift) and the window re-centres when the cursor passes 75% of its half-size; `Z` again returns to the world; worlds under 100 km of radius have no zoom. The first readout line prints the sector as `SECTOR LON:LAT` (`sectorName`, `03-coordinates-and-units.md`); the title says `SECTOR 319:062 - PICK THE SITE` while zoomed. The landing map also prints a comet's gravity in four decimals. C-09: on a world that had a civilisation the window's old roads are drawn over it as hairlines in the ground's own colour under the texel's light (`Game::zoomRoads`: the villages' and the towns' roads of the network of `galaxy/roads.*` that the ground carries, the lanes between hamlets left out, walked for the window on the worker pool at the build), whether or not any of it has been walked: the roads are terrain, as the rivers the zoom draws are, and the one thing on the map that points at ruins not yet found.

## The almanac (W-01, 2026-10-06)

`Ctrl+T` in space, on the surface and on the landing map (also `T` on the data sheet, whose footer says so, and `THE ALMANAC` on the flight computer's devices page) opens the almanac (`ALMANAC`, `game/almanac.cpp`): the next events at the place with their countdowns and a free row, drawn over the scene it was opened from (the landing map keeps its picture). The place is the explorer's feet on the surface, the cursor's site on the landing map (a sunrise at the site chosen can be waited for before landing) and the ship's parking in space (the lap of the orbit mode followed). The events come from the finders of `galaxy/almanac.*` (`almanacOf`; a registry W-03's fronts and W-04's showers add to with `addAlmanacFinder`), every one a zero crossing or an extremum of the geometry the sky draws, scanned at a step small against the shortest period it moves with and refined by bisection or a golden section: on the ground `SUNRISE`/`SUNSET` (the upper limb at the horizon: the centre at minus its angular radius), `NOON` (the transit, the hour angle through zero), `FIRST LIGHT`/`END OF TWILIGHT` (the sine of the sun's altitude through -0.12, where the sky model's glow begins and ends; worlds with an atmosphere only), for every body with a disc of a quarter degree or more in the sky and for the companion sun `<NAME> RISES`/`SETS` (`SECOND SUN RISES`), `<NAME> FULL`/`NEW` (the lit share's extrema, read from the world's centre), `<NAME> OVER THE SUN 85%` (the deepest of the next crossing, M1-08's cover from the place itself), `INTO`/`OUT OF THE RING SHADOW` (O0-01's rule); from the ship `<MOON> ACROSS`/`BEHIND <BODY>` (first contact with the parked body's disc, in front of it or behind), `SHADOW OF <MOON> ON <BODY>` and `<MOON> ECLIPSED BY <BODY>` (the landing map's shadow rule: the centre against the line from the star), `<A> AND <B> 0.4° APART` (two planets within two degrees at their closest), `<NAME> ACROSS THE SUN` (first contact); from both `<COMET> AT PERIAPSIS`/`APOAPSIS`; W-03 (on the ground and at the landing map's cursor) `A FRONT FROM THE W` / `A DUST FRONT FROM THE SW` (the front's line reaches the place: the rain or the dust begins, the bearing it comes from) and `THE FRONT CLEARS` (its rain ends), every front within the window. Names are the explorer's (UNKNOWN otherwise). The window is five days on the ground and thirty from the ship, the first event of each kind per body, sorted by time; the header reads the state (`DAY  DARK IN 2 H 05 MIN`, `NIGHT  LIGHT IN 3 H 10 MIN`, `THE SUN STANDS STILL HERE` on a locked world, `12 EVENTS WITHIN 30 DAYS, FROM THE PARKING`). `Enter` runs the clock to the row: the warp is `(remaining / 3 s)^1.5` clamped to x1..x10000 and set again every frame (`almanacWarpFor`), so the last minute of game time takes about five seconds of ours and the last three seconds run at x1; the last frame's step is cut to the remainder and the clock lands on the event (`SUNRISE - X1` on the status line, a beep). `T` stops a run (`RUN STOPPED - X1`), and the HUD's warp corner reads `TO SUNRISE  2 H 05 MIN  X600` meanwhile. The free row (`RUN FOR 1 H`, Left/Right from a minute to five days) runs for a span, as M5-06's time-lapse did. Under the real-time clock setting the list is shown but nothing can be run to (the footer says so). A run in progress is not saved (the save's `warp` line reads 1), and a load or a new expedition ends it. Harness: `vesperis_test almanac` (`08-testing-and-tools.md`), `testAlmanacInfo`, `testAlmanacSelect`, `testAlmanacRunning`, `testAlmanacTarget`, `testTimeWarp`.

## The cabin (M2)

With the SHIP VIEW setting on CABIN (default) the space state puts you inside the Stardrifter (`Cabin`, `game/cabin.cpp`): a 5 x 5 x 2.4 m room in ship-local metres (+z forward) drawn over the space view with depth, so the outside shows through the window band (0.95-2.05 m) of every wall and the round roof window. Mouse looks (`cabin.yaw/pitch`), `W A S D` walk (walls, the capsule cage of radius 1 m, the GOES console on the left wall the front desk block and, since C-06, the decoder on the back wall), the arrow keys turn the ship itself; `viewBasis()` = head look x ship attitude is used for the space camera, the crosshair and the HUD markers. `E` uses what you face within reach (`cabin.facing`): the GOES console (left wall), the flight computer (front desk), the three right-wall screens (star map, landing map = deploy the capsule, target data), the capsule cage (deploy) and, since C-06, the shard decoder on the back wall (`facing` 7: a desk like the console's whose screen glows and breathes while a shard waits to be read) and, since C-07, the signal radar beside it on the same wall (`facing` 8, x -2.1..-1.0: a narrower set whose screen brightens and pulses while the receiver is on; `E` enters the radar camera) and, since W-06's rework, the telescope's set at the wall's other end (`facing` 9, x 1.0..2.1, its screen glowing while the eye is at the eyepiece; `E` looks through it). R-404 (2026-10-06): every computer's screen glows in a colour of its own (`SCREENS` in `cabin.cpp`: the console amber, the flight computer green, the star map blue, the landing map pink, the target data cyan, the decoder violet, the radar red, the telescope ice-white; banks 9 and 21-27) and the `E` prompt is written in it, so the one wanted is found from across the room. `U` toggles the cabin light (the hull ramp follows `lightLevel`), `Y` depolarises the hull (walls drawn as tinted glass), `PgUp`/`PgDn` ride the lifter to the observation deck on the roof. Banks 8 (hull), 9 (screens), 10 (light, the fuel orb and the capsule) are set by `cabinPalette`. The cabin state is saved (`cabin` line).

The flight computer (`SHIPSCREEN`) has three pages: Flight control (targeting, Vimana, local target, analyzer, approach, centre, orbit, deploy, home, previous; R-407: `VIMANA JUMP: LIGHT YEARS ALONG THE AIM` last, which opens the aim along the nose for the digits), Onboard devices (light, hull, lifter, field amplificator, time warp, scanlines, the signal radar: C-07, the almanac: W-01, `RUNNING` beside it during a run, the telescope: W-06, `X64` or `STOWED` beside it) and Preferences (settings, guide, log, the shards, save); Left/Right change the page, Up/Down and Enter act, `E`/Esc leave. Every action shares code with the key commands (`toggleVimana`, `deployCapsule`, `startApproach`, ...).

The GOES console (`CONSOLE`): a text terminal with a 200-line scrollback and typed commands: `SL [LY]` lists stars within a range (name, class, distance, sector), `DL` lists the bodies and belts of the system, `PAR NAME` / `WHERE NAME` print a star's sector, class and distance (`WHERE` alone prints the ship's sector and region), `ST NAME` sets the remote target, `CAST TEXT` writes a note, `CAT` reads the last notes, `HOME` and `PREV` target the home or the previous star, `JUMP LY` jumps that far along the ship's nose (R-407), `HELP`. Esc leaves. Renamed stars are found by their new names.

The shard decoder (`SHARDS`, C-06, `game/shards_screen.cpp`): the shards taken from the ruins (C-03) are read on the ship, at the decoder on the cabin's back wall (`E` facing it), from the flight computer's preferences page or from the guide menu's `THE SHARDS` (on the surface the menu says they are read on the ship). Three levels: the worlds the guide holds shards of (the name or UNKNOWN with the sector and the body, `N OF 50 SHARDS`, `THE LANGUAGE P% LEARNT`, how many are read; the world under the ship is selected first; C-13: a world of two peoples is listed once a people whose shards are held, the people's name after the sector (`THE KHOKURT`), each entry's count, share and reads its own, and the people named in the list's and the shard's headers; a shard taken on such a world says `N OF 100 ON THIS WORLD`; C-14: a world with lent shards shows `N OF 50 SHARDS (M LENT)`, a lent row in cyan, `LENT` in cyan after the shard's header, a friend's name for a piece in cyan, and a friend's name for the world in cyan), a world's shards in the order of their years (each row the shard's first words as the computer last read them, an unread shard all in the people's tongue and dim, `*` on one not yet read at the language's current share), and a shard: `YEAR N OF <town>` and the text laid out word by word, a word read in white, a name in amber, a word not learnt yet dim in the people's own tongue (`12-civilisations.md`). The language is learnt from the shards held of that world (`languageShare`: 30% of the words with one, 8% more per shard, all with ten; the computer knows the words the world's fifty use most first). A text shard is a recording that speaks (C-05, `galaxy/voice.h`, `AudioState::speech`): opening it plays the people's words in the people's voice (the voice named under the year: `A DEEP BREATHY VOICE, SLOW`), 12-25 s for a text of thirty words, and the word being spoken is underlined. Reading a shard the computer has not read at the current share resolves the words as they are spoken, the one being spoken flickering, with `DECODING P%` and a bar below (`ENTER` skips to the end, `SPACE` pauses, `ESC` leaves it unread, a beep when it is done); then the guide records the count it was read with (`decoded`), so the next reading shows the whole text at once (the recording speaking over it, `PLAYING P%`; `ENTER` skips it, or plays it again when it has ended) until more of that world's shards are found and it decodes again with fewer gaps. `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move to the world's other shards (the voice stops), `ESC` goes back a level. The voice, like the music, plays on the decoder screen only (`Game::frame` clears `audio.speech` off it) and ducks the ship's hum. Game time runs on the screen as it does at the flight computer. C-12: every shard's screen is dated in the people's own calendar (`YEAR 212 OF AIYIN, MOON 69, DAY 5`; `shardDate`, `12-civilisations.md`), the world's list carries the calendar line (`YEAR: 101 MOONS OF 5 DAYS AND 1 OVER`) and a timeline under its rows (a rule from YEAR 1, a tick a shard held: read green, unread dim, the cursor's white; dotted past the latest held while the record is open), and the world's last recording, once read, heads its screen THE LAST RECORDING, turns its row's year amber, closes the timeline with THE RECORD ENDS, YEAR N in amber and writes a `SHARD` log line; the guide keeps the world in `ended`.

A piece of music (C-04, `galaxy/music.h`): twelve to eighteen of a world's fifty shards are music, not text. In the world's list a piece's row is its form and length (`A LULLABY, 52 S`) or, once named, `'THE RIVER AT NIGHT' - A LULLABY`; `*` marks one not heard yet. Opening it plays it through the synth (`AudioState::piece`): the screen names the piece (its form "OF THE <people>", or its name) and the tradition in three lines (`A SCALE OF 7 NOTES TO THE OCTAVE`, `A CYCLE OF 2+2+3 AT 112 BEATS A MINUTE`, the timbres: `A PLUCKED STRING, A PIPE AND A DRUM`), and draws the notes as a roll across the piece's length, a bar per note at its degree's height (the lead white and two rows thick, the second voice green, a third cyan, the drum amber ticks under them), the cycles marked, an amber playhead; what the playhead has passed is in colour, the rest dim. The footer is `PLAYING P%` with a bar, then `THE PIECE ENDED`. Keys: `SPACE` pauses and resumes, `N` names the piece (TEXT_ENTRY over the screen, the music going on; the name goes into the guide's `names` under the shard's key and the log gets a note), `ENTER` plays an ended piece again, `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move on (the music stops), `ESC` goes back. A piece is heard whole from the first (the language has no part in it); hearing it records it in the guide as read at the world's full count, so it never shows `*` again. The music stops the moment the decoder screen is left by any road (`Game::frame` clears `audio.piece` when the state is neither SHARDS nor its TEXT_ENTRY); nothing plays it elsewhere on the ship yet (C-11, the instruments, is where that belongs).

A star chart (C-10, 2026-10-03, `galaxy/charts.h`, `12-civilisations.md`): three to five of a world's fifty shards are charts of the people's sky round a star they marked (the world's list says `A CHART:` and the caption). Opening one draws it beside its caption: a disc of the hemisphere round the marked star with the stars as dots by their brightness, the people's figures joined by lines and named at their brightest star (dim in the people's words until every word is learnt), the marked star's amber diamond, and at the right the caption word by word (coloured as a text's) and `THEY MARKED`: the star as the explorer knows it (or UNKNOWN), its class, its distance from here and its sector (`- THE REMOTE TARGET` in cyan once it is). A first reading at the language's current share draws the chart over four seconds, the stars first, then the lines, then the names flickering into place (`DECODING P%`; `ENTER` skips the wait, `ESC` leaves it unread); on a chart read `ENTER` sets the remote target on the marked star (`REMOTE TARGET: ... - V TO FLY`, the chain's next link), `O` holds the chart up to the sky and puts it down again (`HELD UP TO THE SKY` under the star), `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move on, `ESC` goes back. Held up, the chart's figures are drawn on the space view (not during a Vimana flight or in the radar camera) and on the surface at night between the real stars from wherever the ship is, on the sky alone (a line's pixel is skipped where the frame has depth: the cabin's walls, a globe, the ground, a mountain), with the names at their brightest star, the marked star's diamond and `THEY MARKED THIS STAR` (an amber `+` at the edge when it is behind the view) and `A CHART OF THE <PEOPLE> HELD UP` at the top right; it stays up across screens and landings until put down, and is not saved. A chart read logs a `CHART` entry naming the marked star's sector and class only. No chart has a voice: a map, not a sound.

## The GUIDE (`guide.txt`, M3)

`Guide` (`game/guide.h`) holds your names for stars (`sx,sy,sz`) and bodies (`sx,sy,sz/index`), names received from other explorers (`inbox`, shown in cyan), the expedition log, visited stars, landed bodies, star classes and world types seen, the shards taken (`shard <body key>/S<index>`, C-03: `Game::shardKey`; `Guide::shardWorlds` counts the worlds they come from), the shards read on the decoder (`decoded <body key>/S<index> <n>`, the count of that world's shards the language had then, C-06), the signals the radar locked (`signal <key> <kind>`: a world's key or a pulsar's star key, C-07) and the recordings heard on it (`heard <body key>/S<index>`, C-07), the explorer's marks on the worlds (`mark <body key> <lat> <lon> <kind> <lent> <when> <name>`: radians, the kind a `LandmarkKind` or 8 for a place, the name last and possibly empty, R-408), the probes' records (`probe <id> <lent> <body key> <gen> <saved> <launchT> <aimLat> <aimLon> <seed> <start xyz> <headYaw> <descYaw> <chuteOpen> <chuteClose> <crushBar> <endClock> <endBar> <endKm> <endK> <endCause> <storm key|->`, each followed by `probecam <seed> <n> ...`, `probetime <seed> <n> ...`, `probeprofile <seed> <n> <clock bar K wind light layer flashes> ...` and `probelayers <seed> <n> <layer from to> ...`; `stat probes`, X-04), the travel history (last 20 stars), the home star and the statistics. Text file, header `vesperis-guide 1`, saved whenever it changes and with every game save. `Game::starNameOf`/`bodyNameOf` resolve display names everywhere (HUD, lists, sheets, landing map, statuses); a renamed star renames the generated names hanging off it ("HOME OF CATS II", "HOME OF CATS II-a").

Keys: `G` opens the guide menu in space and on the surface (star map, rename this star / this world, write a note, log, gallery, statistics, the shards (C-06: the decoder, from the ship; C-04: `N` on a piece names it), target by name, target by coordinates, return to the previous star, target the home star, set home here, export, import); `M` in space opens the star map directly; `J` the log. Text entry (`TEXT_ENTRY`) takes letters, digits, space, `-` and `.`; Enter confirms, Esc cancels.

Log entries are written on arrival (class, counts, sector), when parked in orbit, on landing (place, temperature, pressure, gravity, day or night), on launch (longest walk, distance driven), when a shard is taken (`SHARD`: the place, the settlement's class, the world and the count of the world's fifty, C-03) when the radar locks a source it had not (`SIGNAL`: the kind, what is heard, the source and its distance and age, the sector, C-07), at a jump (`JUMP`, R-407), at a mark placed (`MARK`: its name, what it marks, the world, the place, R-408) and for notes; the log screen pages newest first. Statistics: systems visited, worlds landed on, classes and types seen (S-02: the classes' codes on their own line, two past twelve classes), furthest from home (LY), longest walk from the capsule, highest point, distance on foot and driven (one line since C-03), shards taken and the worlds they come from (C-03) and how many are decoded (C-06), under it a dim line with the graves read and the records ended (C-12), names given, photographs, creatures sighted and (C-07) the signals heard on the same line.

Star map (`STAR_MAP`): the neighbourhood (10 LY) around the ship, turned with the mouse or arrows, zoomed with the wheel or `+`/`-`; rings at 2, 5 and 10 LY on the galactic plane; stars coloured by class and dimmed with depth; visited stars boxed, the current star crossed, the remote target framed, the home star a diamond (R-407: a legend beside the class filter says so, an unnamed visited star carries the name of a world the explorer named there in brackets and its card lists them: `YOUR WORLDS THERE: XOHORT`); the class filter cycles with `C` (forward) and `X` (back) through ALL and every class of the table, whatever their number (S-02: the keys `1`-`9` toggled S00-S08 and ran out at S-01's tenth class), `0` shows all; the legend reads `CLASSES: ALL` or, in amber, `CLASS: S10 NEUTRON STAR`, and the title counts the stars shown; the star nearest the crosshair is described and `Enter` (or a click) makes it the remote target.

Gallery: screenshots taken with `P`/`F12` get a sidecar `.txt` with the place and EPOC (`Game::screenshotCaption`); the gallery lists `shots/screenshot_*.png`, decodes them with `readPNG` (the game's own PNG format) and shows them with their captions. W-06: a photo through the telescope carries `THROUGH THE TELESCOPE X16: UNKNOWN  ROCKY MOON  64397 KM  6.4 DEG - IN ORBIT OF UNKNOWN  EPOC ...` (the glyph written as `DEG` in the text file). X-04: the probes' recordings are listed among them (their final images, `shots/probe_<n>.png`), merged by time; Enter plays one; a photo of a recording carries `THE RECORDING OF PROBE 3 INTO UNKNOWN AT T+04:31, 0.62 ATM - ...`.

Sharing: "export the guide" writes `guide_export.txt`; "import an inbox file" reads `guide_inbox.txt` (a friend's export placed next to the game) into the inbox; your own names always win. C-14: the import also lends the shards the friend found and read (their `shard` keys with a `decoded` line; what they were lent themselves does not travel on, like the inbox's names): a lent shard is listed and read on the decoder in cyan, counts in its world's language like one of your own, plays or speaks like one, and is never taken (the ruins still hold it: taking it there makes it your own). The status says `N NEW NAMES AND M LENT SHARDS FROM THE INBOX`, an `INBOX` log line counts the worlds, and the statistics count the lent (`Guide::lent`, `lent` lines). X-04: it lends the friend's own probe recordings too (not those lent to them; a seed already held is skipped): a lent recording is marked (`lent` in its `probe` line), framed and titled in cyan in the gallery and on the giant's profile pages (`LENT`), plays like one's own and counts in no statistic; an `INBOX` log line counts them. R-408: and the friend's own marks (not those lent to them; one standing within about 30 m of a mark already held is the same place): drawn in cyan on the maps and over the view, heard by the scanner like any sight (only the explorer's own marks leave a sight out), the explorer's own once renamed. The status reads `INBOX: N NAMES, M SHARDS, K RECORDINGS, J MARKS` (the parts there are).

## Settings (`vesperis_settings.txt`, M0-02)

`Settings` (`game/settings.h`): `mouse_sensitivity` 0.2-3.0, `invert_y`, `fov` 50-100 (horizontal degrees), `master_volume` 0-1, `scanlines`, `window_scale` 2-6 (window = 320*s x 200*s), `render_scale` 1-4 (framebuffer = 320*s x 200*s, default 4 since B-310, 2 before), `mush` 0 classic / 1 soft / 2 sharp / 3 fine (box size scale+1 / scale+2 / max(2, scale) / max(2, scale-1); fine is the default since B-310), `shading` 0 flat cells / 1 stepped gradient / 2 smooth (the default since B-311), `crt` and `bloom` (optional post shader in the platform layer, off), `sprint_toggle` (hold or toggle) and `sprint_speed` 2.5-4.0 (M8-10). Text file with a `vesperis-settings 2` header; unknown keys ignored, values clamped on load; a version-1 file (from before B-310) keeps its other settings but takes the new look once (render scale 4, the fine mush, smooth shading) and is saved as version 2. `Game::applySettings` pushes them into the projections (`spaceR.proj`, `SurfaceView::setFov` rebuilds the direction table), the mouse factors, and `AudioState::master`. The Settings screen (menu item 4, state `SETTINGS`) edits them with up/down and left/right (Enter also advances); Esc or BACK saves the file. `Ctrl+K`/`F10` toggles scanlines directly and saves. A window-size change sets `Game::wantsWindowScale`, which the platform layer applies with `platformSetWindowSize` (ignored in fullscreen). The `Game` constructor touches no files: `loadFromDisk()` reads the settings and the save (the window build calls it; tests set `settingsPath`/`savePath` under `shots/`).

`clock` (M5-05): 0 game time (warpable, saved), 1 real time: every simulated frame sets `t = 3.6e6 + (unix time - 2026-01-01 00:00 UTC)`, so the sky follows the wall clock as in the original and positions stay consistent when switching (`t` is the only state). The data sheet shows `EPOC:SINISTER.MEDIUS.DEXTER` (10^9, 10^6, 10^3, 1 s groups) top right and a `CLOCK REAL TIME` line in that mode.

The data sheet on the surface (M5-06/09; W-01: `T` on the sheet opens the almanac of the place, in space as on the surface) adds `SEASON` (declination, day length or polar day/night, hours or days to the next equinox and solstice, scanned over one orbit), W-03 `FORECAST` on worlds with fronts (`RAIN FROM THE SW IN 2 H 05 MIN`, `SNOW` below -1 C now, `DUST` on desert and thin-atmosphere worlds, `A FRONT` on a quartz world; `IN A FRONT, CLEARING IN 1 H 05 MIN` under one; `NO FRONT WITHIN 5 DAYS`; `Game::updateForecast` lists the next fronts at the feet every thirty seconds of game time, `forecastLine` reads them), `IN THE SKY` (the two largest bodies above the horizon with altitude and width) and `SECOND SUN` when the system is multiple. The HUD, the analyzer and the arrival log use `StarSystem::classString()` (`S00+S01 MULTIPLE`); the analyzer's class description wraps onto a second line when it needs one (S-02: the long rows were cut at 50 characters) and the list gives up a row.

`aspect` (M6-01): 0 square pixels, 1 the original's 4:3 (the platform layer draws the picture 1.2 times taller); `dither` (ordered 2x2 dither over the 64 palette stops instead of the smooth 256-entry interpolation; the pattern is applied per logical pixel at every render scale); `hud_color` 0 green, 1 amber, 2 white (`setHudScheme` rewrites the `HUD_*` colours, so every screen follows).

## Key map and gamepad (M6-02)

`vesperis_keys.txt` next to the game (see `vesperis_keys.example.txt`) holds `PHYSICAL LOGICAL` pairs: the front end applies `KeyMap::apply` after the platform filled the `Input`, so pressing the physical key acts as the logical one everywhere (swap both ways with two lines). Names: letters, digits, `SPACE ENTER ESC TAB BACKSPACE UP DOWN LEFT RIGHT PGUP PGDN HOME END INSERT DELETE LSHIFT RSHIFT LCTRL RCTRL LALT RALT F1..F12` and the punctuation names in the example file.

The first gamepad (`PlatformPad` in `platform.h`, converted to `PadState` and mapped by `applyPad` in `core/input.cpp`): left stick = analogue walking and driving (`Input::moveX/moveY`, used by `updateWalking` and `updateBuggy` when nonzero), right stick = look (fed as mouse motion, scaled by the mouse sensitivity), A enter, B escape, X space (jump/jetpack/brake), Y data sheet, LB sprint, RB chase view, left trigger crouch, right trigger sprint, start escape (menu), back analyzer (Tab), d-pad arrows, left thumb C, right thumb shift, guide E, spare M. Dead zone 18%.

## Photo tools (M6-05)

`Ctrl+P` toggles photo mode in space and on the surface: the HUD goes away and on the surface the camera detaches (`SurfaceView::freeCam`): W A S D fly along the view, Space and C rise and sink, Shift is five times faster, never below the ground and no higher than 400 m above it; the explorer stays where they were and normal play resumes on the second `Ctrl+P`. `Ctrl+Shift+P` writes a panorama, three views at yaw -fov, 0, +fov side by side (`Game::renderPanorama`, `shots/panorama_N.png`, 960x200 at 1x). `Ctrl+R` starts and stops the recorder: every presented frame goes to `movies/take_NNN/frame_NNNNN.png` (a blinking REC on the HUD) for a video tool to assemble. W-06: `P` in the telescope is the telescope's photo (its caption names the target), and `Ctrl+P` hides the reticle with the rest of the HUD.

## Title, attract mode and credits (M6-06)

`C` on the title opens the credits (help page 2). After 20 s without a key or mouse motion the title camera leaves the parked ship and tours the current system, fourteen seconds per body in order, circling at four radii (rings included), with the body in palette bank 2; any input returns to the parked view.

## Saves (M0-04)

Three slots and an autosave, named from `Game::savePrefix` (default `vesperis_save`): `vesperis_save_1.txt` .. `_3.txt` and `vesperis_save_auto.txt`; a legacy `vesperis_save.txt` (version 1, no timestamp) is still read. `Ctrl+S`/`F5` saves to `currentSlot` (also allowed during descent and ascent), `Ctrl+L`/`F9` loads it; the menu's Save/Load open the slot picker (`SLOTS` state, `openSlots`/`updateSlots`/`renderSlots`) whose rows show `saveSummary`: star name, EPOC and where (in space, on the surface, descending, ascending). Autosave (`autosave()`) runs every 5 minutes of real time in a simulating state, on every landing (Enter on the landing map), on every launch (boarding the capsule) and on quit. Continue on the title screen (`loadFromDisk` -> `loadNewest`) takes the file with the newest `saved` stamp, slots first on a tie.

```
vesperis-save 2
saved <unix seconds>
name <star name>
t <game seconds>
warp <time warp>
star <sx> <sy> <sz> <valid 0|1>
ship <x> <y> <z> <yaw> <pitch>
mode <mode> <parkedBody> <localTarget> <orbiting>
park <dirx> <diry> <dirz> <distKm>
belt <targetBelt> <parkedBelt> <rockIr> <rockIa> <rockIy> <rockM> <rockDistKm>   (O3; -1 -1 when no belt is involved)
remote <hasRemote> <sx> <sy> <sz>
surface <0|1>
site <body> <lat> <lon>            (only when surface 1)
player <x> <z> <yaw> <pitch>       (only when surface 1)
capsule <x> <z>                    (only when surface 1)
phase <state> <transT>             (only when saved during DESCENT (3) or ASCENT (5))
buggy <deployed> <x> <z> <heading> <odometer> <in>   (surface only, M8-06)
drone <deployed> <x> <z> <heading> <odometer> <in>   (surface only, R-403; loads landed on the ground where it was, the explorer back in it if they were; a save without the line has no drone)
waypoint <has> <x> <z>             (surface only)
```

Load regenerates the star and the system from the sector, restores the ship, drops VIMANA/APPROACH back to STANDBY, and rebuilds the surface site if `surface 1`; a `phase` line resumes the descent or ascent at the same `transT` (the camera path is a function of it). Unknown keys are ignored; the header prefix must match (versions 1 and 2 are read).

## Audio state

`Game::audio` levels per state: space hum 0.6, engine 0.9 during Vimana and 0.35 during approach; surface hum 0.05, wind from knots and pressure with a tone per place (M4-09: sea 0.25, desert 0.6, mountain 1.0), surf near water, bird calls by day, one footstep per stride by material, rain, lava rumble and eruptions on molten worlds, breath while sprinting; beeps 1 lock, 2 arrive, 3 error, 4 UI; thunder with lightning. `AudioSynth::render` mixes sines, filtered noise and envelopes at 22050 Hz mono. C-07: `radar` is 1 while the radar camera is on (the space hum drops to 0.25 under it): the bed of the void (a rumble far down that breathes, a drone of two tones a quarter-tone apart beating with their octave, a wisp of resonant static wandering between 300 and 1500 Hz, the hiss), thinned as `radarSignal` (the beam's clarity on the strongest signal, 0.85 and over when locked) rises; `radarKind` names the signal in the beam for its own sound (a people's: a carrier at 1.2 kHz drifting under the voice; a pulsar: a thump far down at `radarPulseHz` with a growl falling away after each and a thin whine that circles; a comet: its song, a warble near 92 Hz with a second voice a semitone off, geysers of hiss, ice cracking, a rumble; a magnetosphere: whistlers falling slowly from 2 kHz, bursts of chorus, a band of hiss that breathes, a choir of three voices a quarter-tone apart that comes and goes); `radio` marks the piece or the speech on the synth as the radar's, ghosted by the receiver (the band 250 Hz to 2.6 kHz, a metallic ring at 37 Hz under it, a slow fade, dropouts now and then) at the clarity, and keeps `Game::frame` from stopping them off the decoder; everything the receiver gives goes through an echo a third of a second behind with half of it back. The user's second call ("MORE sounds for signals, greater variety in that cosmic horror direction") added `radarVoice` (the programme a people's transmission carries, `RP_*` of `galaxy/signals.h`: the recording as a voice, whispers, a chant, a numbers station, a loop, or the world's machines: a beacon, data bursts, a bell tolling, a siren, a murmur of phantom voices, or rarely the piece slowed by `pieceSpeed`; the synth adds the machines, the hum under a chant, the breath under whispers, the chime or the relay click at a loop's or a numbers station's start, a tape wow on the content), `radarSeed` (the signal's seed, from which `AudioSynth::rvDerive` draws every natural kind's own parameters: no two pulsars thump alike) and the bed's events (every twelve to forty seconds something in the static: a carrier passing, a boom, a moan, knocking, a murmur, a breath, a scream far away). `vesperis_test signals wav` renders the bed over thirty seconds, three seeds of each kind and every programme to `shots/tests/radar_*.wav` (twenty-one files); `12-civilisations.md` has the whole list. X-01: `probe` is 1 on the probe's screen and 0.12 in the cabin while one falls (the relay murmuring from the landing screen): the link's hiss (more as `probeSignal` falls with the depth) and in space a tick a second, the air's rush by `probeWind` (darker as it deepens, fluttering at 7 Hz under the chute: `probeChute`), the entry's roar and crackle (`probeHeat`), the deep's rumble and past half the depth the hull's groans (`probeDepth`), a thunderclap through the relay at a flash (`probeThunder`, one-shot) and at the end only the hiss of the lost signal (`probeLost`); `vesperis_test probe wav` writes each stage's sound to `shots/tests/probe_*.wav` (rms 0.06-0.10, the entry's peak 0.73).
