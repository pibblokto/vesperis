# Game flow, controls, persistence

## States (`GameState`)

```
TITLE -> SPACE (Enter, or SURFACE when a loaded save was on the surface) ; N starts a new expedition (G-02: parked at the first living world of the pinned home star, `HOME_SX/SY/SZ` in `galaxy/starfield.h`, Heilya at sector 195 0 46; the search of sectors 176..196 x 36..56 for a yellow or orange star with a living world is the fallback when a generation change takes it away)
SPACE -> LANDING_MAP (C, parked at a landable body) -> DESCENT (Enter) -> SURFACE -> ASCENT (Q/E at the capsule) -> SPACE
SPACE/SURFACE -> HELP (H, ?, F1), MENU (Esc), DATA (I, F2); SPACE -> SYSTEM_LIST (Tab); MENU -> SETTINGS, SLOTS (save/load pickers)
SPACE -> SHARDS (C-06: the shard decoder, from the cabin's back wall, the flight computer's preferences page or the guide menu; the menu refuses it on the surface; C-04: a piece of music plays there and `N` names it through TEXT_ENTRY, which returns to the decoder; C-05: a text shard speaks there)
```

`returnState` remembers where an overlay came from and is set explicitly by every overlay entry (B-006). Only SPACE, SURFACE, DESCENT, ASCENT and LANDING_MAP advance game time.

## Ship (`ShipState`)

Modes: `STANDBY` (free in a system or deep space), `VIMANA` (interstellar flight), `APPROACH` (fine approach), `PARKED` (following a body, optionally orbiting).

* Remote target: `R` toggles crosshair mode; the star nearest to the crosshair within 14 px is named live; Enter/click locks it (`lockRemoteTarget`). The current star is refused. `N` turns the ship to the next nearest star and (G-04) faces the cabin view forward as `X` does, so the lock finds it: in the cabin the view kept its own yaw and `Enter` missed the star the ship pointed at. While aiming the star field is amplified x1.5, the six nearest stars carry diamond markers with name and distance (LY), and `N` cycles the crosshair through them (`cycleTargetStar`). `F` toggles the field amplificator at any time (M1-10); arrival from a Vimana flight flashes the palette (`arrivalFlash`).
* Vimana: `V` starts a flight (`flightFrom`, `flightTo`, real-time duration `7 + 2 sqrt(LY)`); the system is unloaded (`sys.valid = false`) during flight; arrival regenerates the target system and parks the ship in standby facing the star. `V` again aborts and reloads the nearest star's system.
* Local target: `L` picks the body under the crosshair (within 18 px of its disc) or cycles; click also picks; `Tab` opens the list (`Enter` approaches, `L` targets). `X` centres the view on it.
* Approach: `startApproach(body)` chooses a parking direction on the lit side, offset sideways and slightly above the orbital plane; `updateShipMotion` moves with smoothstep easing and turns the view.
* Parked: position = body position + `parkDir * parkDist`; `O` toggles orbiting (slow rotation of `parkDir` about the spin axis). `parkDist` comes from `Game::parkDistanceFor`: 3.5 radii, outside the rings for ringed worlds, 12 nucleus radii for a comet (N0-01: the nucleus is 76 px across at 2x with its jets around it).
* Belts (O3, R-302): `ShipState::targetBelt` is the local target when it is a belt (`localTarget` is then -1), `parkedBelt` the belt the ship is parked in, and `rockIr/rockIa/rockIy/rockM` + `rockDist` name the rock it parks beside (`04-galaxy-and-systems.md`). `L` cycles the bodies and then the belts, the analyzer lists the belts after the bodies (selectable), `Enter` calls `startApproachBelt`: `pickBeltRock` keeps the ship's angle round the star, clamps its radius into the belt and takes the biggest rock within two cells of that anchor; the parking point is six rock radii (at least 2 km) off on the sunlit side, a little above (`beltParkPos`), and the parked ship follows it (mode name `IN THE BELT`). `X` aims at the rock from this frame's parking point (a parked ship moves a hundred kilometres per frame at x10). Arrival logs a `BELT` entry. `C` in a belt says there is no world under the ship. `localTargetLabel()` names either kind of target. Saved as `belt <target> <parked> <ir> <ia> <iy> <m> <rockDist>`.
* Signal radar (C-07, 2026-10-02, `game/radar.cpp`, `galaxy/signals.h`, `12-civilisations.md`): `B` in space (the radar set beside the decoder on the cabin's back wall, `facing` 8, or the flight computer's devices page) enters the radar camera, the ship's own instrument seen through like the vehicles' nose cameras: the cabin is left behind (the head faces forward, `drawCabin` is skipped), the mouse turns the ship (`updateCabin` is not run), the picture is the camera's (`radarCameraFeed`: a cold blue-green phosphor, scanlines, a rolling band, a vignette, static on every pixel and snow in flecks that thin with the clarity), and `B`/`Esc` leave it. It listens where the camera looks (`viewBasis()`). The far signals are scanned once per sector of the ship (`signalsNear`: the transmitter sectors within 200 ly and the loud pulsars within 25, under a millisecond, the current star's own left to the local list), the system's own every frame (`localSignals`: a comet within 1.5 AU of its sun, a giant's or a strong big world's magnetosphere, and the people's world of a transmitter system). The meter reads the sum of the beam's gains over the static (`beamGain`: a wide lobe within forty degrees, a middle within ten, a narrow within three, times the signal's strength by its distance; the static a slow wander plus a jitter of about 0.07); a hold within three degrees of the strongest for `2.5 + 3 (1 - strength)` seconds locks (`radarLock`: a `SIGNAL` log entry for a new source naming only the system (the star, its class code, the distance and age, the sector; a body's name for a signal of this system), `Guide::signals`, a beep), the lock holds while the beam stays within eight degrees (lost after 1.5 s outside, `LOCK LOST`), and `Enter` on a lock locks the star and flies (`radarAccept`: `setRemoteStar` then `toggleVimana`, which leaves the camera; a body of this system: the camera left and `startApproach`). What sent the signal is never told: it is heard, and found on arrival. A people's world within the middle lobe is heard before the lock: its slot on the air (`transmittedShard`: the fifty walked in a stride of the world's own, one a slot of 75 s of game time; the next when it ends; C-13: on a world of two peoples the slot's people is a coin of the slot's, the world built again when the air changes hands) is built as a `ShardWorld` of the radar's own (`buildShardWorld`, shared with the decoder) and put on the synth through the receiver (`AudioState::radio`) as the slot's programme (`programmeFor`: the recording spoken, whispered, chanted or looped, a numbers station, a machine of the world's, or rarely its piece slowed; a loop's and a numbers station's repeats run on the game's clock, `radarRepeatContent`); what is heard while locked goes into `Guide::heard` when the programme carried the recording, and a shard taken later that the radar caught says so. The radar is off during a Vimana flight, at an arrival (the scan is fresh there), on the decoder (which takes the synth), on the surface and at a load; the other screens pause the sweep and keep the sound. `testAimAtSignal`, `testRadarInfo` for the harness.
* `choosePaletteBodies` assigns palette banks 2/3 to the two bodies with the largest apparent size.

## Landing map (`LANDING_MAP`)

Shows the body's `PlanetMap` through `buildLandingMapBase` (N1-04: `materialRampColor(ramp[material], exposureStop(albedo, light factor, atmosphere) x hillshade)` with the body's `matColor`, the star tint and the body's noon sky light, so the map is the ground seen from above at noon; north-west hillshade normalised by the map's RMS gradient; lava emissive; clouds at 55% on felisian/thin-atmosphere/quartz worlds, an opaque deck in the body's cloud colour on venusian worlds) times the day/night term from the current sub-solar direction and, since O0-04 (B-306), the shadows on the day side: the moons and the parent between a texel and the star and the world's own rings (`Game::buildShadowMap`, the globe's own rule, 90% dark inside the umbra; rebuilt for another body, every 20 s of game time and at most twice a second of real time; the zoom window gets the same), the cursor (arrows, Shift for 6 deg steps), `R` for a random lit land site, `C` toggles the cloud overlay, `Enter` to land, `Esc` back. The readout (five lines at 8 px pitch since N1): name, coordinates and sector; `MATERIAL +height RELIEF` with a `GROUND` swatch showing the noon ground colour at the cursor from the same ramp (N1-04) and the relief class from `Game::siteSlope` (the mean grade over 150 m and 600 m around the probe scaled to a gradient, O6-02; the steepest rise before; `reliefClass`: FLAT under 0.05, HILLS under 0.2, else MOUNTAINS, N1-06); biome and season (M9-05/09) with the sun's altitude, azimuth and day/twilight/night; local time, day length, gravity, atmosphere; `SKY: ...`. The data sheet adds a generated description of the body and the star (M9-14) and, on the surface, the biome and season here; the analyzer lists asteroid belts after the bodies; the space HUD names the galaxy region. The readout uses a cached `SurfaceSite` probe at the cursor: exact terrain material and elevation at 64 m detail, sun altitude/azimuth, day/twilight/night, local time, day length (or "tidally locked"), gravity, atmosphere. Sector numbers follow the original's 360 x 120 convention.

`J` (M5-09) moves the cursor under the biggest body in this world's sky (the sub-body point, where it stands at the zenith); the fifth readout line says what hangs over the cursor now (`SKY: name, altitude, width, phase, +N PTS`); on a ringed world it starts with the ring's own arc (`RINGS 62 deg UP`, `RINGS EDGE-ON OVERHEAD` within 1.5 deg of the equator, `RINGS BELOW THE HORIZON`) and a ringed sky body reads `RINGED` in place of its phase (O0-01).

Sector zoom (O2, R-301): `Z` zooms into the cursor's sector (`Game::buildLandingZoom`): a window one sector (one degree of latitude) tall and two sectors' worth of ground wide (`zoomHalfLon = zoomHalfLat x 2 / cos(lat)`, the frame being 2:1), 256 x 128 texels sampled from the planet function at `clamp(0.6 texel, 64, 512)` m of detail on all threads (`parallelFor`), coloured like the whole-world map (`mapRampsFor`, `mapColor`: the body's material ramps under the star's light, `exposureStop`, a north-west hillshade normalised over the window and capped at a 3% grade) and lit by the day side per frame; the one-degree grid is drawn over it with the sectors' names at their centres, a scale bar (50, 20, 5 or 1 km) sits at the bottom right, the cursor steps a hundredth of a degree (five with Shift) and the window re-centres when the cursor passes 75% of its half-size; `Z` again returns to the world; worlds under 100 km of radius have no zoom. The first readout line prints the sector as `SECTOR LON:LAT` (`sectorName`, `03-coordinates-and-units.md`); the title says `SECTOR 319:062 - PICK THE SITE` while zoomed. The landing map also prints a comet's gravity in four decimals. C-09: on a world that had a civilisation the window's old roads are drawn over it as hairlines in the ground's own colour under the texel's light (`Game::zoomRoads`: the villages' and the towns' roads of the network of `galaxy/roads.*` that the ground carries, the lanes between hamlets left out, walked for the window on the worker pool at the build), whether or not any of it has been walked: the roads are terrain, as the rivers the zoom draws are, and the one thing on the map that points at ruins not yet found.

## Surface controls

Mouse look, `WASD` (arrows also turn/pitch), `Shift` sprint (stamina), `Space` jump and, held in the air, jetpack, `1`-`9`/`0` autowalk, `C` crouch, `Z` stand tall, `Ctrl` dive while swimming, `B` unfold a buggy at the capsule (a new one each time; any buggy already out is scrapped, R-204), `E` enter/leave the buggy, take a shard of the old people lying within 2.2 m (C-03: `A SHARD HERE - E` on the HUD; the guide, the log and the statistics record it; it is read on the ship's decoder, C-06; `12-civilisations.md`), or board the capsule (leaving reports the trip and the top speed); inside the buggy the mouse pans the nose camera +-70 deg and tilts it -35..+25 (never backwards, N4-06), `V` the outside chase view, `F` unfold a drone at the capsule (R-403, 2026-10-02: a new one each time, the old one scrapped, like the buggy; none on a comet), `E` within 3.5 m gets in and, on the ground, out (the kilometres flown and the top speed reported; `LAND FIRST - HOLD SHIFT TO COME DOWN` in the air); in the drone `Space` lifts off and climbs, `Shift` descends and lands, `W`/`S` thrust fore and aft to 320 km/h, `A`/`D` turn, the mouse pans the nose camera +-70 deg and tilts it -60..+25, `V` the chase view, a ceiling of 400 m over the ground, `M` waypoint (O1: on the spot under the crosshair when the rangefinder has one, `WAYPOINT SET 8.4 KM AWAY`, else where you stand; `M` again clears), `Ctrl` in the air thrusts down on light worlds (O4), `Q`/`E` board the capsule within 7 m (otherwise the HUD tells you where it is), `K` recalls the capsule next to you (always; the status names a buggy left over 50 m away, B-205), `N` sector map (M4-02/O2/O1-04: `+`/`-` or up/down zoom through 2, 8, 32, 128, 512 and 2048 km wide (`Game::sectorMaxLevel`: a level is offered while its half-width stays within 1.2 radii, so a comet has two and a planet six; the image is 100 x 80 samples of the planet function at 16 m to 16 km of detail, always centred on you, so walking never leaves it and the neighbouring sectors are simply there), coloured like the landing map (`mapRampsFor`), the sector grid on the levels from 32 km up (B-402: a step of 1, 2, 5, 10, 15 or 30 degrees keeping the lines 24 px apart; the one-degree cells named where they are at least 46 px wide, the coarser lines labelled with their latitude and longitude along the edges), the sights already found (B-401: `landmarksSeen`, the nearest 24, the explorer's own names at 8-128 km), `S LON:LAT` on the panel, `M` places the waypoint at the cursor, `Esc`/`N`/`Enter` close; the image is rebuilt when you move a tenth of the width, change the level, land or re-anchor (`siteEpoch`, B-303)), crossing into another sector on foot or in the buggy shows `ENTERING SECTOR 226:113 - GRASS, GRASSLAND` and logs a `SECTOR` entry (`lastSecX/Y`, set silently at landing and after a load), `V` on foot cycles the vision modes (M4-10: radiation visor, supervision, infrared, plant vision), `X` highlights nearby creatures and flocks for four seconds with the species name and its state (M4-05, N3-06), `I`/`F2` data sheet, `T` time warp (`Ctrl+T` a 25 s time-lapse at x600 that returns to x1, M5-06; refused when the clock setting is real time), `Ctrl+S`/`F5` save, `Ctrl+L`/`F9` load, `H`/`F1` help, `Esc` menu, `P`/`F12` screenshot, `Ctrl+K`/`F10` scanlines, `Ctrl+F`/`Alt+Enter`/`F11` fullscreen. Movement keys are ignored while `Ctrl` is held.

Key bindings (N5-05): the settings' last item, `KEY BINDINGS`, opens the `KEYS` state: 28 actions in two columns (R-403: `E` is `USE / VEHICLE`, `F` `FIELD AMP. / DRONE`; C-07: `B` `BUGGY / SIGNAL RADAR`) with the physical keys that act as them; arrows select, Enter captures the next raw key (`Game::rawKey`, handed by the platform layer before the key map is applied) and maps it to the action's logical key (`KeyMap::map[raw] = logical`; the action's own key keeps working), Backspace resets the action, Esc writes `vesperis_keys.txt` (`KeyMap::save`, only the pairs that differ) and returns; the file is still read at start-up as before. Global keys are ignored while capturing.

Key twins exist because Mac laptops route the top row to hardware functions unless `Fn` is held (B-002); `Game::macHints` (set by the platform layer on macOS) shows a one-time reminder after the title screen. The helpers `helpKey`, `dataKey`, `saveKey`, `loadKey`, `enterKey` in `game.cpp` define the pairs; `enterKey` excludes `Alt+Enter` (fullscreen).

## HUD

Space: system name and class, sector coordinates, EPOC, mode (standby / vimana / approach / orbit / chase / in the belt), remote target line and marker box, local target line and bracket (label flips to the left near the edge), off-screen arrow, radiation warning, crosshair, status line, time warp indicator. With a belt as the local target (O3): `LOCAL: <belt> <distance to the rock>`, `ROCK 24.3 KM ACROSS` (`- NO GROUND FOR THE CAPSULE` when parked) and a bracket sized to the rock; the data sheet (`I`) shows the belt's sheet: edges, width, periods at both edges, a rock estimate, `LANDABLE NO`, the target or parked rock. When the local target is a comet a second line gives `Game::cometMotionString`: `216 KM/S, PERIAPSIS IN 6.5 H` or `... RECEDING, APOAPSIS IN 14 H` (N0-01); the data sheet adds `NOW <distance> FROM THE STAR` plus that line, the analyzer appends the speed to comet rows, and the help says `T TIME WARP (ORBITS MOVE)`. In the radar camera (C-07, `renderRadarCamera`, in place of the space HUD): the vehicles' frame (the visor and the inner brackets, `CAM 03 RADAR` with a blinking `REC`, `AZ 123  EL +12` the ship's attitude, the EPOC and the system's name at the right), `RECEIVER` and `SWEEP`/`LOCKING`/`LOCKED` under a scope of the meter's last four seconds (x 80-240, y 36-56, the trace in green) with the meter beside it (amber when locked); the crosshair and the lock cone of three degrees round it (dim while sweeping, cyan with a white arc filling while the hold lasts, amber when locked); the source as a smudge of static within the middle lobe (nothing outside ten degrees) and as an amber diamond once locked (an edge `+` when it is behind); three readout lines at y 146, 154 and 162 (the hint `SWEEP THE SKY - A RISE ON THE METER IS A SIGNAL`, `A RISE - CENTRE IT AND HOLD TO LOCK`, `LOCKING 62% - HOLD STEADY`, or the lock, the system only: `A SIGNAL FROM UNKNOWN (S00)  79 LY` / `SECTOR 149 56 78 - THE SIGNAL IS 79 YEARS OLD` / `ENTER LOCKS THE STAR AND FLIES THERE`; of this system: `A SIGNAL FROM THIS SYSTEM` / the body's name / `ENTER APPROACHES IT`); `B / ESC LEAVES THE CAMERA` at the bottom left.

Surface: body name and latitude/longitude, `SECTOR LON:LAT` on the second line (O2), EPOC, compass strip with heading and under it the rangefinder (O1: `8.4 KM  33 MIN WALK` / `6 MIN DRIVE`, `WATER` when the crosshair rests on water; nothing when it rests on the sky), sun altitude/azimuth, local time, elevation, temperature, pressure, gravity (`GRAV 0.0005 G  ESCAPE 9.2 M/S` on a comet, O4), wind, rain, `RING SHADOW` (O0-01), `VENTING 40%` (O4), capsule distance and bearing (kilometres beyond 1000 m), `A SHARD HERE - E` in amber over the capsule line while a shard is within reach on foot (C-03), `OLD ROAD NE-SW` in cyan above the life line while the explorer stands on an old road (C-09: the way it runs; the first step onto each road of a landing is a status line, `AN OLD ROAD RUNS HERE, NE TO SW`, and a `ROAD` entry in the log; in the buggy the same readout stands above the speed), `A GRAVE: THAELU, 140-212` in amber above the road line while a grave's stone is within 2.2 m on foot (C-12: the name and the years in the people's count; the first time at each stone a status line `A GRAVE: THAELU, YEARS 140 TO 212 OF AIYIN`, a `GRAVE` entry in the log and a `grave` line in the guide), swimming flag. In the buggy (N4-06) the picture is the nose camera's (`SurfaceView::cameraFeed`) and the HUD is its interface: inner brackets, `CAM 01 NOSE` with a blinking `REC`, `PAN`/`TILT` in degrees, a pan gauge under the compass showing the +-70 deg range, a crosshair, the speed in double-size digits with the gear (and `REVERSE`) and the trip on the left, a steering bar under the picture (the marker moves right when steering right), the heading, the state (`AIRBORNE`, `SKID`, `BRAKE`, `LIGHTS ON` or the temperature), the capsule line and the local time on the right; the walking readouts and the stamina bar are hidden. In the drone (R-403) the same frame reads `CAM 02 DRONE`, the speed with `KM/H BACK` in reverse, `ALT n M  VS +n.n` (amber at the ceiling), the trip, a `TURN` bar, the heading, the state (`LANDED`, `LIFTING`, `CLIMB`, `DESCENT`, `CEILING`, `HOVER`, `LIGHTS ON` or the temperature), the capsule line and the local time; the rangefinder's time is a `FLIGHT` at 80 m/s; on foot `DRONE 123 M` sits over the buggy's line, and the sector map marks `DRONE`. N3: the data sheet on the surface lists the world's bestiary (`LIFE`, sighted species starred), a first sighting writes a `SIGHTING` entry to the expedition log and a status line and counts in `CREATURES SIGHTED` on the statistics screen (`Guide::creaturesSeen`, `stat creatures` in the guide file); N4-05 adds `LONGEST DRIVE` and `TOP SPEED` (`stat longestdrive`, `stat topspeed`); the landing map's biome line ends with `LIFE: <species>`, the land species most likely met in that biome.

## The cabin (M2)

With the SHIP VIEW setting on CABIN (default) the space state puts you inside the Stardrifter (`Cabin`, `game/cabin.cpp`): a 5 x 5 x 2.4 m room in ship-local metres (+z forward) drawn over the space view with depth, so the outside shows through the window band (0.95-2.05 m) of every wall and the round roof window. Mouse looks (`cabin.yaw/pitch`), `W A S D` walk (walls, the capsule cage of radius 1 m, the GOES console on the left wall the front desk block and, since C-06, the decoder on the back wall), the arrow keys turn the ship itself; `viewBasis()` = head look x ship attitude is used for the space camera, the crosshair and the HUD markers. `E` uses what you face within reach (`cabin.facing`): the GOES console (left wall), the flight computer (front desk), the three right-wall screens (star map, landing map = deploy the capsule, target data), the capsule cage (deploy) and, since C-06, the shard decoder on the back wall (`facing` 7: a desk like the console's whose screen glows and breathes while a shard waits to be read) and, since C-07, the signal radar beside it on the same wall (`facing` 8, x -2.1..-1.0: a narrower set whose screen brightens and pulses while the receiver is on; `E` enters the radar camera). `U` toggles the cabin light (the hull ramp follows `lightLevel`), `Y` depolarises the hull (walls drawn as tinted glass), `PgUp`/`PgDn` ride the lifter to the observation deck on the roof. Banks 8 (hull), 9 (screens), 10 (light, the fuel orb and the capsule) are set by `cabinPalette`. The cabin state is saved (`cabin` line).

The flight computer (`SHIPSCREEN`) has three pages: Flight control (targeting, Vimana, local target, analyzer, approach, centre, orbit, deploy, home, previous), Onboard devices (light, hull, lifter, field amplificator, time warp, scanlines, the signal radar: C-07) and Preferences (settings, guide, log, the shards, save); Left/Right change the page, Up/Down and Enter act, `E`/Esc leave. Every action shares code with the key commands (`toggleVimana`, `deployCapsule`, `startApproach`, ...).

The GOES console (`CONSOLE`): a text terminal with a 200-line scrollback and typed commands: `SL [LY]` lists stars within a range (name, class, distance, sector), `DL` lists the bodies and belts of the system, `PAR NAME` / `WHERE NAME` print a star's sector, class and distance (`WHERE` alone prints the ship's sector and region), `ST NAME` sets the remote target, `CAST TEXT` writes a note, `CAT` reads the last notes, `HOME` and `PREV` target the home or the previous star, `HELP`. Esc leaves. Renamed stars are found by their new names.

The shard decoder (`SHARDS`, C-06, `game/shards_screen.cpp`): the shards taken from the ruins (C-03) are read on the ship, at the decoder on the cabin's back wall (`E` facing it), from the flight computer's preferences page or from the guide menu's `THE SHARDS` (on the surface the menu says they are read on the ship). Three levels: the worlds the guide holds shards of (the name or UNKNOWN with the sector and the body, `N OF 50 SHARDS`, `THE LANGUAGE P% LEARNT`, how many are read; the world under the ship is selected first; C-13: a world of two peoples is listed once a people whose shards are held, the people's name after the sector (`THE KHOKURT`), each entry's count, share and reads its own, and the people named in the list's and the shard's headers; a shard taken on such a world says `N OF 100 ON THIS WORLD`; C-14: a world with lent shards shows `N OF 50 SHARDS (M LENT)`, a lent row in cyan, `LENT` in cyan after the shard's header, a friend's name for a piece in cyan, and a friend's name for the world in cyan), a world's shards in the order of their years (each row the shard's first words as the computer last read them, an unread shard all in the people's tongue and dim, `*` on one not yet read at the language's current share), and a shard: `YEAR N OF <town>` and the text laid out word by word, a word read in white, a name in amber, a word not learnt yet dim in the people's own tongue (`12-civilisations.md`). The language is learnt from the shards held of that world (`languageShare`: 30% of the words with one, 8% more per shard, all with ten; the computer knows the words the world's fifty use most first). A text shard is a recording that speaks (C-05, `galaxy/voice.h`, `AudioState::speech`): opening it plays the people's words in the people's voice (the voice named under the year: `A DEEP BREATHY VOICE, SLOW`), 12-25 s for a text of thirty words, and the word being spoken is underlined. Reading a shard the computer has not read at the current share resolves the words as they are spoken, the one being spoken flickering, with `DECODING P%` and a bar below (`ENTER` skips to the end, `SPACE` pauses, `ESC` leaves it unread, a beep when it is done); then the guide records the count it was read with (`decoded`), so the next reading shows the whole text at once (the recording speaking over it, `PLAYING P%`; `ENTER` skips it, or plays it again when it has ended) until more of that world's shards are found and it decodes again with fewer gaps. `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move to the world's other shards (the voice stops), `ESC` goes back a level. The voice, like the music, plays on the decoder screen only (`Game::frame` clears `audio.speech` off it) and ducks the ship's hum. Game time runs on the screen as it does at the flight computer. C-12: every shard's screen is dated in the people's own calendar (`YEAR 212 OF AIYIN, MOON 69, DAY 5`; `shardDate`, `12-civilisations.md`), the world's list carries the calendar line (`YEAR: 101 MOONS OF 5 DAYS AND 1 OVER`) and a timeline under its rows (a rule from YEAR 1, a tick a shard held: read green, unread dim, the cursor's white; dotted past the latest held while the record is open), and the world's last recording, once read, heads its screen THE LAST RECORDING, turns its row's year amber, closes the timeline with THE RECORD ENDS, YEAR N in amber and writes a `SHARD` log line; the guide keeps the world in `ended`.

A piece of music (C-04, `galaxy/music.h`): twelve to eighteen of a world's fifty shards are music, not text. In the world's list a piece's row is its form and length (`A LULLABY, 52 S`) or, once named, `'THE RIVER AT NIGHT' - A LULLABY`; `*` marks one not heard yet. Opening it plays it through the synth (`AudioState::piece`): the screen names the piece (its form "OF THE <people>", or its name) and the tradition in three lines (`A SCALE OF 7 NOTES TO THE OCTAVE`, `A CYCLE OF 2+2+3 AT 112 BEATS A MINUTE`, the timbres: `A PLUCKED STRING, A PIPE AND A DRUM`), and draws the notes as a roll across the piece's length, a bar per note at its degree's height (the lead white and two rows thick, the second voice green, a third cyan, the drum amber ticks under them), the cycles marked, an amber playhead; what the playhead has passed is in colour, the rest dim. The footer is `PLAYING P%` with a bar, then `THE PIECE ENDED`. Keys: `SPACE` pauses and resumes, `N` names the piece (TEXT_ENTRY over the screen, the music going on; the name goes into the guide's `names` under the shard's key and the log gets a note), `ENTER` plays an ended piece again, `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move on (the music stops), `ESC` goes back. A piece is heard whole from the first (the language has no part in it); hearing it records it in the guide as read at the world's full count, so it never shows `*` again. The music stops the moment the decoder screen is left by any road (`Game::frame` clears `audio.piece` when the state is neither SHARDS nor its TEXT_ENTRY); nothing plays it elsewhere on the ship yet (C-11, the instruments, is where that belongs).

A star chart (C-10, 2026-10-03, `galaxy/charts.h`, `12-civilisations.md`): three to five of a world's fifty shards are charts of the people's sky round a star they marked (the world's list says `A CHART:` and the caption). Opening one draws it beside its caption: a disc of the hemisphere round the marked star with the stars as dots by their brightness, the people's figures joined by lines and named at their brightest star (dim in the people's words until every word is learnt), the marked star's amber diamond, and at the right the caption word by word (coloured as a text's) and `THEY MARKED`: the star as the explorer knows it (or UNKNOWN), its class, its distance from here and its sector (`- THE REMOTE TARGET` in cyan once it is). A first reading at the language's current share draws the chart over four seconds, the stars first, then the lines, then the names flickering into place (`DECODING P%`; `ENTER` skips the wait, `ESC` leaves it unread); on a chart read `ENTER` sets the remote target on the marked star (`REMOTE TARGET: ... - V TO FLY`, the chain's next link), `O` holds the chart up to the sky and puts it down again (`HELD UP TO THE SKY` under the star), `LEFT`/`RIGHT` move on, `ESC` goes back. Held up, the chart's figures are drawn on the space view (not during a Vimana flight or in the radar camera) and on the surface at night between the real stars from wherever the ship is, on the sky alone (a line's pixel is skipped where the frame has depth: the cabin's walls, a globe, the ground, a mountain), with the names at their brightest star, the marked star's diamond and `THEY MARKED THIS STAR` (an amber `+` at the edge when it is behind the view) and `A CHART OF THE <PEOPLE> HELD UP` at the top right; it stays up across screens and landings until put down, and is not saved. A chart read logs a `CHART` entry naming the marked star's sector and class only. No chart has a voice: a map, not a sound.

## The GUIDE (`guide.txt`, M3)

`Guide` (`game/guide.h`) holds your names for stars (`sx,sy,sz`) and bodies (`sx,sy,sz/index`), names received from other explorers (`inbox`, shown in cyan), the expedition log, visited stars, landed bodies, star classes and world types seen, the shards taken (`shard <body key>/S<index>`, C-03: `Game::shardKey`; `Guide::shardWorlds` counts the worlds they come from), the shards read on the decoder (`decoded <body key>/S<index> <n>`, the count of that world's shards the language had then, C-06), the signals the radar locked (`signal <key> <kind>`: a world's key or a pulsar's star key, C-07) and the recordings heard on it (`heard <body key>/S<index>`, C-07), the travel history (last 20 stars), the home star and the statistics. Text file, header `vesperis-guide 1`, saved whenever it changes and with every game save. `Game::starNameOf`/`bodyNameOf` resolve display names everywhere (HUD, lists, sheets, landing map, statuses); a renamed star renames the generated names hanging off it ("HOME OF CATS II", "HOME OF CATS II-a").

Keys: `G` opens the guide menu in space and on the surface (star map, rename this star / this world, write a note, log, gallery, statistics, the shards (C-06: the decoder, from the ship; C-04: `N` on a piece names it), target by name, target by coordinates, return to the previous star, target the home star, set home here, export, import); `M` in space opens the star map directly; `J` the log. Text entry (`TEXT_ENTRY`) takes letters, digits, space, `-` and `.`; Enter confirms, Esc cancels.

Log entries are written on arrival (class, counts, sector), when parked in orbit, on landing (place, temperature, pressure, gravity, day or night), on launch (longest walk, distance driven), when a shard is taken (`SHARD`: the place, the settlement's class, the world and the count of the world's fifty, C-03) when the radar locks a source it had not (`SIGNAL`: the kind, what is heard, the source and its distance and age, the sector, C-07) and for notes; the log screen pages newest first. Statistics: systems visited, worlds landed on, classes and types seen (S-02: the classes' codes on their own line, two past twelve classes), furthest from home (LY), longest walk from the capsule, highest point, distance on foot and driven (one line since C-03), shards taken and the worlds they come from (C-03) and how many are decoded (C-06), under it a dim line with the graves read and the records ended (C-12), names given, photographs, creatures sighted and (C-07) the signals heard on the same line.

Star map (`STAR_MAP`): the neighbourhood (10 LY) around the ship, turned with the mouse or arrows, zoomed with the wheel or `+`/`-`; rings at 2, 5 and 10 LY on the galactic plane; stars coloured by class and dimmed with depth; visited stars boxed, the current star crossed, the remote target framed, the home star a diamond; the class filter cycles with `C` (forward) and `X` (back) through ALL and every class of the table, whatever their number (S-02: the keys `1`-`9` toggled S00-S08 and ran out at S-01's tenth class), `0` shows all; the legend reads `CLASSES: ALL` or, in amber, `CLASS: S10 NEUTRON STAR`, and the title counts the stars shown; the star nearest the crosshair is described and `Enter` (or a click) makes it the remote target.

Gallery: screenshots taken with `P`/`F12` get a sidecar `.txt` with the place and EPOC (`Game::screenshotCaption`); the gallery lists `shots/screenshot_*.png`, decodes them with `readPNG` (the game's own PNG format) and shows them with their captions.

Sharing: "export the guide" writes `guide_export.txt`; "import an inbox file" reads `guide_inbox.txt` (a friend's export placed next to the game) into the inbox; your own names always win. C-14: the import also lends the shards the friend found and read (their `shard` keys with a `decoded` line; what they were lent themselves does not travel on, like the inbox's names): a lent shard is listed and read on the decoder in cyan, counts in its world's language like one of your own, plays or speaks like one, and is never taken (the ruins still hold it: taking it there makes it your own). The status says `N NEW NAMES AND M LENT SHARDS FROM THE INBOX`, an `INBOX` log line counts the worlds, and the statistics count the lent (`Guide::lent`, `lent` lines).

## Settings (`vesperis_settings.txt`, M0-02)

`Settings` (`game/settings.h`): `mouse_sensitivity` 0.2-3.0, `invert_y`, `fov` 50-100 (horizontal degrees), `master_volume` 0-1, `scanlines`, `window_scale` 2-6 (window = 320*s x 200*s), `render_scale` 1-4 (framebuffer = 320*s x 200*s, default 4 since B-310, 2 before), `mush` 0 classic / 1 soft / 2 sharp / 3 fine (box size scale+1 / scale+2 / max(2, scale) / max(2, scale-1); fine is the default since B-310), `shading` 0 flat cells / 1 stepped gradient / 2 smooth (the default since B-311), `crt` and `bloom` (optional post shader in the platform layer, off), `sprint_toggle` (hold or toggle) and `sprint_speed` 2.5-4.0 (M8-10). Text file with a `vesperis-settings 2` header; unknown keys ignored, values clamped on load; a version-1 file (from before B-310) keeps its other settings but takes the new look once (render scale 4, the fine mush, smooth shading) and is saved as version 2. `Game::applySettings` pushes them into the projections (`spaceR.proj`, `SurfaceView::setFov` rebuilds the direction table), the mouse factors, and `AudioState::master`. The Settings screen (menu item 4, state `SETTINGS`) edits them with up/down and left/right (Enter also advances); Esc or BACK saves the file. `Ctrl+K`/`F10` toggles scanlines directly and saves. A window-size change sets `Game::wantsWindowScale`, which the platform layer applies with `platformSetWindowSize` (ignored in fullscreen). The `Game` constructor touches no files: `loadFromDisk()` reads the settings and the save (the window build calls it; tests set `settingsPath`/`savePath` under `shots/`).

`clock` (M5-05): 0 game time (warpable, saved), 1 real time: every simulated frame sets `t = 3.6e6 + (unix time - 2026-01-01 00:00 UTC)`, so the sky follows the wall clock as in the original and positions stay consistent when switching (`t` is the only state). The data sheet shows `EPOC:SINISTER.MEDIUS.DEXTER` (10^9, 10^6, 10^3, 1 s groups) top right and a `CLOCK REAL TIME` line in that mode.

The data sheet on the surface (M5-06/09) adds `SEASON` (declination, day length or polar day/night, hours or days to the next equinox and solstice, scanned over one orbit), `IN THE SKY` (the two largest bodies above the horizon with altitude and width) and `SECOND SUN` when the system is multiple. The HUD, the analyzer and the arrival log use `StarSystem::classString()` (`S00+S01 MULTIPLE`); the analyzer's class description wraps onto a second line when it needs one (S-02: the long rows were cut at 50 characters) and the list gives up a row.

`aspect` (M6-01): 0 square pixels, 1 the original's 4:3 (the platform layer draws the picture 1.2 times taller); `dither` (ordered 2x2 dither over the 64 palette stops instead of the smooth 256-entry interpolation; the pattern is applied per logical pixel at every render scale); `hud_color` 0 green, 1 amber, 2 white (`setHudScheme` rewrites the `HUD_*` colours, so every screen follows).

## Key map and gamepad (M6-02)

`vesperis_keys.txt` next to the game (see `vesperis_keys.example.txt`) holds `PHYSICAL LOGICAL` pairs: the front end applies `KeyMap::apply` after the platform filled the `Input`, so pressing the physical key acts as the logical one everywhere (swap both ways with two lines). Names: letters, digits, `SPACE ENTER ESC TAB BACKSPACE UP DOWN LEFT RIGHT PGUP PGDN HOME END INSERT DELETE LSHIFT RSHIFT LCTRL RCTRL LALT RALT F1..F12` and the punctuation names in the example file.

The first gamepad (`PlatformPad` in `platform.h`, converted to `PadState` and mapped by `applyPad` in `core/input.cpp`): left stick = analogue walking and driving (`Input::moveX/moveY`, used by `updateWalking` and `updateBuggy` when nonzero), right stick = look (fed as mouse motion, scaled by the mouse sensitivity), A enter, B escape, X space (jump/jetpack/brake), Y data sheet, LB sprint, RB chase view, left trigger crouch, right trigger sprint, start escape (menu), back analyzer (Tab), d-pad arrows, left thumb C, right thumb shift, guide E, spare M. Dead zone 18%.

## Photo tools (M6-05)

`Ctrl+P` toggles photo mode in space and on the surface: the HUD goes away and on the surface the camera detaches (`SurfaceView::freeCam`): W A S D fly along the view, Space and C rise and sink, Shift is five times faster, never below the ground and no higher than 400 m above it; the explorer stays where they were and normal play resumes on the second `Ctrl+P`. `Ctrl+Shift+P` writes a panorama, three views at yaw -fov, 0, +fov side by side (`Game::renderPanorama`, `shots/panorama_N.png`, 960x200 at 1x). `Ctrl+R` starts and stops the recorder: every presented frame goes to `movies/take_NNN/frame_NNNNN.png` (a blinking REC on the HUD) for a video tool to assemble.

## Title, attract mode and credits (M6-06)

`C` on the title opens the credits (help page 2). After 20 s without a key or mouse motion the title camera leaves the parked ship and tours the current system, fourteen seconds per body in order, circling at four radii (rings included), with the body in palette bank 2; any input returns to the parked view.

## Saves (M0-04)

Three slots and an autosave, named from `Game::savePrefix` (default `vesperis_save`): `vesperis_save_1.txt` .. `_3.txt` and `vesperis_save_auto.txt`; a legacy `vesperis_save.txt` (version 1, no timestamp) is still read. `Ctrl+S`/`F5` saves to `currentSlot` (also allowed during descent and ascent), `Ctrl+L`/`F9` loads it; the menu's Save/Load open the slot picker (`SLOTS` state, `openSlots`/`updateSlots`/`renderSlots`) whose rows show `saveSummary`: star name, EPOC and where (in space, on the surface, descending, ascending). Autosave (`autosave()`) runs every 5 minutes of real time in a simulating state, on every landing (Enter on the landing map), on every launch (boarding the capsule) and on quit. Continue on the title screen (`loadFromDisk` -> `loadNewest`) takes the file with the newest `saved` stamp, slots first on a tie.

```
vesperis-save 2
saved <unix seconds>
name <star name>
t <game seconds>
warp <time warp>
star <sx> <sy> <sz> <valid 0|1>
ship <x> <y> <z> <yaw> <pitch>
mode <mode> <parkedBody> <localTarget> <orbiting>
park <dirx> <diry> <dirz> <distKm>
belt <targetBelt> <parkedBelt> <rockIr> <rockIa> <rockIy> <rockM> <rockDistKm>   (O3; -1 -1 when no belt is involved)
remote <hasRemote> <sx> <sy> <sz>
surface <0|1>
site <body> <lat> <lon>            (only when surface 1)
player <x> <z> <yaw> <pitch>       (only when surface 1)
capsule <x> <z>                    (only when surface 1)
phase <state> <transT>             (only when saved during DESCENT (3) or ASCENT (5))
buggy <deployed> <x> <z> <heading> <odometer> <in>   (surface only, M8-06)
drone <deployed> <x> <z> <heading> <odometer> <in>   (surface only, R-403; loads landed on the ground where it was, the explorer back in it if they were; a save without the line has no drone)
waypoint <has> <x> <z>             (surface only)
stamina <0..100>                   (surface only)
```

Load regenerates the star and the system from the sector, restores the ship, drops VIMANA/APPROACH back to STANDBY, and rebuilds the surface site if `surface 1`; a `phase` line resumes the descent or ascent at the same `transT` (the camera path is a function of it). Unknown keys are ignored; the header prefix must match (versions 1 and 2 are read).

## Audio state

`Game::audio` levels per state: space hum 0.6, engine 0.9 during Vimana and 0.35 during approach; surface hum 0.05, wind from knots and pressure with a tone per place (M4-09: sea 0.25, desert 0.6, mountain 1.0), surf near water, bird calls by day, one footstep per stride by material, rain, lava rumble and eruptions on molten worlds, breath while sprinting; beeps 1 lock, 2 arrive, 3 error, 4 UI; thunder with lightning. `AudioSynth::render` mixes sines, filtered noise and envelopes at 22050 Hz mono. C-07: `radar` is 1 while the radar camera is on (the space hum drops to 0.25 under it): the bed of the void (a rumble far down that breathes, a drone of two tones a quarter-tone apart beating with their octave, a wisp of resonant static wandering between 300 and 1500 Hz, the hiss), thinned as `radarSignal` (the beam's clarity on the strongest signal, 0.85 and over when locked) rises; `radarKind` names the signal in the beam for its own sound (a people's: a carrier at 1.2 kHz drifting under the voice; a pulsar: a thump far down at `radarPulseHz` with a growl falling away after each and a thin whine that circles; a comet: its song, a warble near 92 Hz with a second voice a semitone off, geysers of hiss, ice cracking, a rumble; a magnetosphere: whistlers falling slowly from 2 kHz, bursts of chorus, a band of hiss that breathes, a choir of three voices a quarter-tone apart that comes and goes); `radio` marks the piece or the speech on the synth as the radar's, ghosted by the receiver (the band 250 Hz to 2.6 kHz, a metallic ring at 37 Hz under it, a slow fade, dropouts now and then) at the clarity, and keeps `Game::frame` from stopping them off the decoder; everything the receiver gives goes through an echo a third of a second behind with half of it back. The user's second call ("MORE sounds for signals, greater variety in that cosmic horror direction") added `radarVoice` (the programme a people's transmission carries, `RP_*` of `galaxy/signals.h`: the recording as a voice, whispers, a chant, a numbers station, a loop, or the world's machines: a beacon, data bursts, a bell tolling, a siren, a murmur of phantom voices, or rarely the piece slowed by `pieceSpeed`; the synth adds the machines, the hum under a chant, the breath under whispers, the chime or the relay click at a loop's or a numbers station's start, a tape wow on the content), `radarSeed` (the signal's seed, from which `AudioSynth::rvDerive` draws every natural kind's own parameters: no two pulsars thump alike) and the bed's events (every twelve to forty seconds something in the static: a carrier passing, a boom, a moan, knocking, a murmur, a breath, a scream far away). `vesperis_test signals wav` renders the bed over thirty seconds, three seeds of each kind and every programme to `shots/tests/radar_*.wav` (twenty-one files); `12-civilisations.md` has the whole list.
